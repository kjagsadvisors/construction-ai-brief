OpenAI's new "dots" are always-on AI agents you assign work to through text, Slack, or Microsoft Teams, and each can be given its own identity, credentials, and tools. For a project manager, that makes dots a candidate for open-item follow-up. It also means the real decision is access, not features.

## What did OpenAI announce?

At DevDay on September 29, OpenAI introduced dots, which it describes as always-on agents built to handle everything. Coverage from [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openai-launches-dots-its-bubbly-agentic-avatar/) and [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/29/openai-takes-on-metas-muse-with-new-dot-agents-and-unveils-a-potential-google-workspace-competitor/) says they run multistep tasks like keeping track of a project or working a to-do list, use whatever apps you have granted, and only reach out when something needs a person. Individual dots can be provisioned with specific identities, credentials, and tools through existing systems.

Availability is limited. Dots are rolling out to ChatGPT Pro and Business Premium users in eligible markets, and Enterprise workspaces can try them once an administrator enables it. OpenAI also launched a $500-a-month Pro 500 plan with a faster "Ultrafast" tier, but that is a separate story from dots.

## Which PM work fits an agent like this?

Follow-up is the obvious fit. Most PM time on RFIs and submittals goes to chasing, not deciding. Here is how the work sorts:

| Task | Delegate to an agent? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Listing RFIs and submittals past their due date | Yes, read-only | Pure lookup and summary |
| Drafting a reminder to a sub or design team | Yes, draft only | A person hits send |
| Sending reminders on its own | Maybe, later | Only after weeks of clean drafts |
| Answering an RFI or approving a submittal | No | Contractual authority stays with a named person |
| Editing the schedule, budget, or pay app | No | Errors move money and dates |

## What should a GC give it access to?

Treat a dot like a new coordinator on day one. Start it on a read-only view of the RFI log and submittal register, connected to a single project channel rather than the whole company workspace. Do not hand it accounting, payroll, or owner-facing portals to start.

Two checks are worth building in. First, compare what it reports having done to what your systems actually recorded. Second, make sure anything that leaves the building, especially to an owner, architect, or sub, passes through a human. OpenAI itself pulled a model this week over an agent acting outside its permitted scope, as [covered here](/posts/2026-09-29-openai-astra-shelved-scope-authorization-construction-change-orders), so scoped access is not paranoia.

## What's still unproven?

Nothing in the launch coverage shows dots connecting to construction platforms such as Procore or Autodesk Construction Cloud, so assume you would be wiring them through generic app access until someone confirms otherwise. Launch demos also cover personal tasks like appointments, not a 40-item submittal log with revision histories. And a Slack or Teams assignment leaves a thin paper trail unless you save it to the project record.

## Takeaway

If you run projects, pick one recurring chase, such as the weekly past-due RFI list, and pilot an agent on read-only access for a month. Judge it on whether the list was right. Grant more only after it has earned it.
