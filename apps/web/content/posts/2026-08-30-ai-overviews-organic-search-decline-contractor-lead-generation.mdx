Organic search sessions fell 10.5% across a 54-client advertiser dataset after Google's AI Overviews became common, while traffic referred from ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity rose 163% and converted at roughly 1.5 times the rate of regular search traffic. For a remodeler or trade sub whose new business still runs through "someone found us on Google," the channel that's been reliable for a decade is changing shape under you.

## What does the data actually show?

Digital marketing agency Brainlabs pulled Google Analytics data from 54 client accounts — 24 US, 24 UK, six elsewhere, a mix of large and mid-size advertisers — spanning January 2025 through April 2026. The headline numbers, reported by Digiday:

- Organic search sessions across the dataset dropped from 140.1 million to 125.4 million, a 10.5% decline. 46 of the 54 clients saw a drop.
- Referral traffic from AI platforms rose 163% over the same window.
- "Key events" (Brainlabs' term for a meaningful visitor action — a form fill, a quote request, a call click) driven by AI referrals rose 335%, and AI-referred visitors completed those actions at about 1.5x the rate of organic-search visitors.

The pattern: fewer total visitors arriving from a Google search results page, because AI Overviews now answer a lot of informational queries without a click. The visitors who do arrive from an AI platform have usually already done more of their research and convert better once they land.

## Does this show up in construction specifically?

There's no construction-specific version of the Brainlabs study, but a same-audience data point exists: Scorpion's 2026 State of Home Services report, based on a survey of 2,000 homeowners and nearly 1,000 home-services business leaders, found 22% of homeowners now use AI tools like ChatGPT to research or find a service provider — alongside, not instead of, Google search. The same report found 80% of home-services business leaders say they're unsure how to appear in AI search results.

That's the gap. Homeowners looking for a remodeler, roofer, or mechanical contractor are already using AI tools as part of the search. Most contractor websites weren't built with that in mind — they were built to rank on Google, full stop.

## What should a contractor actually do differently?

| Old target | Google organic ranking | AI-answer citation |
|---|---|---|
| What matters | Keyword-optimized pages, backlinks | Clear, direct answers AI can quote; being cited on trusted third-party sites |
| Where the work shows up | Your own site's meta tags and content | Schema markup, review platforms, local directories, press mentions |
| What "winning" looks like | Page 1 of search results | Being the source an AI answer names or links to |
| Who owns it | Marketing/SEO vendor | Same vendor, different scorecard — ask if they're tracking AI citations at all |

Three concrete moves, in order of effort:

1. **Add schema markup for services and service areas.** This is the structured data AI systems use to understand what you do and where — most contractor sites either don't have it or have it incomplete.
2. **Rewrite your top service pages to answer the question in the first sentence.** "Do you install tankless water heaters in [city]?" should have a direct yes/no-plus-detail answer at the top of that page, not three paragraphs of company history first.
3. **Get named on sites AI models treat as trustworthy** — local business directories, trade association pages, review platforms, and any local press coverage. AI answer engines cite across the web, not just your own domain's ranking.

## Should a mid-size GC or trade sub act on this now?

This matters most for firms that win work through inbound leads — remodelers, residential and light-commercial trade subs, anyone whose new business starts with a homeowner or owner's rep typing a question into a search box or a chatbot. It matters less for a GC that wins almost entirely off bid lists and repeat owner relationships, though even there, a prospective owner or architect often checks the firm's site before a first call.

Don't overhaul the whole site this week. Do audit your three or four highest-traffic service pages against the checklist above, and ask whoever runs your marketing whether they're tracking AI referral traffic at all — most agencies are still reporting on Google organic alone, which means you're flying blind on a channel that's already sending you leads that convert better than the ones you're used to watching.
