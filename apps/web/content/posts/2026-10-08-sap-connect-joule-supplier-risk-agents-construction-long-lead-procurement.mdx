SAP used its Connect conference in Las Vegas this week to show Joule agents that flag supplier risk, plan around supply exceptions, and work with suppliers and logistics partners. For a GC or large sub on SAP, the useful question is not "should we buy this." It is whether your purchase order data is clean enough for an agent to watch long-lead equipment, because most of these agents will not be generally available until late 2026 or early 2027.

## What did SAP actually announce?

According to SAP's own coverage and independent reports, the agents extend into supplier collaboration, supply planning, manufacturing, logistics, and asset maintenance. Two pieces matter most to a contractor's procurement team:

- A business-network assistant meant to help with collaboration with suppliers and logistics partners.
- A supply chain planning assistant meant to sense plan exceptions and propose responses, along with agents that flag supplier-risk signals.

SAP also said Joule agents will be able to connect to third-party AI agents and systems. Analysts are more cautious on timing: inbound calls from outside agents are planned for the fourth quarter, not live today.

## When can anyone use it?

Mostly not yet. Constellation Research reports that much of what was announced reaches SAP's Early Adopter Care program in the first quarter of 2027, with some items in the fourth quarter of 2026, and notes that little announced was generally available now. ERP Today's headline says the same: most new assistants arrive by early 2027. Sources differ on specific dates, so get them in writing from your SAP rep.

## Where does this touch a construction project?

SAP is built for manufacturers and big enterprises, so the direct buyers in construction are large GCs, owners, and equipment-heavy subs. The use case that fits is long-lead procurement: switchgear, transformers, chillers, elevators, and generators.

| Task | What an agent could watch | What it depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Long-lead tracking | PO dates, promised ship dates, supplier signals | Accurate POs and promised dates |
| Schedule risk | Items likely to miss a needed-on-site date | Needed-on-site dates tied to the PO |
| Substitution options | Alternate suppliers when one is flagged | Approved-equal lists and submittal status |
| Sub and vendor updates | Structured status requests | Vendors willing to answer in a system, not a phone call |

## What are the limits?

Three, and they are not small. First, these are vendor demos and early-adopter programs, and the timing is not firm. Second, a supplier-risk flag is only as good as the data behind it; if the promised date lives in a PM's email, the agent never sees it. Third, a flagged risk does not fix a switchgear delay. Someone still has to rework the schedule, talk to the owner, and price any substitution.

## What should a procurement manager do now?

You do not need to wait for the product to do the groundwork:

1. Pick your five longest-lead items on the current job and check whether the PO, promised ship date, and needed-on-site date all sit in one system.
2. Record who approves a substitution and what submittal status it requires.
3. If you run SAP, ask your account team which of these agents are in Early Adopter Care, what data they read, and what they are allowed to send to suppliers without a person approving it.
4. If you do not run SAP, treat this as a preview of what your ERP or project software vendor will pitch next.

The takeaway: the agent will not make up for missing dates. Clean long-lead data is the part of this you can fix this month.
