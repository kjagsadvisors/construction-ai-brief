Nvidia finalized its guarantee for OpenAI's Ohio data center campus on August 17 at up to $105 billion — a little over a third of the $250 billion figure first reported in late July. The number matters less than what the signed deal confirms: SB Energy, not OpenAI or Nvidia, is the entity building, owning, and operating the site, which is the party a GC or sub would actually be contracting with.

## What's now confirmed that wasn't in July

[Construction AI Brief covered the rumored version of this deal three weeks ago](/posts/2026-07-27-nvidia-openai-ohio-data-center-guarantee-contractor-payment-risk): Nvidia was reportedly in early talks to backstop $250 billion in debt for a 10-gigawatt campus, with the deal described as fluid and possibly headed nowhere. It's no longer a rumor. Nvidia, OpenAI, and SB Energy announced the signed terms on August 17:

| | Reported in July | Signed August 17 |
|---|---|---|
| Nvidia guarantee | ~$250 billion (talks only) | Up to $105 billion, covering the first 4.25 GW |
| Total campus size | 10 GW | 8 GW-IT, with 3.75 GW of the guarantee still optional |
| First phase | ~800 MW | 4.25 GW |
| Nvidia's other involvement | Not specified | $1.5 billion direct equity stake in SB Energy |
| Deal status | Early-stage talks | Signed |

The site is the former Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant in Pike County, Ohio — the same location reported in July. SB Energy, SoftBank's energy subsidiary, is developing the campus and will own and operate it under a 20-year lease to OpenAI. The first capacity is targeted for 2028, with the full buildout running through 2032.

## Who does a GC actually sign with here?

This is the piece that was still fuzzy in July. With the deal signed, the contracting chain is specific: SB Energy is the developer and long-term owner. OpenAI is the tenant paying rent under a 20-year lease. Nvidia is a financial guarantor plus a minority equity investor in SB Energy — not a party to construction contracts. For a GC or major sub evaluating this award, the entity whose bonding requirements, payment terms, and change-order process you're actually working under is SB Energy (or its general contractor), not OpenAI and not Nvidia. The guarantee is what got the debt priced and the project funded; it doesn't sit anywhere in your pay chain.

## Why did the guarantee shrink by more than half?

Going from a rumored $250 billion covering a full 10-gigawatt build to a signed $105 billion covering the first 4.25 gigawatts — with the remaining 3.75 gigawatts left as an option rather than a commitment — reads as lenders and Nvidia declining to underwrite the whole campus in one shot. That's consistent with what this newsletter has tracked all year: even with a chipmaker's balance sheet behind it, debt markets are pricing AI data center risk in phases, not all at once. A 35,000-job, six-year construction commitment is real, but the financing structure signals that the people writing the checks still want to see the first phase perform before extending to the rest.

## The takeaway for anyone bidding this work

The mechanic's lien and payment-bond questions from July haven't changed — Nvidia's guarantee still doesn't reach subcontractors directly. What's new is that there's now a specific, named owner of record to run diligence on: SB Energy's bonding capacity, its track record on prior builds, and its GC selection process, rather than an abstract question about who might eventually stand behind the money. If your firm is being pulled into pre-qualification for PORTS-Pike or a comparable SB Energy site, that's where the due diligence should start.
