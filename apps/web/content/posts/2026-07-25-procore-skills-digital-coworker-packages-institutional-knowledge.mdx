Procore moved its AI agents out of private beta and into three paid, generally available packages on July 23 — and previewed a companion feature called Skills that lets a firm feed the AI its own standard operating procedures instead of relying on generic defaults. That's the more consequential half of the announcement. Pricing tiers are a normal product step. A feature explicitly built to capture "how the company operates" before the people who know how the company operates retire is a direct answer to a problem every GC already has.

## What did Procore actually ship?

Three Digital Coworker packages, all now generally available: Starter Pack (five agents), Pro (the full library of 20 agents — covering things like Deep Search, Submittals, RFI, Daily Log, and Contract Review), and Enterprise, which adds Agent Studio for building custom agents on top of the pre-built set. Every tier includes Control Tower, an admin dashboard that reports AI usage and credit consumption by agent, project, or team member — the kind of visibility a controller needs before signing off on a company-wide AI line item.

| Package | What's included |
|---|---|
| Starter Pack | 5 pre-built AI agents |
| Pro | Full library of 20 pre-built AI agents |
| Enterprise | Everything in Pro, plus Agent Studio for custom agents and deeper customization |
| All tiers | Control Tower usage/credit tracking; Skills rolling out in August |

## What does Skills actually do?

Skills lets an organization upload its own SOPs, project standards, or plain-language process descriptions, and have Procore's agents apply that specific method — not a generic industry default — across every project. Procore SVP of AI and data Thiago da Costa framed the goal directly: AI output that "consistently reflects how the company operates," plus "a way to preserve institutional knowledge as experienced workers retire, change roles, or leave the organization." That's not a vague AI pitch. It names the exact failure mode a lot of GCs are already living through — a superintendent with 25 years of unwritten judgment calls walks out the door, and the next hire relearns all of it from scratch, on your schedule's dime.

## What should a GC actually do with this?

Skills only works on what your firm has actually written down. If your submittal review process, your RFI escalation rules, or your daily-log standards live entirely in one PM's head, Skills has nothing to encode yet — and that's worth treating as the real prerequisite here, not the AI feature itself. Before August:

1. **Audit what's actually documented.** Pull your current SOPs for submittals, RFIs, and daily logs. If they're thin or outdated, that's the gap Skills will expose, not fix.
2. **Ask what "generally available" costs.** Procore hasn't published per-tier pricing publicly as of this announcement — get real numbers from your account team before assuming Starter Pack is the safe default.
3. **Keep the approval line where it is.** When Procore first demoed these agents in June, it was explicit that the Submittal agent flags discrepancies and the RFI agent suggests edits — a human still decides. Nothing in this announcement suggests that changed, and Control Tower's usage tracking is about visibility, not an approval workflow. Don't let a Skills rollout quietly shift sign-off authority to the agent by default.

The Haskell case study Procore cited — submittal review time cut from seven days to 10 minutes — is a real number from a real customer, but it's one firm's result, not a benchmark. If you're evaluating Pro or Enterprise, ask your Procore rep for the same metric on a submittal volume close to yours before you budget the time savings into next year's staffing plan.
