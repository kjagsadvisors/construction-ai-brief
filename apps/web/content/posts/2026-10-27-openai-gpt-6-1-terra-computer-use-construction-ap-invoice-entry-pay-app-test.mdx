A subcontractor's accounts payable desk can now test a computer-use AI that is cheap enough to try on real invoices. OpenAI launched GPT-6.1 Terra on October 26, a mid-tier model that works a screen the way a person does: it looks, clicks, types and switches between apps. Its lead demo is reading incoming invoices and entering them into accounting software.

## What did OpenAI actually release?

Terra sits below OpenAI's top models and is priced at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens, according to coverage from [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-gpt-6-1-terra-computer-use) and [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/openai-launches-gpt-6-1-terra-with-advanced-computer-use-capabilities-at-half-the-api-price-563959-2026-10-27). It is available in ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu, and in Codex.

OpenAI's performance claims are its own: roughly the capability of its larger Astra model at about a tenth of the price per token, and much faster completion times. The Neuron's write-up notes independent tests found a smaller speed gap, and that on a 15-field form test run three times, Terra got every field right each time. An OpenAI engineer described it as more a way to stretch usage limits than a breakthrough ([The Neuron](https://www.theneurondaily.com/p/openai-s-big-day-brought-a-disproof-and-terra)).

## Why should a sub's back office care?

Because most contractor admin is screen work: keying vendor invoices, matching them to POs, entering pay application lines, updating job-cost codes. Until now, automating that meant fragile integrations or a human typing. A model that operates the software directly doesn't need an integration, which is useful for the small shops running older accounting packages.

The cost point matters too. Cheap per-token pricing is what makes a "try it on every invoice" experiment affordable instead of a budget request.

## What are the limits?

- **Money moves need a person.** A wrong cost code or a duplicate payment is a margin problem, not a typo.
- **Benchmarks are the vendor's.** Nobody has tested it on a retainage-heavy AIA pay app or a lien waiver packet yet.
- **Screens change.** Computer-use agents can break when a vendor portal or accounting screen updates.
- **Data exposure.** Pointing an agent at your accounting system gives it your vendor and pricing data. Check your plan's data terms first.

## How should a sub test it?

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1. Pick one task | Vendor invoice entry for one job, not the whole AP process |
| 2. Use a sandbox | A test company file or copied data, never live books |
| 3. Run 50 real invoices | Include messy ones: handwritten, multi-page, credit memos |
| 4. Score against staff | Compare fields keyed: vendor, amount, job, cost code, date |
| 5. Time and cost it | Include review time, not just model time |
| 6. Decide | Keep a human approval step on anything that pays |

## The takeaway

Don't buy a back-office overhaul on launch-day claims. Spend two weeks running Terra against 50 of your own invoices in a sandbox, count the field errors, and compare review time to keying time. If the error rate is low and a clerk can approve in seconds, you have a real hours-saved number. If not, you've spent a few dollars finding out.
