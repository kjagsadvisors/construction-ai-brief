CloudNC, a London-based machining software company, raised a $20 million round on September 8 — with Lockheed Martin's venture arm among the backers — to scale CAM Assist, AI software that automates CNC programming, and to launch a new tool called Quote Agent that turns a technical drawing into a price in minutes instead of days. Neither product touches construction. But the two problems they're built to solve — the gap between a finished digital model and a machine that can cut it, and the gap between a drawing landing in the inbox and a number you can bid — are the same two bottlenecks that slow down structural steel, precast, and MEP prefab shops running their own CNC equipment.

## What did CloudNC actually announce?

Nimble Ventures led the $20 million Series B extension, joined by Calculus Venture Capital, Entrepreneur First, and LM Capital — Lockheed Martin's venture fund — bringing CloudNC's total funding since its 2015 founding to $128 million. CAM Assist, its lead product, plugs into Autodesk Fusion and Mastercam and automates the programming step for CNC mills and lathes; it's now used at more than 1,000 machine shops, roughly 80% of them in the US, including named customers Lockheed Martin and Major Tool and Machine. CloudNC is also working with Knox Systems toward FedRAMP certification, aiming CAM Assist at defense-contractor workloads. Quote Agent, the new product funded by this round, is due later in 2026.

## Does this apply to a steel fabricator or precast plant, or just aerospace parts?

Directly, no. CAM Assist programs discrete-part milling and turning machines — the equipment that cuts aircraft brackets and valve bodies, not the plate and beam lines (commonly Peddinghaus or Voortman) that structural steel fabricators run, or the CNC routers precast plants use for formwork. Those run on different machine types and different CAM software entirely, with no current bridge to CloudNC's tools. The closest overlap is an MEP prefab shop running its own CNC routers, plasma tables, or waterjets to cut brackets, sleeves, and unistrut — those are, mechanically, the same kind of shop CloudNC already sells into.

## What's the actual bottleneck, and why should an estimator care?

| Step | Aerospace machine shop today | Structural steel / precast fab shop today |
|---|---|---|
| Model to machine | CAM Assist auto-generates the program from a Fusion or Mastercam file | A detailer manually posts a Tekla or SDS2 model to the plasma, laser, or drill line |
| Drawing to price | Quote Agent estimates cost and cycle time in minutes | An estimator manually takes off connections, calculates fab hours, and builds a number over days |
| Who loses when it's slow | The shop that quotes last loses the job to a faster competitor | The fabricator that quotes late loses the bid, or eats margin pricing it in a rush |

CloudNC's own framing of the quoting problem — move too slowly and lose the opportunity, price inaccurately under pressure and lose margin once the job is in production — is a line-for-line description of what a steel or precast estimating desk deals with on every bid.

## Should a fabrication shop go buy CloudNC's software?

No — it doesn't run on the equipment construction fabricators own, and there's no announced construction integration. The useful move is watching the vendors that do sit on top of steel and precast workflows. Tekla, SDS2, and similar detailing platforms already hold the model; the open question is whether any of them ship an equivalent of CAM Assist (auto-generating NC programs for a plate or beam line straight from the model) or Quote Agent (auto-estimating a fabrication price from a drawing or takeoff). Neither exists in structural steel or precast today. CloudNC's raise, and the fact that a defense prime's venture arm backed it, is a signal that a well-funded competitor could build the equivalent for your equipment before your incumbent vendor does.
