An AI model can now produce a first-draft physical design in days, but it still misses what the shop floor needs. That is the useful lesson from OpenAI's own account of building a device with GPT-6 Astra, and it maps directly onto prefab shop drawings, spool sheets and rack layouts.

## What did OpenAI say GPT-6 Astra did?

According to [The Decoder's summary](https://the-decoder.com/openai-says-gpt-6-astra-designed-a-hardware-device-from-scratch-in-under-a-week/) of OpenAI's post, two employees used Astra to design a device for talking to the Codex coding agent. The model handled the enclosure, the circuit board layout, firmware and interface, and the design took under a week. A contract manufacturer then built prototypes.

The catch is in the same account. Several revision rounds were needed because Astra "didn't always account for manufacturing constraints, tolerances, and assembly details." The [Neuron's daily roundup](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-wednesday-november-4-2026/) reports the same: prototypes were built, and revisions followed because the model missed manufacturing requirements. OpenAI's original post is [here](https://openai.com/index/accelerating-engineering-for-the-physical-world/), though we could not load it directly, so the details above rest on those two outlets. This is also OpenAI describing its own project, not an independent test.

## Why does this matter to a prefab shop or detailer?

A prefab shop sells the thing that AI skipped: knowing whether a drawing can be built with the saw, brake, welder and crew you actually have. Three places the same gap shows up:

| Work product | What AI can draft | What still needs your people |
|---|---|---|
| Spool and hanger drawings | Layouts, part lists, option studies | Weld access, field fit-up, support spacing against the real structure |
| Electrical rack or skid assemblies | Component placement, wire lists | Clearances, heat, torque access, shipping size |
| Modular wall or MEP racks | Dimensioned first pass | Tolerances to the surrounding trades, lift and transport limits |

The pattern is the same as the device: a fast, plausible draft that fails on tolerances and assembly sequence.

## What changes for estimators and PMs?

1. **Estimators:** Draft-stage design time may shrink, but don't cut the checking hours. Price the review, not just the drafting.
2. **PMs:** If a GC or owner hands you AI-generated layouts, treat them as a proposal. Ask who stamps or accepts responsibility when a revision is needed.
3. **Shop foremen:** You are the constructability filter. A short checklist of your shop's limits (max sizes, bend radii, welding positions) is the thing to hand to any AI tool.

## Should a mid-size sub act now?

Run a small test rather than a rollout. Take one past assembly you already built, give an AI tool the same inputs, and have your foreman mark every place the draft would not have worked. Count the issues and the review time. If the draft plus review beats your normal drafting hours, use it on low-risk items first.

Limits worth stating: this is one company's single hardware project, the revision count wasn't specified in the coverage we could check, and construction assemblies carry code, inspection and warranty obligations that a consumer gadget doesn't.

The takeaway for your next prefab job: let AI draft, but keep the person who knows the shop in charge of "can we build this."
