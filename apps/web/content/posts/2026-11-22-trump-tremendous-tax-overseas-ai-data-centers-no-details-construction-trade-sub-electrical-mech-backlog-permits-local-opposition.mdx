President Trump said on November 20 that US tech companies building AI data centers in other countries will face "a tremendous tax." He gave no rate, no scope and no start date, and said he had not yet told his own staff. For an electrical or mechanical sub deciding whether to add crews or inventory for data center work, that is not a reason to change anything yet. The same reporting points to a bigger near-term risk for the domestic pipeline: local permits.

## What exactly did Trump say?

Speaking in the White House briefing room on Friday, Trump said, "We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country," and "We're going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try and do it," according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/corporations/trump-data-centers-overseas-penalties-rcna610445). He compared it to chipmaking, which he said was allowed to move overseas.

He also added, "I haven't even said that to my people." The White House did not immediately respond to NBC's request for details. The [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261121/trump-vows-tremendous-tax-for-us-firms-building-data-centers-in-other-countries) and [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/world/ai-data-centres-us-tech-companies-trump-9.7430426) reported the same lack of specifics.

| What was said | What is still missing |
|---|---|
| US firms building AI data centers abroad will be taxed | The rate or amount |
| "Tremendous penalties" for firms that try | Which companies, countries or projects it covers |
| Restrictions are needed to keep AI infrastructure in the US | How it would be implemented: executive order, tariff, or a bill |
| Communities should "make them pay you" rather than block data centers | Any start date, or whether staff were consulted |

## Does this mean more data center work for US subs?

Not on its own. Hyperscalers pick sites based on power, land, fiber, permits and financing. A remark with no mechanism does not change any of those. If an actual rule follows, it could push more capacity onshore over time, but there is nothing to price into a bid today.

The same week's reporting cuts the other way. [AP](https://abcnews.com/Technology/wireStory/trump-plans-penalize-us-tech-companies-build-ai-138323831) reports that host communities across the US are turning against data centers and that opposition is blocking municipal permits. NBC reports that voters in this year's midterms backed candidates who promised to curb data center growth, and that the S&P 500 has fallen about 9% since November 4. We covered the financing side of that pressure earlier this week: [banks are selling data center construction loans below face value](/posts/2026-11-19-banks-sell-ai-data-center-construction-loans-discount-hsbc-ing-socgen-construction-trade-sub-payment-bond-lien-retainage-check).

## What should an electrical or mechanical sub do with this?

Treat federal policy as a watch item and local approval as the real gate. Before you commit crews, equipment or long-lead gear to a data center job:

1. **Confirm what is actually approved.** Ask whether zoning, site plan approval and building permits are issued, pending or under challenge. "The owner is confident" is not an answer.
2. **Ask about community deals.** Trump told communities to "make them pay you." AP reports developers are offering more money to host communities. Those agreements can add scope, local-hire requirements or work-hour limits. Find out before you price the job.
3. **Write permit risk into your clarifications.** State what happens to your schedule, mobilization costs and stored material if a permit is delayed or a challenge stops work.
4. **Don't hire ahead of a rule.** If you're tempted to add a crew because "everything's coming back onshore," wait for an executive order or bill with a rate and a start date.
5. **Keep watching for the follow-up.** If the White House publishes a mechanism, read who it covers. A rule aimed only at advanced AI facilities would affect different owners than one covering all overseas data centers.

## What's still unclear?

Everything that would let a contractor plan. Nobody knows yet whether there will be a rule, what it would cover, or when it would start.

**The takeaway:** a "tremendous tax" with no rate is a headline, not backlog. If you have data center work in your pipeline, spend this week on the question that decides whether those jobs get built on time: are the local permits in hand, and what does your subcontract say if they aren't?
