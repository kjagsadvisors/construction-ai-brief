Anthropic now lets eligible businesses run Claude without Anthropic keeping their prompts. The prompts and answers go into the customer's own Amazon or Google cloud storage instead. The catch for a general contractor is that someone at the company has to read the misuse alerts this system raises. If your firm runs Claude through the API or Amazon Bedrock on jobs where the owner limits who can hold the drawings, decide who that reviewer is before you turn it on.

## What did Anthropic launch?

[Anthropic's beta page](https://claude.com/resources/articles/zero-data-retention-with-enterprise-frontier-safeguards), dated November 23, says zero data retention with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS) is now in public beta. EFS was announced in September and built with more than 100 customers. Bridgewater, Salesforce and Stripe tested it privately.

Here is how it works, according to Anthropic:

- Each prompt and answer is written to storage the customer owns, either Amazon S3 or Google Cloud Storage, and then deleted on Anthropic's side.
- Automated monitoring scans that stored record for misuse patterns that span many requests. Anthropic's single-request filters can't catch those.
- When it finds something, it opens a Safety Case with a case ID, harm category, status and flagged transcript IDs. Anthropic sees those fields. Only the customer's team can read the actual prompts and answers.
- Inputs and outputs are never used for training, and no Anthropic or cloud provider staff review them.

The reason for the stored record, [The Next Web reports](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-enterprise-frontier-safeguards-zero-data-retention-beta), is that Anthropic began keeping data for 30 days when it launched Fable 5, and regulated firms pushed back. Anthropic's September threat report described an AI agent attack on about 50 organizations that was split into small tasks, [Interesting Engineering notes](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/anthropic-enterprise-safeguards-zero-data-retention). Catching that kind of attack takes records across sessions. EFS keeps those records in the customer's account instead of Anthropic's.

## Why does this matter on a construction job?

Owners on hospital, federal, data center and other secure work often write contract language about who can hold their drawings, specs and site information. If your precon or project engineering team has built a tool that sends spec sections or RFI drafts to Claude through the API, a 30-day copy on a third party's servers is the kind of thing an owner's IT reviewer asks about. EFS gives you a cleaner answer: Anthropic doesn't keep the content, and the record sits in your cloud account under your encryption keys.

It also hands you work you didn't have before.

| What changes | Who at a GC it lands on |
|---|---|
| Every prompt and answer is stored in your AWS or Google account | Whoever sets retention and access on that account, usually IT or the managed IT provider |
| Safety Cases have to be reviewed | Someone who can read a flagged transcript and judge it. The Next Web reports a seven-day window, or you risk losing access |
| Access policies, audit logging and encryption keys have to be configured | Same person, before go-live |
| Storage, reads, writes and transfer are billed by the cloud provider | Whoever owns the cloud bill |

A record of every prompt your estimators and PEs sent is also a business record. Ask your counsel how long you should keep it, the same way you would for project email.

## Does this cover the tools we already use?

Probably not directly. Anthropic lists the Claude Platform, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock and Claude on Google Cloud. Microsoft Foundry is "coming soon." The page doesn't mention the Claude chat app plans. If a construction software vendor calls Claude for you, the vendor is the customer, so the vendor decides whether to enroll. Ask them, along with the provider questions from our piece on [the Dragos canary prompt study](/posts/2026-11-22-dragos-two-million-canary-prompts-three-ai-providers-breached-zdr-ignored-construction-trade-sub-homemade-ai-tools-which-provider-serves-it).

## What are the limits?

It's a beta for "eligible businesses," and Anthropic doesn't define who qualifies. It says eligible customers will be notified. The seven-day review rule comes from The Next Web's reporting. Anthropic's own page lists reviewing Safety Cases as your job but gives no deadline, so confirm the terms when you enroll. Anthropic names Accenture, Deloitte, KPMG and PwC as deployment partners. That tells you who this was built for first. A 150-person GC with no security team can still use it, but only if someone actually checks the cases.

**Takeaway:** If you run Claude through the API or Bedrock on any job with an owner data clause, pick one named person to review Safety Cases and write down how long you'll keep the stored prompts. Do both before you enroll. Then send the owner's IT reviewer a one-paragraph description of where the data now lives.
