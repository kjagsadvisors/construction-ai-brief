Mistral Large 4 is the first trillion-parameter model whose weights are promised for download, and for a trade sub the point is control: a model you can host privately is one you can point at bid history and labor rates without sending them to a third party's API. It isn't a tool you install next week, and the license terms aren't known yet.

## What did Mistral actually release?

Mistral launched a public preview of Large 4 on October 6 through its own cloud platform, per [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/06/mistral-launches-open-source-mistral-large-4-details-ai-roadmap/). It is a mixture-of-experts model with about 1 trillion total parameters that activates roughly 49 billion for each request, according to [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/06/mistral-ai-releases-mistral-large-4-le-chonk-a-1-05t-parameter-open-weight-multimodal-moe/). Reports say it was trained over about two months on roughly 4,000 Nvidia Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral's European data centers.

The weights are promised for October 27. On coding, Mistral's reported DeepSWE score is 62%, narrowly ahead of Zhipu's GLM-5.3 at 61% and DeepSeek-V4-Pro at 57%, per [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/mistral-releases-large-4-a-1-trillion-parameter-open-weight-ai-model). Those are vendor-reported benchmark numbers; treat them as a starting point, not proof it handles your documents.

## Why would a subcontractor care about open weights?

Your most sensitive files are not drawings. They are old bids, labor burden rates, markup, supplier quotes and the numbers behind your win rate. Many owners and primes also restrict where project documents can go. A hosted model means that data crosses to a vendor. An open-weight model can run somewhere you control.

| Option | Where your data goes | Setup effort | Fits when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closed model via vendor app | Vendor's cloud, under their terms | Minutes | General drafting, nothing sensitive |
| Closed model via enterprise plan | Vendor's cloud, with contract limits on retention and training | Days | You can accept the vendor contract |
| Open-weight model, managed private host | A host's environment under your contract | Weeks | Sensitive bid data, one IT partner |
| Open-weight model, your own hardware | Your building or your cloud account | Months plus hardware | You already run servers and have IT staff |

## What are the limits?

1. **The license is unconfirmed.** Reports say Large 4 will use a custom Mistral license rather than Apache 2.0, per [Beri](https://www.beri.net/article/mistral-large-4-le-chonk-1t-open-weight-preview-pricing-licence-weights-vs-kimi-k3-beam-glm). Commercial-use terms can't be checked until the weights are out.
2. **It is big.** A trillion-parameter model needs a multi-GPU server even though only a slice runs per request. For a 50-person sub, that points to a managed host, not a rack in the shop.
3. **Open weights are not a security program.** Running privately removes one exposure. You still need access controls and a rule on what staff may paste where.
4. **Benchmarks aren't your work.** Nothing here tests it on spec extraction, takeoff notes or bid leveling.

## What should a sub do before October 27?

- Write down which of your files you would never send to an outside API. That list decides whether a private model is worth pursuing.
- Ask your IT provider whether they host models for clients, and what it would cost per month.
- Check your prime contracts for clauses on where project documents can be processed. See our earlier piece on [who builds AI in-house](/posts/2026-10-05-anthropic-frontier-academy-construction-who-builds-ai-in-house).
- When the weights and license publish, run a closed bid through it and compare against the number you submitted. If it can't reproduce your scope list, the privacy gain doesn't matter.

Most subs should do nothing but the first step this month. The point of open weights is that the choice to keep data private now has a real option behind it.
