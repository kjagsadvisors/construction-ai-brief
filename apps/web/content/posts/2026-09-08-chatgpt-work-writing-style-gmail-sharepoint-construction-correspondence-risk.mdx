OpenAI's ChatGPT Work can now learn how a specific person writes — their phrasing, sign-offs, and formatting habits — by reading their connected Gmail, Google Drive, Slack, and SharePoint content, then draft new messages in that voice. For a PM who writes the same three kinds of documents on repeat — RFI responses, daily logs, owner update emails — that's a real fit. It's also a reason to read the fine print before connecting a company mailbox, because OpenAI hasn't said how long it keeps the samples it reads or whether they train on them.

## What exactly did OpenAI ship?

The feature, called Writing Style, rolled out September 7 to paid ChatGPT Work users on the web. In Settings under Personalization, a user connects Gmail, Google Drive, Slack, and SharePoint, and ChatGPT reads content from those apps to pick up recurring word choices, sign-off habits, and capitalization quirks — the small tics that make a draft sound like a specific person instead of generic AI copy. OpenAI says the feature does not use anything typed directly into a ChatGPT conversation; it draws only from the connected apps a user authorizes.

## What's the actual construction use here?

A GC project engineer writes dozens of RFI responses a month, each one needing the same defensive, precise register — cite the spec section, state the field condition, don't concede scope. A super files a daily log every night in a voice that's terse and specific. An ops director's owner-facing emails read differently than the same person's texts to a foreman. Those are exactly the repetitive, voice-specific writing tasks this feature targets:

| Document type | Who writes it | Why voice matters |
|---|---|---|
| RFI response | PM / project engineer | Precise, non-concessive language protects against future claims |
| Daily log narrative | Superintendent | Terse, factual, consistent format day to day |
| Owner update email | PM / ops director | Formal register, different from field communication |
| Bid cover letter | Estimator | Matches the firm's established client-facing tone |
| Change-order correspondence | Sub-side PM / owner | Needs to read as the same person across a negotiation |

Done well, this cuts the editing pass a PM currently does after an AI-drafted RFI response comes back sounding like nobody in particular. Done carelessly, it means routing a mailbox full of pricing, personnel, and dispute correspondence through a connector nobody on the team has actually reviewed.

## What's the catch?

To get the personalization, ChatGPT needs read access to the apps where that writing already lives — which for most PMs means the same inbox and SharePoint site holding change-order negotiations, subcontractor disputes, and pricing discussions. Coverage of the launch has flagged the same gap: OpenAI hasn't published how long the referenced writing samples are retained or whether they factor into model training. That's not a confirmed problem — it's an open question the company hasn't answered, and it's the wrong thing to guess about with a company's actual correspondence history.

## Should a GC or sub act on this now?

Not by turning it on for the whole PM staff on day one. The sequence that makes sense:

1. **Pilot with one volunteer's individual account** — not a shared PM inbox, not the whole team's SharePoint site.
2. **Check the actual OAuth scope** the Gmail/Drive/Slack/SharePoint connector requests. "Connect Gmail" can mean read access to an entire mailbox, not just RFI-related threads — know which one you're granting.
3. **Ask your OpenAI rep in writing** how long Writing Style reference data is retained and whether it's used for training, and get that answer before extending access past the pilot.
4. **Measure the actual editing time saved** on a real RFI response or daily log against what a PM does today with a generic prompt.

If the drafts genuinely need less cleanup and the access question gets a real answer, it's worth expanding. If either one is unclear, the productivity gain isn't worth connecting a full mailbox on a guess.
