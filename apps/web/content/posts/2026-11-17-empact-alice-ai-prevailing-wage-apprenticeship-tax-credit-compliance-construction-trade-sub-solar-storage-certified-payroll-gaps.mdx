If your crew is building a solar or battery storage project that claims federal energy tax credits, the owner's compliance team can now type "which contractors are missing payroll records?" and get a list back with the documents attached. Empact Technologies launched that assistant, called Alice, on November 16. For an electrical or mechanical sub, the practical effect is that payroll gaps on these jobs get found sooner and in more detail than a quarterly document chase ever managed.

The details come from Empact's [press release](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/empact-technologies-launches-alice-the-energy-industrys-first-ai-compliance-expert-to-protect-tax-credits-302939098.html) and [product page](https://empacttechnologies.com/alice). The claims about what Alice can do are the vendor's own.

## What did Empact launch?

Alice sits on top of NexusIQ, Empact's compliance platform, which the company says reviews every required and supplemental payroll record, document and contractor submission on a project. Alice lets a developer, asset owner or EPC ask questions of that data in plain language. Empact's own example questions:

- What's still open before our tax equity closing?
- Which contractors are missing payroll records?
- Which projects have outstanding compliance issues?
- What needs to happen next to resolve this issue?

Answers link back to source documents and, per the release, go "down to worker-level wage discrepancies." Empact says it has supported more than 800 projects, 19 GW of capacity and over $5 billion in tax credit value, and has more than 80 in-house compliance staff who still chase documents and close issues with contractors.

## Why would a sub care about an owner's compliance tool?

Because the rules reach all the way down the contract chain. The [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/frequently-asked-questions-about-the-prevailing-wage-and-apprenticeship-under-the-inflation-reduction-act) says a project that meets the prevailing wage and apprenticeship requirements multiplies its base credit by five, and that the wage requirement covers "all laborers and mechanics employed by the taxpayer, contractor, or subcontractor."

| Requirement (per IRS) | What it means on the job | Cost of a miss |
|---|---|---|
| Prevailing wage | Every laborer and mechanic paid at least the prevailing rate for their classification | Back pay plus interest at the federal short-term rate plus 6 points, and a $5,000 penalty per underpaid worker |
| Apprentice labor hours | 15% of total labor hours by qualified apprentices, for construction beginning in 2024 or later | $50 per labor hour short; $500 if intentional |
| Apprentice ratio | The registered program's apprentice-to-journeyworker ratio met each day apprentices work | Falls under the apprenticeship penalty above; needs daily records to prove |
| Participation | Any contractor or sub with 4 or more employees on the work hires at least one apprentice | Falls under the apprenticeship penalty above |

Those penalties land on the taxpayer claiming the credit, which is the owner. The release says a single missing payroll record or unresolved contractor issue can delay a tax equity closing or a credit transfer. An owner in that position leans on the EPC, and the EPC leans on the sub whose record is missing. Read your subcontract to see what it says about who pays if your payroll causes a shortfall.

## What can't Alice do yet?

Quite a bit, going by Empact's own page:

- **Subs can't fix issues in it.** Uploading evidence, bulk issue updates and re-running checks are listed as "coming later this year." For now a flagged issue still gets resolved by email and resubmittal.
- **No accuracy numbers.** Neither the release nor the product page gives an error rate. The example on the product page is labeled illustrative, not a live project.
- **No pricing.** Not disclosed.
- **It only knows what was submitted.** An assistant reading payroll records can spot a rate below the wage determination. It cannot tell whether a worker classified as a laborer spent the day pulling wire.

That last one is the judgment call that stays with the sub's foreman and payroll clerk.

## What should an electrical or mechanical sub do now?

Assume the review of your certified payroll is getting faster and more granular, whether the owner uses Empact or one of its competitors. Before the next pay period on any tax-credit job:

1. **Check classifications against the wage determination** in your subcontract, worker by worker, including fringe benefits.
2. **Track apprentice hours weekly**, not at closeout. The 15% is measured on total labor hours, and the ratio is measured daily.
3. **Keep the paper on apprentice requests.** The IRS lists written requests to registered programs among the records that show compliance.
4. **Ask the EPC which platform the owner uses** and whether you get a login or a list of open items.
