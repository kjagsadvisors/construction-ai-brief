Cisco said this week that AI agents, including Anthropic's Claude, will be able to join Webex spaces, meetings and calls and keep working after the people log off. Almost all of it is planned for early 2027. For a GC's project manager, the useful work now is deciding who owns the meeting record before an agent starts writing it.

## What did Cisco actually announce?

At WebexOne in Austin on October 7, Cisco described agents that can be invited into meetings and calls the way a person is. Per SiliconANGLE, Unite.AI and Cisco's release:

- Agents can take notes, schedule, translate and handle multi-step tasks across Webex and third-party apps.
- Claude, running on Claude Managed Agents, is planned as one of those agents. OpenAI's "dots" is planned through an MCP integration.
- Cisco is also adding governance tools, including deepfake detection from Pindrop and a compliance hub with Theta Lake.

Timing is the catch. Coverage says most features arrive in early 2027, and we found no firm date for the Claude piece. Treat this as a preview, not a rollout.

## Why does this matter on a jobsite?

Weekly OAC meetings, preconstruction coordination, and sub kickoff calls all produce a record. Someone writes minutes, someone disputes them, and months later those minutes show up in a delay claim or a change order fight. An agent that attends and writes up the meeting changes who holds the pen.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Who issues the minutes? | Today it is usually the GC or architect. An agent draft needs a named person to accept it. |
| Who can correct them? | Late corrections are how disputes start. |
| Is the call recorded or transcribed? | Consent rules vary by state, and owners may prohibit it. |
| Where does the transcript live? | In a vendor's cloud, your document system, or both. |
| What can the agent do after the call? | Cisco's pitch includes follow-on work, so check whether it can send anything outside the team. |

## What should a PM do before 2027?

1. Add one line to your meeting protocol: no AI note-taker or agent joins without every party agreeing in writing, in the meeting invite or the minutes of the first meeting.
2. Name a human who reviews and issues the minutes. The agent drafts; a person owns it.
3. Keep the raw transcript out of the contract record unless the contract calls for it. Draft notes that disagree with issued minutes are an argument waiting to happen.
4. Ask your owner and architect how they handle it. Some will already have a policy.

We covered the related liability question in [the FTC's view that whoever instructs an agent owns what it does](/posts/2026-10-01-ftc-agent-liability-instructed-conduct-construction-ai-audit-trail).

## What is still hype or unproven?

Nobody has shown that an agent in a noisy, multi-party coordination meeting attributes decisions to the right person or catches a verbal scope change. Cisco's examples are office tasks like pulling usage data and updating a deck. Jobsite trailer calls with a speakerphone and six people talking over each other are a harder test, and we have not seen results.

The takeaway: you do not need the product to fix the policy. Write down who issues the minutes and whether agents may attend, and you will be ready whichever vendor shows up first.
