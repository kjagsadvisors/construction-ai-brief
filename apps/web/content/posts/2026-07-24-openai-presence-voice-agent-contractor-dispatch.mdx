OpenAI's newest product isn't a chatbot upgrade — it's a claim that AI can now run a company's phone line. Presence, which OpenAI launched on July 22, resolves 75% of inbound calls on its own English-language support line without a human stepping in, according to the company. For a mechanical, electrical, or plumbing sub that lives and dies by its dispatch and service-call line, that's a real number worth paying attention to. The catch: Presence isn't for sale to a 50-person trade contractor, and a cheaper version of the same idea is already running on their phones.

## What did OpenAI actually launch?

Presence is a platform for building voice and chat agents that combine a company's own policies, guardrails, and escalation rules with OpenAI's models, so the agent can answer questions, pull account context, take pre-approved actions, and hand off to a person when a rule says it should. OpenAI is running it on its own support line — reachable at 1-888-GPT-0090 — where it says the system verifies callers, resolves issues, and now handles 75% of calls end-to-end. A Codex-powered feedback loop reviews the agent's real conversations and proposes behavior changes that staff test and approve, which OpenAI says cut human handoffs by 15 percentage points in 10 days. Early enterprise use cases are customer support, sales, and internal workflows at banks, telecoms, and insurers.

## Why doesn't this reach a trade contractor yet?

Presence isn't a product you sign up for. It rolled out through limited general availability, backed by OpenAI's $10 billion Deployment Company (formed after its acquisition of the consultancy Tomoro), and deployments are led by OpenAI's own Forward Deployed Engineers or select systems integrators. Pricing isn't public — it's scoped per customer, which multiple outlets have flagged as enterprise-consulting rates rather than a monthly software bill. That model fits a call center handling tens of thousands of contacts a month. It doesn't fit a mechanical sub's two-person dispatch desk.

## What does a trade contractor actually have access to right now?

A market of AI phone-answering tools built specifically for contractors already exists, and several run on the same underlying models Presence uses. Services like OnCrew and Bland AI sell AI voice agents that answer, qualify, and route service calls for HVAC, plumbing, and electrical companies today, priced closer to the traditional live-answering-service range than to enterprise consulting.

| | OpenAI Presence | Contractor-market AI answering (OnCrew, Bland AI, similar) | Traditional live answering service |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it's sold to | Large enterprises (banking, telecom, insurance) | Trades and home-service contractors | Trades and home-service contractors |
| Deployment | OpenAI Forward Deployed Engineers / systems integrators | Self-serve signup | Self-serve signup |
| Pricing | Scoped per customer, not public | Roughly $50–$300/month depending on volume | Roughly $400–$800/month for full 24/7 coverage |
| Escalation/guardrail layer | Built and tuned by OpenAI + customer policy team | Vendor-defined, often less customizable | Human dispatcher judgment |

## What should a contractor evaluating any of these actually check?

Presence's real contribution isn't the phone call itself — voice AI answering trade calls isn't new. It's the explicit policy and escalation layer: a written rule set for what the agent can decide alone and what it must hand to a person, tested before it goes live and revised as those rules change. That's the question worth asking any AI answering vendor, not just OpenAI's: can you see the escalation rules in writing, and do they name every life-safety call type — gas odor, no heat in freezing weather, active flooding, electrical burning smell — as an automatic route to a human, every time? A tool that can't answer that isn't ready for a service line, regardless of price.
