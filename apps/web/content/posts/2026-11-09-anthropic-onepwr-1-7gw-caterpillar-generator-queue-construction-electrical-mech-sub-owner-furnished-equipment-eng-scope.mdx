Anthropic has reportedly bought a place in line for 1.7 GW of Caterpillar gas generators, along with the engineering and construction work to install them. For electrical and mechanical subs, the useful lesson is that generation equipment is now a scarce asset owners trade directly, which changes what you are bidding and who carries delivery risk.

One caveat first: this is a single-source report. The Financial Times first reported it, citing a person familiar with the matter. As of the coverage we found, Anthropic, OnePWR and Caterpillar had not commented, and one summary noted OnePWR's own earlier figure was about 1.2 GW, below the 1.7 GW reported. Treat the details as provisional.

## What was reportedly bought?

According to [VKTR's summary](https://www.vktr.com/ai-market/anthropic-buys-its-way-out-of-the-power-crunch-with-34b-gas-grab/) and [AI Weekly's alert](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/anthropic-pays-34b-for-onepwrs-17gw-generator-order):

- **Price:** about $3.4 billion, or roughly $2 million per megawatt including installation.
- **Equipment:** Caterpillar gas generator orders that San Antonio supplier OnePWR Solutions placed about two years ago and could not finance.
- **Scope:** the engineering and construction work needed to install the units came along with the orders.
- **Not included:** OnePWR itself or its staff.
- **Timing:** the units were ordered before equipment prices rose and are expected over the next two years. New orders face longer backlogs.
- **Unknown:** where the generators will go.

VKTR also notes that Piper Sandler lists a $3.4 billion transaction in which it advised OnePWR on selling its power generation platform to an unnamed "frontier AI lab," which is consistent with the figure but does not name the buyer.

## Why does the equipment slot matter to a sub?

The money here is paying for time. A generator order placed two years ago is worth more than a new one because the new one may not arrive for years. That makes the queue position a tradeable asset, and owners with capital will buy it.

For a sub, that points to three things to check when a gas-generation package shows up:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Who furnishes the generators? | Owner-furnished equipment means you bid install, piping, tie-in and commissioning, not supply. |
| Who carries a delivery slip? | If units arrive late, standby, resequencing and extended general conditions need an owner-side answer. |
| Who stores and insures delivered units? | Large engines sitting on site create stored-material, security and warranty-start questions. |

## What should electrical subs look at?

On-site generation changes the one-line. Output has to be paralleled, protected and tied into campus distribution, usually with switchgear, step-up transformers and controls tested together. Our [piece on Google's Wyoming fuel cell campus](/posts/2026-11-06-google-wyoming-17b-702mw-fuel-cell-air-cooled-construction-mech-elec-sub-onsite-gas-power-scope) walks through the same shift. If an owner has already bought the generators, the open question is who procures the switchgear and transformers, since those have their own lead times.

## What should mechanical subs look at?

Gas-fired plants need fuel piping, exhaust, cooling and controls interfaces. The first question is whether the pipeline lateral, gas train and regulator station are in your scope, the utility's, or a separate contractor's. Welding procedures, testing and inspection responsibilities for gas piping should be written down before a price goes out.

## Does the construction scope come with the deal?

The reports say Anthropic took over the engineering and construction work needed to install the units, but they do not say who will self-perform it, who will be hired, or how packages will be split. Do not read this as a bid invitation. It is a signal that a buyer of this size is assembling power plants like an owner-developer, which usually means more owner-direct packages and fewer layers of a traditional GC.

## What are the limits?

Owning generation exposes the buyer to fuel prices, permitting and local opposition, as VKTR notes. Equipment in a queue is not equipment on a pad. Until sites, schedules and a delivery date are public, there is nothing to price.

## The takeaway

If you do medium-voltage, switchgear, gas piping or large-equipment rigging, add one standing question to your owner and GC conversations: who owns the generation equipment, and who pays when it arrives late? Put the answer in the subcontract before mobilizing.
