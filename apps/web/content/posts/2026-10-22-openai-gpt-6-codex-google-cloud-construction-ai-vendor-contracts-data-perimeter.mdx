**OpenAI's newest models and its Codex coding agent are coming to Google Cloud, so the same model can now be bought through all three big US cloud providers.** For a contractor, the model itself matters less than what the deal does to purchasing and data control: your software vendors will have more freedom to switch models, and you will have more places to ask where your files actually go.

## What was announced?

On October 21, 2026, OpenAI and Google Cloud said OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna models, plus Codex, are coming to Google Cloud's Model Garden. Reports say the models are in private preview now, with general availability planned in the coming weeks. They are also headed to Google's Antigravity development environment and Gemini Agent.

Customers will be able to buy the models through Google's marketplace, including against existing cloud spending commitments, with usage on a single Google Cloud invoice. Google says prompts and outputs stay inside the customer's data perimeter and are not used to train models. Neither company has disclosed pricing, a customer list, or the financial terms.

## What does this mean for a GC or sub?

Most contractors will never open Model Garden. The effect shows up one layer down, in the estimating, document-control, and field-reporting tools you already pay for.

- **Vendors get more freedom to swap models.** A tool built on one model on one cloud can now move more easily. That can mean price cuts, but it can also mean the engine behind your submittal or takeoff feature changes without much notice.
- **Your IT or tech partner may buy AI through a cloud account they already have.** If your firm or its software vendors run on Google Cloud, adding a frontier model can be a billing change, not a new vendor review. That is convenient and also how AI features reach project data before anyone has signed off on them.
- **"Where does my file go?" has more possible answers.** The same model can sit behind different hosting, logging, and retention terms depending on the cloud.

## Which questions should you ask your software vendors?

Use this list before the next renewal:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Which model powers each AI feature, and on which cloud? | Model and host can change without a new contract. |
| Are our drawings, specs, and bid files retained or logged? | Bid pricing and owner documents are the sensitive part. |
| Is the "no training on customer data" promise in the contract? | A marketing page is not a contract term. |
| Will you notify us before changing the underlying model? | Output quality on spec review and takeoff can shift. |
| Does this meet the data terms in our owner and federal contracts? | Some owners restrict where project documents can go. |

## What is still unknown?

Pricing is not public, general availability is only "coming weeks," and the data-perimeter claim comes from Google. A model being available on a cloud does not mean any construction software has adopted it. Treat this as a purchasing and contract-review prompt, not a reason to switch tools.

## The takeaway

If you buy or manage construction software, put "which model, which cloud, which data terms" on your renewal checklist this quarter. The model brand on the box is the least stable part of the stack. The contract language about your documents is the part that lasts.
