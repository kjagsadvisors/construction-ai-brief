FieldAI, a startup that builds the software brain for robots rather than the robots themselves, is reportedly raising $700 million at a $10 billion valuation, and construction is one of the markets it sells into. If the round closes, expect more jobsite robots running overnight scans on large GC projects, which makes data ownership and flag accountability the contract items to settle first.

## What was actually reported?

Per [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/02/robotics-ai-developer-fieldai-reportedly-raising-700m-in-funding/) and the [Orange County Business Journal](https://www.ocbj.com/defense-2/robotics-company-fieldai-set-to-raise-700m-at-10b-valuation-bi/), FieldAI is raising $700 million at a $10 billion valuation, about five times its value last August. Reports say it has signed a term sheet, which is a nonbinding preliminary agreement. Treat this as reported, not closed.

FieldAI builds no robots itself. It sells software that runs humanoids, robot dogs, drones and industrial rovers for construction, data center and defense customers. Its models are described as working without a pre-built map, GPS or an internet connection.

## Who is already using it on construction work?

FieldAI and Boston Dynamics [describe](https://bostondynamics.com/case-studies/field-ai-boston-dynamics-made-autonomy-practical-on-construction-sites/) pairing FieldAI's software with the Spot quadruped for jobsite inspection, mapping and monitoring. DPR Construction and Big-D Construction are named as users in FieldAI's materials. [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/engineering-and-construction/caterpillars-fieldai-work-moves-autonomy-from-demos-to-live-construction-sites) reports Caterpillar announced a partnership with FieldAI on Sept. 5, 2026 for physical AI on jobsites.

The vendor-reported tasks:

- Tracking daily progress with 3D scans and images
- Spotting hazards such as standing water or blocked egress routes
- Running overnight so fresh results are ready in the morning

Boston Dynamics' case study claims inspection and documentation time fell by more than 90% versus manual processes. That figure comes from the vendors, on their own deployments, so don't put it in a budget.

## What does this mean for a GC's project team?

More money behind one robot-software vendor means more robots showing up on projects, whether the GC bought them or a sub or owner brought one in. Three things change for a PM or superintendent:

1. **The scan becomes a project record.** Overnight captures show progress, rework and conditions. If a dispute comes up, someone will ask for them. Decide who holds them before the first scan.
2. **A flag is not a finding.** A robot that marks blocked egress creates a record that someone was told. Assign who reads the morning report and who closes each item.
3. **Subs' work is on camera.** Trades will want to know whether their installed work is being scored against the schedule by a machine.

## Should a mid-size GC act now?

Probably not by buying anything. A reasonable move is a written robot-on-site policy before an owner or large GC partner proposes one. Cover data ownership, whether the vendor may train models on your site data, who is accountable for missed hazards and false alarms, and what the robot is not allowed to do near people and active equipment.

## What is still unproven?

A valuation is not a track record. The public construction evidence is mostly vendor case studies from a few large contractors, and the materials describe a robot assisting inspection, not replacing the person who decides what to do about a hazard. Also, the round is reported, not closed. Our earlier look at [Gemini Robotics 2 and jobsite robots](/posts/2026-07-31-gemini-robotics-2-construction-jobsite-robots) covers the same gap between demos and field conditions.

**Takeaway:** Before any robot scans your next project, get the data-ownership and flag-responsibility terms in writing, and name the person who owns the morning report.
