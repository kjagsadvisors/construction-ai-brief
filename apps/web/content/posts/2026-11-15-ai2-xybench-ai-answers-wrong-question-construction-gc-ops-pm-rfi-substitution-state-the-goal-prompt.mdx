If you ask an AI assistant how to do the wrong thing, it will usually tell you how. A benchmark released November 13 by the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2) found that five AI models pointed out a user's mistaken approach in 47% to 60% of answers to real forum questions, while the top-rated human answer did so 79% of the time. For a PM or project engineer, the fix costs one extra sentence: tell the assistant what you are trying to get done, not only the task you picked to get there.

## What did Ai2 test?

[XYBench](https://allenai.org/blog/xybench) is built around what programmers call the XY problem. A person wants X, assumes Y will get them there, and asks only about Y. Ai2's example: someone asks how to thicken frosting that keeps sliding off cupcakes, when the real problem is that the cupcakes are still warm.

The benchmark holds 8,115 such questions: 1,557 from Stack Overflow, 184 from Stack Exchange, 102 hand-picked, and 6,272 generated from WikiHow guides. Ai2 built it with researchers at Carnegie Mellon, NVIDIA and Johns Hopkins ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06842v1)). Each model got one question and one answer, with no follow-up.

Results on the Stack Overflow questions, per Ai2:

| Answer from | Includes a fix for the real goal | Mainly focused on the real goal | Steers user off the mistaken approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top human answer | 67% | 55% | 79% |
| GPT-5 | 71% | 41% | 60% |
| Qwen3.5-9B | 64% | 41% | 60% |
| DeepSeek-V4 Pro | 66% | 35% | 55% |
| Gemini 3.1 Pro | 62% | 40% | 54% |
| Claude Sonnet 4.6 | 59% | 36% | 47% |

The models answered the question as asked in 75% to 86% of responses and spent most of the answer there. The better route, when it showed up, tended to be a side note.

Yet when the same models were handed two finished answers and asked to pick the better one, they chose the goal-focused answer 71% to 84% of the time. They can recognize the better advice. They do not reliably write it unprompted.

## Why does this matter on a project team?

Ai2 did not test construction questions. What follows is our illustration of the same pattern, not study data.

Project paperwork is full of XY requests, because the person typing has already decided what document they need:

- **"Draft an RFI asking the architect to accept this alternate product."** The goal is getting the product approved. On most jobs that runs through the substitution procedure in Division 01, with its own form and deadlines, and an RFI is the wrong vehicle.
- **"Write a change order request for the extra rock we hit."** The goal is getting paid. The first question is what the contract's notice clause requires and whether that clock has already started.

In each case an assistant that behaves like the ones in this benchmark produces a clean, confident draft of the thing you asked for. A senior PM reading over your shoulder would have stopped you first, and a newer project engineer is the person least likely to know the question was off.

## How should a PM or project engineer prompt around it?

1. **State the goal before the task.** "I need this alternate valve approved before the pour on the 20th" and then "here is what I planned to send."
2. **Ask whether the approach is right.** The paper found that asking the model to correct misconceptions improved results, though not to human level.
3. **Ask for two routes and a pick.** The multiple-choice result suggests models judge options better than they volunteer them. "Give me two ways to handle this and say which you would use" plays to that.
4. **Keep the human review.** A draft RFI, notice or change request still goes past someone who knows the contract.

## How far should you trust these numbers?

- The questions are programming and household how-to, in English, and single-turn. Real use is often a back-and-forth.
- The answers were graded by another AI model. Two human reviewers checked 500 of them and agreed with the grader 74% to 80% of the time on the "focus" measure, and more often on the others.
- None of the five models is its maker's newest. We covered a different test of current models on drawing-heavy work [earlier this week](/posts/2026-11-11-deepmind-mka-benchmark-ai-agents-drawings-57-percent-construction-estimator-precon-drawing-review-vision-test).

**Takeaway:** Treat an AI assistant like a fast, literal junior who will draft whatever you name. Add one line with the outcome you need and one line asking if there is a better way to get it, and tell your project engineers to do the same before the next RFI goes out.
