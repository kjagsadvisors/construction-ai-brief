Meta launched an enterprise AI platform on September 28 that includes a Business Agent built to answer customer messages, qualify leads, and book appointments. For a mechanical, electrical, or plumbing service sub that already takes calls over WhatsApp or Messenger, that agent is the piece worth a look, and the piece that needs limits before it talks to a customer.

## What did Meta actually announce?

Meta said it is starting an enterprise platform that puts its Muse models, agents, and developer tools under one business-facing effort. Zuckerberg called it a "next major pillar" of the company, and Chirantan "CJ" Desai, formerly CEO of MongoDB, will run it and report to him. The reported initial pieces:

- **Muse:** Meta's personal AI assistant.
- **Meta Business Agent:** answers customer questions, recommends products, books appointments, and qualifies leads on Meta's messaging platforms.
- **Muse API:** lets developers build Meta's models into their own software.
- **Muse Code:** a terminal and continuous-integration coding agent that plans tasks, edits files, and runs commands.

Coverage doesn't include enterprise pricing, customer commitments, or a delivery timeline for the platform as a whole. Treat it as a direction, not a product you can buy this week.

## Which contractors does this touch?

Not most of them. A sub that gets work from bid invitations, GC portals, and phone calls with a few repeat GCs won't see much here. It matters for **service and small-project work**: a mechanical service company fielding "my rooftop unit is down" texts, an electrical contractor taking tenant-improvement requests, a restoration outfit answering after-hours messages.

| Task | Fit for a messaging agent | Keep a person on it |
|---|---|---|
| Collect address, equipment type, symptom, photos | Good | Review the summary |
| Book a non-emergency visit into open slots | Reasonable | Dispatch confirms |
| Quote a price or say "that's covered" | Poor | Always |
| Promise an arrival time on an emergency call | Poor | Always |
| Qualify a new-build inquiry by size and trade | Reasonable | Estimator follows up |

## What could go wrong?

A customer-facing agent talks in your company's name. If it tells a facility manager "we'll have someone there within two hours" or "that's under warranty," you own that statement. Warranty language and response times are the two answers that most often turn into disputes. Set the agent up for intake only, and put anything involving price, time, or coverage behind a human.

Muse Code and the API are a separate question. The same tools that let a developer automate work could let a 30-person sub build small internal tools, such as a script that turns a service ticket into a work order. Nothing in the launch coverage shows anyone doing that on a jobsite, so don't plan around it yet.

## What should a service sub do this week?

1. Count how many inbound customer requests arrive on WhatsApp or Messenger, and how long each waits for a reply.
2. Write down the five answers your dispatcher gives that a machine must never give (price, ETA, warranty, liability, safety advice).
3. If you pilot any messaging agent, run it in a draft mode where a person approves each outbound message for two weeks and log every edit.

This is also the third major vendor pushing agents into ordinary business channels, following the untagged-agent problem we covered in [this piece on chat agents and RFI authority](/posts/2026-09-28-ando-agent-native-chat-construction-rfi-authority). The rule is the same: decide what the agent may say before it says it.
