ChatGPT can now answer with a working calculator, editable chart or form instead of a paragraph, and that is useful for estimators and PMs who need a quick one-off tool. It is also a new way for a wrong formula to look trustworthy, so test any generated tool before a number from it goes into a bid.

## What did OpenAI launch?

On October 7, 2026, OpenAI introduced "Intelligent UI" with GPT-6 in ChatGPT. According to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/chatgpt-is-getting-a-lot-more-visual-with-the-launch-of-a-new-interface/), responses can include tappable buttons, a customized calculator for a specific task, interactive charts and editable graphs. [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/openai-brings-gpt-6-and-intelligent-ui-to-chatgpt-4f0d797c) and [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/chatgpt-gpt-6-intelligent-ui-3720464) report the same feature set.

Some specifics from the coverage:

- The model chooses the format. It can still answer in plain text when an interface adds little.
- Users can turn the amount of visual material down.
- It rolls out to Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise users first, with Free and Go tiers following. Enterprise availability can depend on an administrator's settings.
- OpenAI's own examples are consumer ones: a trip map, a savings calculator, a bill splitter.

Nothing in the launch coverage is aimed at construction. The angle is ours.

## What does this mean for estimators?

Estimators and PMs constantly need small tools that no software vendor will build: a quick markup-and-contingency comparison, a bid-day alternates spread, a look at how a labor-rate change moves a unit price. Today that is a spreadsheet someone builds at 9 p.m.

Intelligent UI points toward describing the tool in plain English and getting a working interface back, with sliders and editable fields. Useful candidates:

| Task | Why a generated calculator fits | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Alternate and add/deduct spread | Few inputs, simple arithmetic | Sign conventions, rounding |
| Labor-rate sensitivity | Lets you change one input and see the total | Burden and overtime treatment |
| Bid-leveling comparison | Side-by-side with editable scope adjustments | That every line is carried over |
| Material waste and unit conversion | Pure math | Units (LF vs. SF vs. CY) |

## What are the risks?

The tool looks finished whether or not it is correct. A calculator with a clean interface and a wrong formula is harder to catch than a wrong sentence, because people trust a number in a box.

Three specific problems:

1. **Silent formula errors.** The model writes the logic. A misapplied waste factor or a double-counted markup will not announce itself.
2. **No record.** A calculator inside a chat is not your estimating system. If a number from it ends up in a bid, you need to be able to show where it came from.
3. **Data exposure.** Pasting a full bid, subcontractor pricing or an owner's budget into a chat is a policy question. On Business and Enterprise plans an administrator controls the settings, so ask yours before staff start.

OpenAI has not published accuracy figures for the generated tools, so assume none.

## How should a mid-size sub or GC test one?

1. **Pick a closed job** with a known final number.
2. **Build the same calculator** and feed it that job's inputs.
3. **Compare to the answer.** If it is off by any amount you cannot explain, do not use it.
4. **Inspect the formulas.** Ask the model to state each calculation in words and check every one.
5. **Keep the real estimate in your estimating system.** Use the generated tool for scratch work and sanity checks only.

## The takeaway

Treat Intelligent UI as a fast way to draft a throwaway tool, not as a source of numbers. Before anything from it touches a bid, run it against a job you already closed, and write down who is allowed to use it and what data may go in.
