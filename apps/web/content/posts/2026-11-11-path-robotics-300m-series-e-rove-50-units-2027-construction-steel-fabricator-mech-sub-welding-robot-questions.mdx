Path Robotics, the Ohio company that builds AI-guided welding robots, raised $300 million on November 10 at a $2.45 billion valuation. For a steel fabricator or a mechanical sub with a pipe shop, that makes Path a better-funded vendor to talk to, but it does not put a welding robot on your floor soon: the mobile unit is capped at 50 shipments in 2027, and the specs a shop would need are not public.

## What did Path Robotics announce?

Per [Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/path-robotics-announces-300m-series-e-financing-at-2.45b-valuation) and [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/path-robotics-300m-series-e-2-45bn-valuation):

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Round | $300M Series E, led by Georgian |
| Valuation | $2.45B post-money |
| Total raised | More than $610M since 2018 |
| Growth claimed | Annual recurring revenue up 17-fold in 18 months (no dollar figure given) |
| Headcount | Up 97% in a year; target of 400 by end of 2026 |
| Use of funds | More deployments, new robot types, model training, tasks before and after welding |

Path's model, Obsidian, is trained on real welding data. The company says it lets a robot find the joint, plan the weld and adjust as conditions change, instead of running a fixed program. Rove puts that model on a four-legged robot that walks to the part.

## Why does this matter to a construction fabricator?

We covered [Path's Navy shipbuilding deal in August](/posts/2026-08-10-hii-path-robotics-graymatter-welding-shipbuilding-welder-shortage). Two things are new.

First, the customer list is no longer only defense primes. Alongside HII, Lockheed Martin and Saronic, Path now names mid-sized manufacturers: EK Machine, Millerbernd Manufacturing Company, TransAmerican Power Products and TYCROP. It lists energy infrastructure as a target industry. That is closer to the scale of a regional steel fabricator than an aircraft carrier yard is.

Second, The Next Web reports Rove is aimed at shipyards, construction sites and heavy industrial sites, with units working alongside human welders starting next year. Path's own Rove page does not mention construction, so read that as a direction, not a product commitment.

## What is still missing?

- **Supply.** [Path's Rove page](https://www.path-robotics.com/rove) says "only 50 units shipping in 2027," with early adopters picked from a waitlist. The Next Web reports 50 preorders already. Most shops will not get one.
- **Weld specs.** Path's public materials do not list weld processes, positions, materials or joint types. Nothing we found shows Rove making a field weld on a building.
- **Price and terms.** None are published. The only deal structure on record is HII's performance-based agreement.
- **Proof on your kind of work.** The named Rove early adopter is a shipbuilder. No structural steel erector, pipe fabricator or mechanical contractor is named.

## What should a steel or mechanical sub ask the vendor?

1. **Which of our welds can it make?** Bring real joints: process, position, material thickness, fit-up tolerance. Ask for the list in writing.
2. **How does it fit our welding procedures?** Structural and pressure-piping work runs on qualified procedures and inspection under codes such as AWS D1.1 and ASME Section IX. Ask who qualifies the procedure for a robot-made weld and what your inspector will need to see.
3. **What do we pay for, and when?** Purchase, lease or per-weld pricing, and what happens if the cell sits idle.
4. **Who owns the weld data?** Rove captures welding data. Your joint data and shop drawings should stay yours by contract.
5. **Can we visit a shop like ours?** A mid-sized fabricator reference is worth more than a shipyard video.

CEO Andy Lonsberry told Bloomberg that "none of our customers have ever fired a welder because of a task robot." Treat that as a vendor claim until someone outside the company measures it.

**Takeaway:** If welder hours are what limit your shop's throughput, this round is a reason to put a vendor call on your 2027 planning list, not a reason to budget for a mobile field welder. Start with fixed cells on repetitive shop work, and take the five questions above to any vendor, Path included.
