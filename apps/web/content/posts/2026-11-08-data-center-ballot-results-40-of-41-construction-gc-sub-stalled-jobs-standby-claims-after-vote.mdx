**Opponents won virtually every local data center ballot measure on Nov. 3, so any unstarted data center job in a jurisdiction that voted should be treated as at risk until the owner says otherwise in writing.** Whether you get paid for the wait depends on your contract, not on the election.

## What did voters actually decide?

[Route Fifty's preliminary count](https://www.route-fifty.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/voters-reject-data-centers-local-ballot-initiatives/417033/) shows opponents winning in virtually all of roughly 40 measures across 10 states. Harwood, North Dakota, was the exception. [Fortune reports](https://fortune.com/2026/11/04/ohio-data-center-ballot-measures-midterm-elections/) that Ohio held 21 of the 41 measures nationwide, across 18 municipalities, and that at least 14 passed bans or other restrictions. Results are unofficial until counties certify them.

We previewed these votes [before Election Day](/posts/2026-10-31-data-center-ballot-measures-nov-3-construction-sub-gc-zoning-vote-contract-clauses). The results are in, and the wording of each measure now matters more than the headline.

## Which kind of restriction hit your job?

| Measure type | Example | What it means on a project |
|---|---|---|
| Ban on "high impact" campuses over 25 MW | 13 Ohio municipalities, including Granville, Grove City and Urbana | Large campuses are blocked; smaller ones may survive |
| Outright ban | Emporia, Kansas (just over 60%) | Project is effectively dead unless challenged |
| Rezoning rejected | Wilmington, Ohio (about 80% against, four sites) | Planned site changes don't happen; owner needs a new site |
| Financial guarantees for tax breaks | Perry, Ohio (over 87%) | Project may proceed, with owner cost and schedule risk |
| Advisory only | Some measures | Not binding on officials |

## Are projects already under construction safe?

Not reliably. Per Fortune, a ban in Trenton, Ohio, passed roughly 80% to 20% but likely won't reach a four-building AWS campus already under construction nearby. In Ashville, voters backed fully blocking a campus that is already under construction. Route Fifty notes the Harwood amendment would have applied only to future projects. Same election, opposite outcomes: vesting dates and measure language decide it.

## What should a GC or trade sub do this week?

1. **Sort your data center backlog into three bins:** under construction, awarded but unmobilized, and pre-award. Mark which sit in a jurisdiction that voted.
2. **Read the measure text,** not the news summary. Check the megawatt threshold, effective date and any grandfathering.
3. **Send written notice** under your delay, suspension or government-action clause. A phone call from the owner's rep is not notice.
4. **Price your exposure:** standby, remobilization, and long-lead gear such as switchgear and chillers ordered against the job.
5. **Ask for a termination-for-convenience schedule** if the owner starts talking about cancelling or relocating.

## What is still unknown?

Counts differ by source and some results are pending. Litigation could unwind a ban, and owners may relocate to a neighboring jurisdiction rather than cancel, which could keep work alive for a prepared sub. Route Fifty also notes Ohio may see a statewide constitutional amendment on a new-construction ban, possibly on the 2027 ballot, and more votes are expected next month in Arkansas and North Dakota. That makes a pipeline review worth repeating.

## What's the takeaway?

Before you buy anything else against an unstarted data center job, find out what your jurisdiction decided and get the owner's position in writing. Standby you didn't document is standby you won't be paid for.
