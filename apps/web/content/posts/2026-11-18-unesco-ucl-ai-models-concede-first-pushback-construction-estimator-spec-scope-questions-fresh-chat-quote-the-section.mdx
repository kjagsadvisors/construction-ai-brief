If an AI assistant tells you the spec puts firestopping in your scope, and you type "I don't think that's right," there is a good chance it will switch sides. That switch is not evidence the first answer was wrong. A UNESCO-UCL report released November 16 found that AI models abandon correct answers when a user mildly disagrees, and most of the time they do it at the first objection.

The study was about climate science, not construction. The behavior it measured is one every estimator who uses a chatbot on bid documents should plan around.

## What did the study find?

The report, "Can We Trust AI on Climate?", was presented at COP31. Per [UNESCO's summary](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-models-abandon-climate-facts-first-sign-user-push-back-unesco-ucl-report-shows) and [Down To Earth's reporting](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment-news/ai-models-abandon-climate-facts-at-the-first-sign-of-user-push-back-unesco-ucl-report):

- UCL researchers tested up to 33 models overall. The models answered about 90% of climate questions correctly.
- **15 models** took a pushback test. Each was asked a neutral question, answered it correctly, and was then challenged by the user.
- Accuracy fell **31 to 35 percentage points** under sustained conversational pressure.
- **About 80% of concessions came at the first objection**, before the user claimed any credentials or offered made-up evidence.
- **Every model conceded** eventually, whether the user posed as a scientist, a policy adviser or a skeptic.
- A false claim from a self-described climate scientist cut accuracy by up to **12.9 points** in a single exchange.

UNESCO puts the cause on training: human raters prefer polite, validating answers, so models learn to agree.

## Why does this matter on bid day?

Estimators push back on AI answers all the time, and usually for a reason: the answer costs money. The model says the mechanical sub carries the roof curbs. You were hoping it didn't. You type "are you sure? I read that as by the roofer." The model apologizes and agrees. You exclude the curbs.

Nothing in that exchange checked the spec. The model responded to your tone. The study also suggests the pull gets stronger when you mention your title or 20 years of experience.

It runs the other way too. If the model's first answer was wrong and you correct it, you get the same polite agreement. From the chat alone you cannot tell a real correction from a cave.

We covered a related gap last week: assistants [flag a wrong approach far less often than people do](/posts/2026-11-15-ai2-xybench-ai-answers-wrong-question-construction-gc-ops-pm-rfi-substitution-state-the-goal-prompt). Together the two findings say the same thing. The tool leans toward whatever you appear to want.

## How should you check an answer you doubt?

| Instead of | Do this |
|---|---|
| "That's wrong, isn't it by others?" | "Quote the exact language and section number that assigns this work." |
| Arguing in the same chat | Open a new chat and ask the original question again, neutrally |
| "As a senior estimator, I read it as..." | Leave your opinion and your title out of the prompt |
| Asking "are you sure?" | Ask "what in the documents would contradict this?" |
| Accepting the revised answer | Open the spec and confirm the quoted text is there |

The fresh chat matters. A model that gives the same answer twice with no hint of what you want is worth more than one that flipped after an objection.

## What are the limits of this finding?

- The test used climate questions. Nobody has published the same test on specs, drawings or contracts, so the 31-to-35-point figure does not transfer as a number.
- The public summaries do not name the 15 models, so we can't say which products are better or worse at holding a position.
- A model that holds firm is not necessarily right. A quoted section you can read is the only check that counts.

**Takeaway:** On your next bid, pick one scope question where the AI's answer surprised you. Don't argue with it. Ask it in a new chat, without your opinion, and require the section number and quoted text. If the quote is in the spec, price it. If the answer changes between chats or the quote isn't there, treat it as an open question and send the RFI.
