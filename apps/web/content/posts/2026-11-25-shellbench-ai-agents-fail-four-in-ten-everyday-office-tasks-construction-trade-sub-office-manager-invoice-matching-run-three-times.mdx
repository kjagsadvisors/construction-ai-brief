The top AI models in a new benchmark finished only about six in ten everyday office tasks, and they often couldn't repeat a success. For a trade sub thinking about letting an agent match supplier invoices, chase lien waivers or sort the shared inbox, that number is the planning assumption: plan for the agent to miss, and build the check in before it goes live.

## What did ShellBench test?

The OpenClaw Foundation [released ShellBench](https://openclaw.ai/blog/shellbench) on November 24, built with Parsewave, Hugging Face, Red Hat and UC Berkeley. Most AI benchmarks test clean puzzles. This one gives the agent a short request and a cluttered workspace like a real office: emails, calendar exports, spreadsheets, receipts and PDFs, with outdated records, conflicting data and planted malicious instructions mixed in.

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Tasks | 100 tasks, 53,100 graded runs |
| Models and harnesses | 12 models across 5 harnesses, including Claude Code and Codex CLI |
| Top scores | GPT-6 Astra 62.5%, Claude Fable 5.2 61.9%, GPT-6.1 Sol 61.4% |
| Lower end | DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 39.7% |
| Safety | 15.7% of runs failed at least one safety rule; 9.9% of fully correct runs still failed one |
| Repeatability | GPT-6 Astra passed 75.1% of tasks at least once in three tries, 48.8% on all three |

## Why does this matter for a sub's office?

The tasks look a lot like what an office manager or AP clerk does every day. Three findings stand out.

1. **Payments are where it goes wrong.** [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/even-the-best-ai-agents-struggle-with-everyday-life/) that in one task, agents recommended duplicate or incorrect invoice payments 40.3% of the time. That is the exact job many subs want to hand off: matching supplier invoices to POs and delivery tickets.
2. **Agents drop information they already found.** [The Deep View reported](https://www.thedeepview.com/articles/exclusive-openclaw-offers-an-agent-reality-check) that leaving information out caused 51% of completion failures, and in 87% of those cases the agent had already collected it. The OpenClaw writeup gives an example: 522 of 531 attempts picked the right answer, but 331 failed because they didn't say which files they used. A summary that doesn't point to the invoice, PO or ticket it came from can't be checked quickly.
3. **One good demo proves little.** A task that worked once failed on a repeat a large share of the time. The authors note their single-pass grading may account for some of that, but a vendor demo or a first test run is still a sample of one.

## Should a sub stop using agents for back-office work?

No. A 60% agent that drafts and flags can still save a clerk real time, if a person reviews the output. The mistake is treating it like a clerk who gets it right every time. Price also isn't a shortcut: The Deep View put GPT-6 Astra at $1.01 per run and GPT-6.1 Sol at $0.17 for nearly the same score.

## How should an office test an agent before trusting it?

- **Pick a task with a checkable answer.** "Which of these 40 invoices don't match a PO line?" is checkable. "Clean up the AP folder" isn't.
- **Run the same batch three times.** If the answers differ, the agent isn't ready to run unattended on that task.
- **Require the agent to name its sources.** Every flag should cite the invoice number, PO and ticket. No citation, no action.
- **Keep a human on the money.** The agent can queue a payment or a duplicate flag. A person approves it. We covered why that matters when [big banks said losses from an AI agent using your login are yours](/posts/2026-11-20-banks-ai-agent-liability-citi-pnc-citizens-wells-fargo-construction-trade-sub-office-manager-supplier-payments-bank-login).
- **Seed a trap.** Drop a fake "updated bank details" email into the test inbox. ShellBench planted malicious instructions for a reason.

## What are the limits of this benchmark?

ShellBench tasks are general office work, not construction paperwork, so nobody has measured these models on submittal logs or certified payroll. The invoice figure comes from a single task. Results also depend on the harness the model runs in, and models change monthly. Treat the numbers as a direction, not a forecast for your office.

**Takeaway:** Before an agent touches payables, take last month's invoices, run them through it three times, and compare against what your AP clerk actually paid. If the three runs don't agree with each other and with the clerk, keep the agent drafting and the person approving.
