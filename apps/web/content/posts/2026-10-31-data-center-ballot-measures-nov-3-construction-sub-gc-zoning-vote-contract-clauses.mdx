**If you hold an unstarted data center contract in a town with a measure on the Nov. 3 ballot, check your standby and termination terms before polls close.** A yes vote can pause or cancel a project that hasn't broken ground, and many subcontracts don't say who pays for the wait.

## What is actually on the ballot?

Ballotpedia counts dozens of local data center measures this year. [Reporting based on its tally](https://letsdatascience.com/news/voters-weigh-39-data-center-measures-across-eight-states-e8675da5) puts the number in the high 30s across eight states, and says most are on the Nov. 3 ballot. Counts differ by source and date, so treat the exact figure as approximate. [Ballotpedia says Ohio has the most](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/09/21/ohio-leads-states-in-number-of-local-ballot-measures-on-data-centers-for-2026/). Other states with measures include California, Illinois, Florida, Kansas, Nevada and Wisconsin. [Reuters-reported coverage](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/news/politics/2026/10/29/ai-data-center-boom-faces-backlash-us-ballot-box/92494100007/) from Oct. 29 describes the same pattern: local voters deciding limits on the buildout.

The wording differs. Some measures ban data centers outright. Others cap them by electrical load, such as 25 megawatts.

## What does Sunbury, Ohio show?

Sunbury, near Columbus, is the clearest example. [A city charter amendment](https://cwcolumbus.com/news/local/sunbury-petition-seeks-november-ballot-measure-limiting-data-centers-to-25-megawatts) would limit data centers to 25 megawatts. It targets a planned $2 billion Amazon campus. [Local reporting](https://abc6onyourside.com/newsletter-daily/sunbury-petition-seeks-november-ballot-measure-limiting-data-centers-to-25-megawatts) says the city council also passed a moratorium on new data centers through Jan. 31, 2027. Reports differ on how the cap is measured, peak load versus monthly usage, which matters for any contract tied to a specific MW figure.

The Aug. 17 piece we ran on [moratoriums in Fort Worth and Louisville](/posts/2026-08-17-data-center-moratorium-fort-worth-louisville-backlash-trust) covered the same trend at council level. This is the same fight moving to voters, which is harder to negotiate around.

## What should a GC or sub check this week?

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Are zoning and permits final, or still pending? | Vested permits may protect the job. Pending ones may not. |
| Is the project in a town with a measure on the ballot? | Look up your county's sample ballot. |
| Who pays if a government action stops work? | Many subcontracts are silent. Standby, remobilization and escalation cost real money. |
| Is there a termination-for-convenience schedule? | It sets what you're paid for engineered gear, deposits and design work. |
| Have long-lead orders been placed? | Switchgear and chillers ordered against a cancelled job are your exposure. |

## What are the limits of this read?

A ballot measure passing doesn't automatically kill every project. Grandfathering, litigation and a revised site plan are all possible, and the owner may relocate the job rather than cancel it. We don't know how many measures will pass. Earlier 2026 votes leaned toward limits, per the reporting above, but that doesn't predict this round.

## What's the takeaway?

Before Tuesday, pull every data center contract that hasn't mobilized. Mark the ones in jurisdictions with a measure, find the clause that covers government-action delays, and put long-lead purchase orders on hold until you know the result. On Wednesday, you'll want that answer in writing, not on a phone call.
