Google gave the Gemini desktop app for macOS the ability to operate the computer on November 19: it can work across local files, native Mac apps and websites to finish multistep tasks, and you approve its actions before they happen, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/google-gives-gemini-the-ability-to-use-your-mac-for-you/). For a trade sub, the clearest use is the office manager's least favorite job: typing the same company, safety and insurance data into a different prequalification portal for every GC you bid to. Test it there. Keep a person on the review and the signature.

## What did Google release?

Per TechCrunch's report of Google's announcement:

- Gemini can now complete multistep tasks that span **local files, Mac apps and the web**, working in a background window.
- You watch it in a **live preview** inside the chat, and your own screen and cursor stay free.
- You can **pause, stop and approve** its actions before they happen.
- Google's suggested uses: **filling out forms, repeating one task across a batch of files, sorting files into folders by criteria**, and building slideshows from files or dashboard data.
- Launch limits: **English, U.S. only, Mac only**, and it needs a Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra subscription. Google hasn't given a timeline for other markets or platforms.

This isn't a first. TechCrunch notes that OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta already offer agent features in their desktop apps. What's new is that Google has put it in a consumer subscription that many small offices may already be paying for.

## Why does this matter for a trade sub's office?

Most of a sub's back office isn't hard thinking. It's re-entry. The same EMR, OSHA logs, bonding capacity, insurance limits, license numbers and references go into one GC portal after another, each with its own field layout. A browser-only chatbot can't help with that because the data sits in PDFs and spreadsheets on your machine. An agent that can open the local file, read it and type into the web form is the missing piece.

| Office task | Good first test? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Re-entering company and safety data into GC prequal forms | Yes | Repetitive, same source docs every time, easy to check |
| Renaming and sorting a batch of downloaded drawings or submittal PDFs | Yes | Google lists batch file tasks and sorting by criteria |
| Pulling numbers from a stack of supplier quotes into a sheet | Maybe | Useful, but every number needs checking against the quote |
| Submitting a pay app or signing a lien waiver | No | Legal and money commitments stay with a person |
| Anything in Windows-only software | Not possible | It runs on Mac only for now |

## What can go wrong?

- **It types what it reads, right or wrong.** If last year's insurance certificate is in the folder, it may enter last year's limits. Point it at one clean, current folder of source documents.
- **A prequal form is a certified statement.** Your EMR, claims history and financials are representations to the GC. An agent filling the fields doesn't change who is responsible for them.
- **Approve means read.** Clicking "approve" on every step without looking defeats the safeguard. Review the filled form before you hit submit, not after.
- **Account access.** If the agent works inside portals you're logged into, it acts as you. Don't run it on a machine where it can reach banking or payroll while you test.
- **Windows offices are left out for now.** If your office runs on Windows, this specific tool isn't available to you yet. The same test works with the desktop agents from other vendors that TechCrunch mentions.

We covered a related pattern when [Microsoft gave its Copilot agent its own identity](/posts/2026-11-18-microsoft-copilot-autopilot-always-on-agent-own-identity-construction-gc-ops-pm-sub-follow-ups-approval-checks-notice): the more an agent can do on your behalf, the more your approval step matters.

## Should a sub try it now?

If your office already has a Mac and a Google AI subscription, the test costs about an hour. If not, don't buy hardware for it. Agent features are arriving in tools you may already pay for, and Google hasn't published accuracy figures for this feature.

**Takeaway:** Build a single "prequal kit" folder with your current EMR letter, OSHA 300A, insurance certificate, bonding letter, license list and references. Next time a GC sends a prequal portal link, have the agent fill it from that folder while you approve each step, then check every field against the kit before you submit. Time it against doing it by hand. If it saves real time and the error count is zero, use it for the next five. If you catch a wrong number, you've learned where it needs a tighter folder or a closer review.
