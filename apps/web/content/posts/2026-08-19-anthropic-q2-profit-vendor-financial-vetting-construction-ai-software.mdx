Anthropic told investors it generated more than $11.5 billion in preliminary revenue in the second quarter of 2026 and posted its first-ever positive operating income — about $559 million. That milestone is getting scrutinized hard, because the discount that made it possible is scheduled to expire. For any contractor whose project-management, submittal, or estimating software runs on someone else's AI model, that's a reminder to check a vendor's finances the way you'd check a subcontractor's before signing.

## What Anthropic actually reported

Bloomberg first reported the numbers on August 14, and CNBC, Forbes, and others confirmed them: Q2 2026 revenue topped $11.5 billion, up from $787 million in the same quarter of 2025 and $4.73 billion in the first quarter of 2026 — a roughly 14-fold year-over-year jump. The adjusted operating income of $559 million, a margin of about 5%, excludes stock-based compensation and is preliminary, meaning it could still be revised. Anthropic is preparing for a potential fall IPO with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan Chase.

## Why the profit number is disputed

Anthropic pays SpaceX $15 billion a year — about $1.25 billion a month — for compute capacity at SpaceX's Colossus data centers, a deal that runs through May 2029 and could total $45 billion. That rate is discounted during an early ramp-up period. Tech writer Ed Zitron argued in a widely cited critique that Q2 2026 landed inside that discount window, and that once the full contracted rate hits Anthropic's books, the current cost structure won't produce an operating profit. Anthropic hasn't disputed the substance of that mechanic — the company has told investors it doesn't expect profitability to hold in the quarters after Q2, pointing to its own planned infrastructure spending as the reason.

The contrast with OpenAI sharpens the picture. OpenAI reported Q2 2026 revenue of about $6.7 billion, up 18% from the prior quarter — slower growth than Anthropic's — while its losses widened rather than narrowed, according to SiliconANGLE's reporting on the two companies' diverging trajectories.

## What this has to do with a submittal log or a bid

Nothing directly. No construction software vendor appeared in either company's numbers. But a growing list of tools contractors already use — RFI triage assistants, submittal-matching bots, daily-log summarizers, scheduling copilots — are built on top of either Anthropic's or OpenAI's models through an API. When the underlying model provider's costs shift, one of two things happens to the vendor sitting between that provider and your firm: they eat the increase and their runway shortens, or they pass it to you. [Construction AI Brief covered a live version of this in mid-August](/posts/2026-08-17-deepseek-api-price-hike-cache-tokens-construction-buy-vs-build), when DeepSeek raised its API prices as much as 6x in a single week. Anthropic's disputed profit is the same exposure at a much larger scale, sitting underneath tools contractors are signing multi-year contracts for right now.

## The prequalification questions a vendor should be able to answer

GCs already run financial prequalification on subcontractors before awarding a contract — bonding capacity, backlog, financial statements — precisely because a sub's balance sheet determines whether they can finish the job. The same logic applies to an AI vendor whose product depends on a foundation model's API staying priced and available the way it is today.

| Ask the vendor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Which foundation model powers this feature? | Tells you whose cost structure and pricing risk you've inherited |
| Is pricing locked or capped in the contract? | Determines whether a model provider's price hike becomes your invoice |
| Can they fail over to a different model? | Signals whether an outage or a killed API is a config change or a rewrite |
| What's their own funding runway? | A vendor burning cash on a subsidized API is a different risk than one on a sustainable margin |

## The takeaway

Don't read Anthropic's Q2 number as a verdict on whether AI companies can be trusted — read it as confirmation that even the frontier labs' own economics are still unsettled, month to month. Before renewing or signing an AI-enabled software contract this year, ask the vendor the same four questions above. If they can't answer which model they run on or what happens to your pricing if that model's costs change, you're carrying a risk you haven't priced — the same way you wouldn't award work to a sub without checking whether they can actually finish it.
