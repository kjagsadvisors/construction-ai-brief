OpenAI is paying local consultants up to $8,000 a month to work with small businesses at no charge. Independently owned trade subs appear to qualify. Bring one specific office problem to it, and keep in mind that OpenAI is paying the person helping you.

## What is OpenWorks?

OpenWorks is a pilot OpenAI [describes on its site](https://openai.com/openworks/) as a way to get hands-on AI help into small businesses. The basic terms:

| Item | What OpenAI says |
|---|---|
| Partner pay | A monthly stipend of up to $8,000 |
| What partners do | Set aside days each month to work directly with local businesses and host a monthly community workshop |
| Who can get help | Independently owned U.S. small businesses, such as restaurants, retailers, service firms and manufacturers |
| Who is excluded | Software and SaaS companies, venture-backed businesses and private equity-owned businesses |
| What the business gets | Free help plus 3 months of ChatGPT Business at no cost |
| Where | 20 communities, including Chicago, Kansas City, Cleveland, St. Louis, Charlotte, Nashville, Austin, Houston, Atlanta, Los Angeles, Seattle and New York |

[Decrypt's report](https://decrypt.co/383848/openai-will-pay-8k-preach-ai-gospel-small-businesses) adds that partners commit to four full days of in-person work a month, that the $8,000 is a ceiling and not a set salary, and that OpenAI hasn't said what happens when the free ChatGPT Business period ends.

## Does a trade sub qualify?

Probably, if you're independently owned. Contractors aren't named on OpenAI's page, but a family-owned mechanical, electrical or sitework sub is a service business, and many have a fab shop that looks a lot like a small manufacturer.

The exclusion that matters is ownership. If a private equity firm owns your company, even as part of a larger platform of trade contractors, you're out.

## What should a sub bring to the consultant?

Four days a month split across several local businesses doesn't leave much time for each one. Pick one office problem that is repetitive, text-heavy and easy to measure. Good candidates:

1. **T&M tickets to change order requests.** Turning a stack of signed field tickets into a priced CO request with backup.
2. **Submittal log status emails.** Drafting the weekly "here's what's open and overdue" note to the GC from your log.
3. **Bid invite triage.** Reading invites and summarizing scope, bid date, bonding and prevailing wage requirements so the estimator can say yes or no faster.
4. **Certified payroll prep checks.** Flagging missing classifications or hours before the report goes out. Someone who knows the wage determination still signs it.

Write down how long the task takes today before the first session. If you can't measure the before, you won't know if the after is better.

## What should a sub watch out for?

- **The consultant isn't neutral.** They're paid by OpenAI and the package includes ChatGPT Business. Ask directly whether your accounting, estimating or project management software already does this before building something new.
- **The free period ends.** After three months you either pay for the seats or lose what you built inside that account. Price it before you start.
- **Somebody has to own it.** If a partner builds a custom tool, find out where it lives, who can fix it, and what happens if the partner moves on. We covered what goes wrong with homemade apps when [24,664 Lovable-built apps were found exposing data with no login](/posts/2026-11-16-lovable-apps-24664-open-databases-construction-gc-ops-homemade-prequal-punch-list-apps-login-check).
- **Watch what goes in.** Decide before the first session whether owner contracts, employee pay data or bid numbers are allowed in the account, and check your GC and owner contracts for data clauses.
- **The success stories are self-reported.** Decrypt cites a gift shop owner who estimated a custom inventory dashboard would have cost about $50,000. That is the owner's estimate, not an audited number.

## What are the limits?

This is a pilot in 20 communities with no published end date, application deadline or pricing after month three. OpenAI's examples are retail and food businesses, not contractors, so nobody has shown yet how it works for a sub with field crews. A partner may know AI well and know nothing about a submittal log or a wage determination.

**Takeaway:** If you're independently owned and near one of the 20 pilot communities, it costs nothing to try. Pick one office task, time it this week, and bring that number to the first session. If the result won't keep working after the consultant leaves and the free seats end, don't adopt it.
