Meta announced on Oct. 27 that it is raising its planned stock sale to $97.5 billion to pay for AI infrastructure, and Nvidia and AMD are investing in it. For a mechanical, electrical or sitework sub chasing data center work, that is good news about the owner's appetite. It says nothing about whether your pay application gets funded on time, and that depends on your contract.

## What was actually announced?

Per [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/meta-eyes-record-975-billion-equity-raise-to-fund-ai-expansion-4975360) and [The American Bazaar](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/10/27/meta-ai-fundraising-plan-97-5-billion-nvidia-amd-490881), the pieces are:

| Piece | Amount |
|---|---|
| Common stock | $25 billion at $625 per share |
| Preferred depositary shares | $12.5 billion (250 million shares at $50) |
| At-the-market program | Up to $60 billion, starting after new Class C shares begin trading in Q1 2027 |
| Nvidia investment | $3 billion |
| AMD investment | $1 billion |

The total was $22.5 billion the day before. The at-the-market portion has no finalized timing or terms, so about $35 billion is the firm public offering today. Reports say Meta's shares fell roughly 1.4% in morning trading. The coverage we reviewed does not break out how much goes to buildings, power or site work, so we are not assigning a construction number to it.

## Does an owner with cash mean a safe pay app?

Not by itself. Three gaps sit between a parent company's raise and your check:

1. **Who signed your contract.** Large owners often build through project-specific entities or developers. Your counterparty may not be the company in the headline.
2. **Pay-when-paid language.** If your payment is conditioned on the owner paying the GC, a funding delay upstream becomes your delay.
3. **Timing.** Much of this money arrives in stages. The biggest piece depends on shares that have not started trading.

## What are Nvidia and AMD doing in the deal?

Nvidia and AMD sell the chips and server systems that go into these buildings. [Fox News's AI live blog](https://foxnews.com/live-news/ai-news-anthropic-claude-openai-chatgpt-10-27-26) notes Nvidia's $3 billion comes alongside expanded work on Meta's systems. We can't tell from the reporting how much cycles back to the investors as hardware orders, but suppliers funding a customer's build-out is worth noting. It means schedule and spending are tied to equipment deliveries, which is where your sequencing risk lives.

## What should a sub ask before pricing this work?

- Which legal entity is the owner, and who is the guarantor, if anyone?
- Is there evidence of funds or a financing close for this specific project?
- What are the payment terms, and does pay-when-paid or pay-if-paid apply?
- What lien rights or payment bond protection do you have?
- What is the retainage, and when is it released?

## What still isn't known?

We don't know which projects this money lands on, how much is construction versus chips, or when. A bigger war chest makes a pause in owner spending less likely in the near term. It does not remove payment risk on any individual job.

## What should a PM or estimator do this week?

Add a credit and contract check to your pursuit checklist for any large AI data center job. Ask for the owner entity and financing evidence at the same time you ask for the schedule. If the answers are vague, price the extra float on receivables, or ask for shorter billing cycles.
