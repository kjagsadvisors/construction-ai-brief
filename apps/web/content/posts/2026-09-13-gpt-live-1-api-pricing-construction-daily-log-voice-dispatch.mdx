OpenAI opened API access to GPT-Live-1, its full-duplex voice model, on September 10 — at $0.05 a minute for the voice layer. That's the piece that was missing back in July, when [we covered GPT-Live's ChatGPT-only launch](/posts/2026-07-09-openai-gpt-live-full-duplex-voice-daily-log) and noted that Procore, Autodesk, and Trimble couldn't build it into their platforms because there was no API and no price. Now there's both, which means the math on hands-free field dictation just became something you can actually run.

## What changed since July?

Full-duplex means the model listens and talks at the same time — it can murmur "got it" while you're still mid-sentence and ask a follow-up without waiting for a pause, closer to a live phone call than a dictation app. That part isn't new; OpenAI shipped it inside ChatGPT in July. What's new is that it's now available to any developer through the API, OpenAI says it improved full-duplex performance by 30 percentage points over its prior voice model on its own benchmark, and it's priced by the minute instead of bundled into a ChatGPT subscription. OpenAI also shipped 12 new voices covering more accents and languages, and reported that an early partner, the AI vendor EliseAI, cut its voice-agent codebase by roughly 80% after switching to it — a sign of how much custom engineering this replaces for a company that would otherwise build its own turn-taking and interruption handling from scratch.

## What would this actually cost on a jobsite?

At $0.05 a minute, an hour of active back-and-forth — narrating the day's crew count and delays into a daily log, dictating an RFI, or running a dispatch call — costs about $3. Over a 22-day work month, that's roughly $65 per superintendent for the voice layer alone.

| Use | Estimated active voice time/day | Voice-layer cost/day | Voice-layer cost/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| End-of-day daily log dictation | ~15 min | $0.75 | ~$16.50 |
| RFI dictation + field calls | ~30 min | $1.50 | ~$33 |
| Full hands-free field logging | ~60 min | $3.00 | ~$65 |

That's cheap enough that per-minute voice pricing stops being the bottleneck for a vendor deciding whether to build this. But it's only half the bill. GPT-Live-1 is explicitly a front-end: OpenAI says it hands off actual reasoning and actions to a paired model, such as GPT-6 Astra, or a tool like Codex or ChatGPT Work. The model that turns "we lost two hours because the crane delivery was late" into a structured, CSI-coded daily log entry is a separate API call with its own token cost — one that doesn't show up in the $0.05 headline rate. When a vendor prices "AI voice logging" as an add-on to your subscription, the number worth asking for is the blended cost per user, not the voice-minute rate.

## What this doesn't answer yet

OpenAI's 30-point improvement is measured on its own Full Duplex Bench — a lab benchmark, not a jobsite. Nobody has published how the interruption handling performs against a running compressor, two radios on different channels, and a crew member cutting in mid-sentence, which is a rougher acoustic environment than the quiet-room demos or call-center use cases OpenAI highlighted at launch. The same caution we raised in July still applies: cheap and available is not the same as field-tested.

## Should you act on this now?

There's still no packaged product here — no "AI daily log" app you can buy today built specifically on GPT-Live-1. What changed is that the excuse is gone. Any construction software vendor that told you real-time voice dictation was "coming soon" because the underlying model wasn't available now has a priced, working API to build on. The next time a field-reporting or dispatch vendor demos a voice feature, ask which voice model is under it, what the noisy-environment accuracy actually is on a real site recording, and what the total per-user cost looks like once the reasoning layer is added in — not just the price of the part that listens.
