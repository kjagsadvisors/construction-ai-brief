The FCC has approved SpaceX's plan to put AI data centers in orbit, but the first satellite isn't required to be working until November 2033. For an electrical or mechanical sub with data center work in backlog, the approval doesn't change the next five years. The question that matters on today's jobs is whether each ground project you're bidding has its land, power and money in place.

## What did the FCC actually approve?

On Wednesday, November 25, FCC Chairman Brendan Carr announced that SpaceX may deploy up to 1 million satellites for Starmind, an orbital network meant to run AI workloads on solar power and send the results back to Earth, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/spacex-fcc-satellite-approval.html). Carr said the action "opens a bold new frontier for the computing infrastructure needed to power America's leadership," per [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/feds-approve-spacexs-ai-data-center-satellites-all-1-million-of-them-2000845526).

The approval comes with deadlines and conditions:

| Item | What the approval says |
|---|---|
| First operational satellite | By November 2033 |
| 10% of the network (100,000 satellites) | By November 2035 |
| Half the network | By November 2038 |
| Full network | By 2040 |
| Conditions | Communications backups, regular updates and a detailed orbital debris plan ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/fcc-clears-spacex-million-satellites-starmind-data-center/)) |
| Denied | A SpaceX waiver on how satellites above 600 km are disposed of at end of life |

SpaceX has just over 11,000 Starlink satellites in orbit today, so Starmind would grow the number of satellites in Earth orbit roughly 50 times. Gizmodo notes the plan depends on Starship flying at a rate and capacity no rocket has reached. Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society petitioned against it, and Amazon called it "a lofty ambition rather than a real plan."

## Does this threaten data center work on the ground?

Not on any timeline that affects a sub's backlog. Even if SpaceX hits every deadline, the first orbital data center comes online about seven years from now, and 10% of the network two years after that. The switchgear, chillers, busway and fire protection going into campuses now are for buildings that will run long before then.

Expect the headline to come up anyway, at the bank, with a bonding agent, or with a partner wondering whether to add a second data center crew. The answer is the FCC's own schedule.

## What should actually drive data center hiring and equipment decisions?

The near-term risk is on the ground, and it's about money and power. On Nvidia's latest earnings call, CFO and interim CEO Colette Kress said the company's guarantees for neocloud data centers apply only once a facility is built, operational and accepted, and that if a project is delayed or hits building problems, "our guarantee doesn't exist anymore," per [Capacity](https://capacityglobal.com/news/nvidia-trims-neocloud-commitments/). Nvidia's neocloud commitments fell to $32 billion at the end of October from $36 billion three months earlier.

That pushes developers hard on schedule and makes speculative projects the weakest. On TechCrunch's [Equity podcast](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/ai-data-centers-are-becoming-harder-to-build-and-neoclouds-are-feeling-it/), the hosts and a site developer described neoclouds facing construction delays and more expensive debt, community opposition and state rules slowing some markets, and a possible shakeout in 2027.

Before you add crews, prefab shop capacity or equipment for data center work, sort each project in your pipeline:

1. **Power.** Is there a signed utility agreement and a substation date, or only a request?
2. **Money.** Who is the lender or backer, and is the financing closed? A project waiting on a guarantee that only applies after completion is still at risk.
3. **Tenant.** Is there a signed lease with a hyperscaler or AI lab, or is the developer building first and finding a tenant later?
4. **Permits and local approval.** Are zoning and air permits in hand, or still facing hearings?

Projects that pass all four are the ones to staff for. Price the rest for delay, and don't sign equipment leases that only pay off if they go forward on schedule. ## What's still uncertain?

SpaceX has not published compute capacity figures for Starmind, and GeekWire reports only that each satellite would carry Nvidia GPUs and CPUs. Google, Blue Origin and Starcloud are pursuing similar networks, and none runs at data center scale today. If launches begin and early satellites work as advertised, the long-range picture for mega-campuses after the 2030s could change. That is a question to revisit in a few years, not one to plan crews around now.

**The takeaway:** don't let a space headline make you pull back from data center work, or push you into more of it. This month, score every data center job in your pipeline on power, money, tenant and permits, and tie new hiring and equipment to the projects that pass.
