D.R. Horton, the country's largest homebuilder by volume, has signed a multi-year deal to buy robot-built home components from Promise Robotics, covering more than 1,250 homes. For framing, plumbing and HVAC subs, the news is not that robots are taking over. It is that a builder this size is testing off-site production of work that has traditionally been a site subcontract.

## What was announced?

Promise Robotics announced the agreement on October 13. D.R. Horton will use the company's Factory-as-a-Service platform, and the deal makes Horton the first major U.S. builder on it, according to the coverage. Promise's new plant, slated to start manufacturing in early 2027, will supply the homes.

| Item | Reported detail |
|---|---|
| Homes covered | More than 1,250 |
| Structure | Multi-year commercial agreement, Factory-as-a-Service |
| Components | Interior and exterior walls, flooring, stairs, roofs |
| Newer additions | HVAC and plumbing systems (per CoStar) |
| Speed claim | A typical 1,800 sq ft home framed in about eight hours (per HousingWire) |
| Not disclosed | Contract value, production schedule, unit costs, independently measured savings |

HousingWire frames the deal as a pilot. The speed figure is Promise's own claim, not a measured result.

## Why does "Factory-as-a-Service" matter more than the robots?

Horton is not buying a factory. It is renting production capacity run by someone else, which lets a builder shift work off site without taking on plant overhead. If it works, the next question for Horton is which trade packages that capacity should absorb.

## What does this mean for framing and MEP subs?

- **Framers:** Wall, floor and roof panel scope is the first target. Even a partial shift changes the bid: less site framing, more setting and connecting.
- **Plumbing and HVAC subs:** Reports that Promise is adding mechanical systems are the part to watch. Pre-installed rough-in in a panel changes who does the work, who inspects it and who warrants it.
- **Everyone on the schedule:** Faster framing compresses the sequence behind it. Rough-in trades may get shorter, less forgiving windows.

## What is still unproven?

Almost everything commercial. No price, no schedule and no savings data are public, and 1,250 homes is a small slice of what a builder this size starts. Panelized and modular building has a long record of promising more than it delivered at scale. This could be a footnote or the template for a new supply chain.

## What should a sub do now?

Ask your Horton-type builder customers direct questions: Which components are moving off site on your next phases? Who sets and connects panels? How are inspections and warranty split? Does my pricing change? If you do framing or rough-in, price a "setting and connecting" scope now, so you are not building it from scratch under a builder's deadline. The announcement answers none of this, which is exactly why you should ask early.
