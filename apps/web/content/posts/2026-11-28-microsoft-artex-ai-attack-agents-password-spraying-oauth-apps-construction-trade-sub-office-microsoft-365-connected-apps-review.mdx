Microsoft says state-backed hackers and criminals are now using a free AI attack framework called ARTEX to guess passwords at machine speed and to get into Microsoft 365 through third-party apps a company already trusts. For a trade sub, that matters because the office email account is where pay apps, change orders, lien waivers and wire instructions move. The practical step this week: get a list of every app connected to your Microsoft 365 and who approved it.

## What did Microsoft actually report?

In a [November 25 security post](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/25/what-defenders-need-to-know-artex-agentic-ai-framework-threat-actors-abuse/), Microsoft described ARTEX as an open-source penetration-testing tool, published in July 2026, in which several AI agents carry an attack from scanning through break-in and reporting with little or no human involvement. The original code was pulled in October, but copies still circulate, and a Chinese security firm now sells a paid version.

Microsoft names six groups using it, including Storm-0940, Salt Typhoon and North Korea's Onyx Sleet. Two numbers from the report stand out:

- In one Storm-0940 campaign, Microsoft logged more than 151,000 failed sign-ins, peaking at 2,900 per minute.
- In another case, an AI agent went from searching GitHub for leaked app passwords to reading data from a company's Azure Key Vault in five minutes.

The tool doesn't invent new attacks. [The Decoder notes](https://the-decoder.com/microsoft-details-how-state-backed-hackers-use-the-artex-ai-framework-to-automate-cyberattacks/) it runs familiar tools like Nmap and Metasploit. What changes is cost and speed: work that used to need a skilled person now runs unattended, against many targets at once.

## Why should a trade sub care about a nation-state tool?

Because the October attack Microsoft ties to ARTEX didn't go after the victims directly. [CyberScoop reported](https://www.cyberscoop.com/microsoft-attributes-connectwise-supply-chain-attack-china-storm-0940/) that Storm-0940 hit ConnectWise's Cloud Backup service, then used it to reach more than 2,250 Microsoft 365 tenants, combining password guessing with abuse of OAuth apps, the "allow this app to access your account" connections. In some tenants the agents changed app credentials to keep access and altered admin roles.

Many small contractors don't run their own IT. They rely on a managed IT provider, and that provider connects backup, monitoring and remote-support tools to the company's Microsoft 365. Each connection is a door that doesn't need anyone's password. Microsoft also notes that a compromised high-privilege app can reach far more than its job requires.

## What should the office do this week?

| Step | Who does it | What to ask or check |
|---|---|---|
| List connected apps | Office manager with IT provider | In Microsoft Entra, Enterprise applications: what's there, who approved it, what it can read |
| Remove what's unused | IT provider | Old estimating add-ins, trial tools, apps from former employees |
| Lock down approvals | IT provider | Stop regular users from granting apps access to company mail and files |
| MFA for everyone | IT provider | Every account, including the shared estimating and AP mailboxes; stronger MFA for admins |
| Block legacy sign-in | IT provider | Older protocols skip MFA; Microsoft lists blocking them as a baseline step |
| Ask your IT provider one question | Owner | "Which of your tools connect to our Microsoft 365, and how would you tell us if one was breached?" |

None of this is new advice. What's new is that attackers can now try it against thousands of small companies at once, so being too small to bother with is less of a defense than it used to be.

## What are the limits of what we know?

Microsoft didn't say how many tenants were actually breached, and it noted a successful sign-in doesn't always mean data was taken. The report doesn't list construction as a targeted sector. Microsoft's own recommended fixes lean heavily on its Defender and Entra security products. Basics like turning on MFA, blocking legacy sign-in and reviewing connected apps don't require buying anything new, though some finer controls need a higher Microsoft 365 tier, so ask your IT provider what your plan includes.

**Takeaway:** Ask your IT provider for the list of connected apps in your Microsoft 365 by Friday. If nobody can produce it within a day, that tells you more than any report about how exposed your pay-app and wire-instruction email really is.
