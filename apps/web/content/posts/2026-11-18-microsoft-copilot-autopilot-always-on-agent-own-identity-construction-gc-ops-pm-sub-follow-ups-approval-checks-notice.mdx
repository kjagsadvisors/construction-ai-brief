A project manager at a GC running Microsoft 365 may soon have an agent that reads their Teams channels and follows up on their email while they walk the job. Microsoft began rolling out that agent, called Autopilot, at its Ignite conference on November 17. Before a project team turns it on, the ops lead should decide which follow-ups it may send on its own and which ones need a person, because some of a PM's follow-ups are contract notices.

## What did Microsoft announce?

Microsoft's [Book of News](https://news.microsoft.com/events/ignite/2026/book-of-news/) calls Autopilot its "first enterprise-grade long-running personal productivity agent." Satya Nadella's [keynote post](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/11/17/ignite-2026-building-the-frontier-ecosystem/) describes it as "an autonomous agent that keeps moving your work forward 24×7." According to Microsoft's [Copilot blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/blog/2026/11/17/copilot-at-microsoft-ignite-expanding-whats-possible-with-intelligence-built-for-work/):

- Each Autopilot has its own identity, memory and tools, and lives in the company's tenant.
- It can monitor Teams channels, follow up on emails, run recurring work and manage projects without waiting for a prompt.
- People can @mention it in Teams, Outlook, chats, channels and documents. The Book of News adds that colleagues can @mention *someone else's* Autopilot in a channel or group chat.
- Microsoft's example is a supplier review: the agent builds the schedule and workback plan, handles prep, meetings and follow-ups, and contacts stakeholders for updates.

It is rolling out to customers in Frontier, the program where Microsoft ships Copilot features ahead of general release. Microsoft gave no general availability date and no price for Autopilot. Its Copilot blog says only that Copilot combines a per-user subscription with usage-based charges for the most advanced AI.

## What controls come with it?

| Control | What Microsoft says |
|---|---|
| Identity | Each Autopilot has a Microsoft Entra Agent ID and a named manager |
| IT scope | IT can scope its access or block it |
| Audit trail | Its actions are logged under its own name |
| User choice | The user picks which work it takes on |
| Approval | The user can require a check before key actions |

Those are vendor descriptions of a product in early rollout. We have not seen independent testing of how well Autopilot holds up over weeks of real work.

## What does this mean for a GC's project team?

Swap "supplier review" for "submittal chase" and the fit is obvious. A PM's week is full of recurring follow-up: who owes a resubmittal, which RFI has sat past its review period, what's still open before the OAC meeting. An always-on agent that drafts those nudges and assembles the open-items list is a real time saver if it works.

Three things need an answer first.

1. **Can it message people outside the company?** Most of a PM's follow-ups go to subs, architects and owner's reps. Microsoft's announcements do not say whether Autopilot can contact people outside the tenant. If it can't, it is an internal assistant, which is still useful but a smaller thing.
2. **What counts as a "key action"?** A reminder that a submittal is late is routine. An email telling a sub its delay is affecting the schedule may be notice under the subcontract. A reply in a Teams channel saying "yes, proceed" is direction. The approval check Microsoft describes is where a firm draws that line, and the default should be that anything touching schedule, cost, scope or direction waits for the PM.
3. **Who answers when it's @mentioned?** A project engineer can ask the PM's Autopilot a question while the PM is on site. The answer comes from an agent working off the PM's memory and files. We covered Anthropic research last week in which Claude agents assigned one per user made [unsupported claims about other people, such as agreement that never happened,](/posts/2026-11-13-anthropic-worse-together-per-user-agents-vs-shared-coordinator-construction-gc-ops-project-team-shared-logs-schedules) in about 53 to 58% of team test runs. Those tests were not of Autopilot, but the failure is the one to watch for.

## Should a mid-size GC act now?

Most firms can't turn it on yet, so the work this month is policy, not setup. It costs nothing to do early and it applies to any vendor's always-on agent, not only Microsoft's.

**Takeaway:** Write a one-page list with two columns. Left: follow-ups an agent may send unreviewed (internal reminders, meeting prep, open-items lists). Right: anything a person must approve (messages to subs, designers or the owner; anything about time, money or scope). Then ask whoever administers Microsoft 365 two questions: are we in Frontier, and when Autopilot arrives, who gets it first?
