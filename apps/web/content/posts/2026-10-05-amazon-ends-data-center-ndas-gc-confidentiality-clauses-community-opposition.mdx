Amazon Web Services says it no longer signs nondisclosure agreements with government agencies on data center projects, and the change applies only going forward. For a GC or trade sub on a hyperscale job, that is a signal that owner secrecy is under pressure. It does not change the confidentiality clause in your own subcontract.

## What did Amazon announce?

On October 2, AWS CEO Matt Garman announced in a post that Amazon no longer uses NDAs with the government agencies it works with on data center projects, according to TechCrunch and Data Center Dynamics. He paired it with a program called Built Together, worth more than $1 billion over five years for host communities.

Context from the reporting:

- Rep. Jamie Raskin sent letters to Amazon, Google, Meta and Oracle days earlier asking about NDAs signed with public officials.
- Garman cited more than 100 data center moratoriums under consideration across the country.
- The change is forward-looking. Existing agreements are not described as cancelled.
- Microsoft said in March it would end existing NDAs with local governments, per the Implicator report.

## Does this change anything for contractors?

Not directly. The reported change covers agreements between Amazon and government agencies. Nothing in the coverage says it reaches the confidentiality, publicity or project-name clauses that owners and GCs push down to subs. Those still say what they say.

What it does change is the environment those clauses operate in. Permit files, hearings and public-records requests are where opposition forms, and less secrecy at the agency level means more of a project's scope, power and water numbers becomes public sooner.

## What should a GC or sub check now?

| Clause | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Confidentiality / publicity | Does it bar you from confirming you work on the project, even when the owner has disclosed it publicly? |
| Public-records carve-out | If an agency receives your submittals or drawings, who responds to a records request, and who tells you? |
| Hearing and testimony | Can an employee answer a public official's question truthfully without breaching the NDA? |
| Delay and suspension | If a moratorium or permit challenge halts work, is the time and cost yours, the owner's, or shared? |
| Long-lead commitments | Are generators, switchgear or crews committed before permits are final? |

## Is this an AI story or a construction story?

Both. These facilities exist to serve AI demand, and the opposition is aimed at them for that reason. The $1 billion pledge is also small against Amazon's capital spending, roughly 0.1% of its 2026 total according to Implicator, so it is unlikely on its own to end local opposition. Treat it as a PR and political move whose effect on permitting is unproven.

## What is the takeaway?

Read the confidentiality and delay clauses on any data center contract you hold or are bidding. If the NDA would stop you from answering a public official, or the suspension clause leaves moratorium risk on your side, raise it before mobilization, not after a hearing is scheduled. And do not assume a tenant's new transparency policy reaches your paper.
