Palantir reported record second-quarter results this week: revenue up 93% year-over-year to $1.94 billion, U.S. commercial revenue up 149% to $764 million, and raised full-year guidance to as much as $8.16 billion. The stock jumped as much as 29% on the news. The clearest construction-industry read on that growth isn't in the earnings call — it's in a deal Palantir signed months earlier with McCarthy Building Companies, a top-20 U.S. general contractor that's building its entire field-operations system on Palantir's AI platform.

## What did Palantir actually report?

Palantir's Q2 2026 numbers, by segment: U.S. commercial revenue hit $764 million, up 149% year-over-year; U.S. government revenue reached $809 million, up 90%. Net income nearly tripled to $1.07 billion from $329 million a year earlier. The company closed 220 deals worth at least $1 million in the quarter — 98 of them worth $5 million or more, 73 worth $10 million or more — and raised its full-year revenue guidance from roughly $7.65 billion to as much as $8.16 billion. CEO Alex Karp told investors demand for what he calls "AI sovereignty" — customers wanting to own and control the data model behind their own decisions — had been "unleashed."

## What is McCarthy actually running on Palantir?

McCarthy — founded in 1864, 100% employee-owned since 2002, and generating roughly $4.5 billion in annual revenue — signed a multi-year, multi-million-dollar partnership with Palantir in June 2026. The centerpiece is Pulse, McCarthy's AI Operations Suite, built on Palantir's AIP and modeled in Palantir's Ontology — a data layer meant to tie McCarthy's schedules, crews, equipment, and project history into one operational model instead of scattered spreadsheets and point systems. The pitch, per McCarthy senior superintendent Dave Evans: it's supposed to let field leadership "focus on what matters most for our clients and project teams, rather than getting buried in emails, documents and data." Pulse is aimed at giving superintendents and PMs real-time scenario planning and risk analysis without waiting on the office to compile a report.

## Is this the same category as Procore or OpenSpace?

No, and the difference is the part worth understanding before any similar pitch lands on your desk.

| | Point tool | Full operating platform |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Procore, OpenSpace, Buildots | Palantir AIP / Ontology |
| What it needs from you | Connects to data you already have | You model your operations inside its proprietary data layer first |
| Time to value | Weeks | Months, with dedicated build resources |
| Who it fits | Any size GC or sub | Enterprise GCs with in-house data/IT teams |
| Switching cost | Low — export and move on | High — your operating model lives inside the vendor's structure |

A point tool reads your existing data and adds a capability on top. A platform like AIP asks you to build your company's operational model inside its structure before it does anything — which is a bigger lift, and a much stickier one.

## Should a mid-size GC or sub act on this?

Not directly — McCarthy's deal is priced and staffed for a multi-billion-dollar contractor, and there's no disclosed version of it sized for a 50-person mechanical sub or a regional GC. But the signal is worth tracking: this is the largest, most concrete example yet of a big AI platform vendor treating a construction company's entire operating data — not one workflow — as the product. If that category keeps growing, expect more owners and large GCs to push similar "connected" platform requirements down onto their subs' reporting and data-sharing obligations.

## The takeaway

Before you sign anything pitched as a "connected" or "unified data" AI platform — at any scale — ask one question up front: what does getting your data back out look like on day one if you leave in three years. A tool that reads your existing systems is a low-risk add. A platform that asks you to rebuild your operating model inside its own proprietary layer is a different kind of commitment, and McCarthy's deal is the clearest example on record of what that commitment actually looks like in construction.
