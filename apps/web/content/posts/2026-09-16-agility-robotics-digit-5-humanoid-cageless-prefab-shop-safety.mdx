Agility Robotics unveiled Digit 5 on September 15, a humanoid robot built to work shoulder-to-shoulder with people on a factory floor with no safety cage around it. For a general contractor or trade sub, that's not a jobsite story yet — it's a prefab-shop story, and the timeline is 2027, not this quarter.

## What did Agility actually announce?

Digit 5 can repeatedly lift up to 50 pounds, a 40% jump over its predecessor, and runs for 90 minutes on a charge that takes just 9 minutes — a 10:1 run-to-charge ratio, up from 2:1 on Digit 4, which lets the robot stay productive more than 20 hours out of 24. The headline change is safety architecture: instead of the fencing or cages that have kept industrial robots physically separated from workers, Digit 5 uses onboard sensors and AI to continuously detect people nearby and autonomously avoid them, stop, or drop into a seated position when someone gets close. Agility says the model has already drawn more than $300 million in multi-year orders, on top of a predecessor that's logged over 65,000 work hours at customers including GXO, Schaeffler, Amazon, and Toyota's Canadian manufacturing plant.

## What does Digit 5 actually do all day?

Agility is positioning Digit 5 to take on more of a facility's material handling: unloading pallets, tending machines, gathering parts, and inspecting items before loading them back onto pallets for shipment. None of that is jobsite work — it's the task list of a warehouse or manufacturing floor.

## Why does that matter to a prefab shop?

Because a modular or MEP prefab operation *is* that kind of floor. Building wall panels, MEP racks, or bathroom pods off-site is already run more like light manufacturing than like a jobsite — controlled conditions, repeatable stations, material staged and moved on pallets. That's precisely the environment Digit 5's current customers operate in, and it's why the task list lines up so cleanly:

| Digit 5 task | Prefab shop equivalent |
|---|---|
| Unloading pallets | Receiving raw stock — studs, sheet stock, MEP components |
| Tending machines | Feeding a CNC panel line or pipe-fab station |
| Gathering parts | Staging kit-of-parts for a wall or rack assembly |
| Inspecting before shipment | QA check before a panel or rack ships to the field |

A robot that can do that work standing next to a person, without a cage between them, is a closer fit for a fab shop's floor plan than most industrial automation that's come before it.

## What's the catch?

Two, and they're both worth sitting with before anyone gets ahead of the announcement. First, it's not available: Agility's own timeline has early access starting in the first half of 2027 and general availability for manufacturing and warehouse operators by the end of that year — this is a signal to watch, not a purchase decision to make now. Second, a cageless robot working near people is a safety-compliance question, not just a productivity one. Industrial mobile robots operating around workers without physical barriers fall under ANSI/RIA R15.08, and a prefab operator would need to work through that standard — sensor reliability, response distance, what happens on a power loss — the same way any employer would before putting a new class of equipment on a floor with people on it. As CNBC noted covering the broader construction-robotics push this month, the industry is "a long way from humanoid homebuilders" — this is a warehouse-and-factory robot, not a jobsite one, and it's likely to stay that way for years.

## The takeaway

If your shop runs any kind of off-site fabrication — panels, racks, pods — Digit 5 is worth a line on next year's capital-planning radar, not a call to a vendor this week. Watch for two things before it's relevant: Agility opening early access outside its current logistics-and-manufacturing customer base, and whether ANSI/RIA R15.08 compliance guidance starts showing up written for construction-adjacent fabrication rather than pure warehousing. Until then, the material-handling labor question in your prefab shop still has a human answer.
