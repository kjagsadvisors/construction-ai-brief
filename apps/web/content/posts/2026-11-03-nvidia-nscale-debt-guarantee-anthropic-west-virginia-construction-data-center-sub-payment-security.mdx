Nvidia is reportedly in talks to guarantee billions of dollars of Nscale's borrowing for a West Virginia data center that Anthropic plans to use. If you're a sub on that kind of job, the guarantee helps the owner borrow, but it does not promise you a check. Your protection is still written in your subcontract.

## What was reported?

On Oct. 29, The Information reported that Nvidia is discussing credit support for Nscale, and Reuters reported the same, according to [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/nvidia-weighs-guaranteeing-billions-in-nscale-debt-for-anthropic). The guarantee would help Nscale borrow more cheaply to build the campus. The reports say the money would cover power infrastructure and construction costs. The deal is not signed, and terms could change.

Anthropic's side is a separate arrangement. Reports describe a roughly $45 billion deal to rent computing capacity from Nscale's West Virginia campus, and Nscale's filing reportedly values the Anthropic agreements at up to $44.6 billion ([StockTwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/this-nvidia-backed-cloud-computing-firm-is-reportedly-in-talks-to-sign-a-45-b-compute-deal-with-anthropic/cZx8CiMRBu2)). Bloomberg-sourced reports describe a second building of 460 megawatts that is still in talks. Treat the specific numbers as reported, not confirmed.

## Why does a debt guarantee matter to a subcontractor?

It tells you the project's financing is leaning on a stronger credit than the developer's own. [Finimize](https://finimize.com/content/nvidia-looks-to-backstop-nscales-ai-data-center-debt) notes the context: other Nvidia-backed AI cloud firms have faced scrutiny over debt, and CoreWeave's shares slumped amid concern about its $50 billion debt load.

That cuts two ways:

| Signal | What it suggests | What it doesn't tell you |
|---|---|---|
| Guarantor attached to the loan | Lenders want extra comfort, so financing may close | Whether your contracting entity can pay you |
| Large tenant commitment | Demand for the space is real | Whether construction funds are released on your pay-app schedule |
| Talks, not a signed deal | Financing is still being arranged | Whether it closes before you mobilize |

A backstop between a lender and a guarantor runs between those two parties. You are not a party to it.

## What should a sub ask before pricing this work?

1. **Who is the owner entity?** Special-purpose entities are common on campus builds. Know whose name is on your contract.
2. **Is construction financing closed?** Ask for written confirmation, not a verbal assurance.
3. **What are the payment terms?** Look for pay-if-paid or pay-when-paid language that ties your money to the owner's funding.
4. **What security do you have?** Confirm lien rights, bond requirements, and whether a payment bond or parent guarantee is available.
5. **What happens on a stop?** Check suspension rights and demobilization compensation if funding is delayed.

## What's still unclear?

Whether the guarantee is signed, how large it is, and what conditions attach. Reports also conflict on timing of the Anthropic arrangements, with a West Virginia official publicly discussing a reported $45 billion investment in August ([WDTV](https://www.wdtv.com/2026/08/27/west-virginia-data-center-lands-reported-45-billion-ai-agreement-morrisey-says/)). Until the financing is documented, none of this changes your risk on a bid.

## The takeaway

Before you price a campus job, get the owner entity and financing status in writing, and negotiate payment terms that don't hinge on someone else's loan closing. A big name on the guarantee is a reason to look closer at the contract, not to relax about it.
