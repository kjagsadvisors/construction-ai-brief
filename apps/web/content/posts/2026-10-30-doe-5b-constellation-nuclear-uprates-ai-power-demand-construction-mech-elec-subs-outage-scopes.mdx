DOE has offered Constellation Energy up to $5 billion to add nearly 1.1 gigawatts at eight existing nuclear plants, and the tech-company power contracts behind these upgrades mean mechanical, electrical, and controls subs near those plants should start asking now how the work will be packaged. The commitment is conditional, and no scope or schedule has been published.

## What did DOE actually announce?

On October 27, 2026, DOE's Office of Energy Dominance Financing announced a conditional commitment of up to $5 billion. It covers uprates and modernization at Braidwood, Byron, Clinton, Dresden, LaSalle, and Quad Cities in Illinois, Calvert Cliffs in Maryland, and Limerick in Pennsylvania. DOE says the projects would add nearly 1.1 GW and support more than 10,000 project-related jobs, without splitting construction from other roles. Constellation must still satisfy technical, legal, environmental, and financial conditions before the loan funds.

DOE's release doesn't mention data centers. The AI link comes from the offtake side: Power Engineering reports a 20-year Google agreement for 890 MW of uprates at 11 reactors, with Constellation expecting to invest more than $4.3 billion, and 20-year Meta contracts with Vistra that include 433 MW of uprates. The first additional Constellation capacity is expected in 2028.

## Why does this matter to a trade sub?

Uprates are not ground-up construction. Trade coverage describes them as upgrades to turbines, steam generators, controls, and other equipment at running plants. That points to a specific mix of work:

| Likely scope area | Trades touched | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Turbine and steam-side equipment | Mechanical, millwrights, pipefitters | Which scopes are bid by the owner and which sit under an EPC or OEM? |
| Controls and instrumentation | Electrical, controls | Are upgrades staged across outages? |
| Balance-of-plant electrical | Electrical (Div 26) | Who supplies switchgear and long-lead gear? |
| Support work | Rigging, insulation, scaffolding, welding | Which qualification requirements apply? |

Treat that table as a prompt for questions, not a bid list. We found no published scope, bid schedule, or contracting plan for these eight sites.

## Should a mid-size sub chase this?

Probably not directly, and that's the honest answer. Nuclear-site work typically carries quality-assurance programs, personnel qualification, and badging requirements that most commercial subs don't have, and the work is usually sequenced around plant outages. The practical paths are:

1. **Second-tier supply or labor** to an established nuclear contractor already on site.
2. **Adjacent work**: offsite fabrication, rigging, or industrial electrical packages that don't need full nuclear qualification.
3. **Labor-market defense**: expect pipefitters, electricians, and welders in Illinois, Maryland, and Pennsylvania to get recruited into these projects. Wage pressure on your commercial jobs is the near-term effect most subs will feel.

## What's still unknown?

Plenty. The loan is conditional. DOE gives no completion dates. Which scopes are competitively bid is unpublished. And capacity depends on regulatory approvals that no one has confirmed here. Don't price labor around announced megawatts.

If you work within a few hours of these plants, call your estimating lead and one supplier this week: ask who the prime contractors will be, and whether your crews' qualifications match.
