OpenAI started testing a new ChatGPT ad format on September 16, 2026, called Sponsored Agents: a business can attach its own AI agent to an ad, and a user who clicks it drops into a separate, clearly labeled chat with that agent before deciding whether to follow through. Angi — the home-services marketplace that a large share of remodelers, electricians, plumbers, and HVAC subs already buy leads from — is one of the first companies testing it. For a trade sub whose pipeline runs partly through Angi, the practical change is this: the first conversation a homeowner has about their project may now happen entirely inside ChatGPT, with an AI agent, before your name ever comes up.

## What exactly did OpenAI ship?

Sponsored Agents let an advertiser's business agent answer follow-up questions after someone clicks an ad in ChatGPT — OpenAI's own example is a shopper asking whether a dining table fits their space. The conversation is walled off from ChatGPT's regular answers and from the user's underlying chat history; OpenAI says advertisers don't get access to a user's private conversations, and ad content doesn't influence ChatGPT's independent answers. The feature is live in limited testing with select U.S. advertisers, alongside new OpenAI ad integrations with HubSpot and Shopify. It is not yet available broadly, and OpenAI is still taking signups from businesses that want in.

## How is Angi actually using it?

Angi announced on September 16 that its own branded agent can now surface inside relevant ChatGPT conversations about home services. A homeowner asking about, say, replacing a water heater or adding a bathroom can keep talking to the Angi agent about scope, and when the project is ready for a professional, the agent routes them into Angi's existing service-request flow to get matched with local pros. This builds on the Angi app in ChatGPT, which launched in March 2026 and let homeowners move from a home-improvement question straight to a contractor match without leaving the chat.

## Does chat-based intake actually change lead quality?

Angi has already published numbers on its predecessor feature, AI Helper (live since June 2025), and they're specific: homeowners who use AI Helper are three times more likely to request a quote than homeowners who just browse Angi's site, and those who start a project with AI Helper are 25% more likely to say the project got finished successfully. That's Angi's own claim, not an independent audit, but it's a concrete signal that a conversational intake funnel produces a different — likely further-along, better-scoped — lead than a static form fill. A sub evaluating whether to lean into Angi leads now has a specific question to ask: does that same lift apply to leads sourced through the new ChatGPT ad pilot, or is it limited to homeowners who already opened the Angi app?

## What's different about the funnel now

| Old Angi lead funnel | New ChatGPT-agent funnel |
|---|---|
| Homeowner searches, browses Angi.com or app, fills a project form | Homeowner is mid-conversation in ChatGPT, clicks a sponsored ad, and describes the job to Angi's agent directly |
| Sub sees the lead after it's submitted, with whatever fields Angi's form captured | Job scope, timeline, and homeowner expectations get shaped by an AI conversation the sub never sees |
| Angi controls placement on its own platform | OpenAI controls placement inside ChatGPT's ad inventory — a paid surface Angi has to buy into |
| Lead cost and exclusivity terms are Angi's standard rules | Pilot-stage; pricing and lead terms for agent-sourced leads aren't yet standardized |

## What should a trade sub actually do this week?

Three concrete steps, none of which require waiting for the pilot to expand:

1. **Ask your Angi rep directly** whether any of your current leads are coming from the Sponsored Agent pilot, and if not, when your trade category might be included.
2. **Get a copy of what the agent actually asks homeowners.** If an AI agent is setting expectations on price range or timeline before you ever see the job, you need to know what was promised so your quote doesn't look like a bait-and-switch.
3. **Watch your close rate on Angi leads specifically**, separate from other lead sources, over the next few months. If Angi's 3x-quote-request claim holds for these new leads too, the leads are worth more — and Angi will likely price them accordingly.

The bigger pattern to watch is the same one behind [the AI-search-optimization funding surge for product recommendations](/posts/2026-09-16-profound-ai-search-startup-180m-construction-product-substitutes): AI chat interfaces are becoming a paid, controllable surface that sits between you and your customer. On the estimating side, that's about which products get recommended. On the trade side, it's about who gets recommended — and right now, that recommendation engine belongs to Angi and OpenAI, not to you.
