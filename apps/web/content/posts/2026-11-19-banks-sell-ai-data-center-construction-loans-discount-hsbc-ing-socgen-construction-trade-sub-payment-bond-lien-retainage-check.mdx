Banks that financed AI data center construction are selling their shares of those loans for less than face value. HSBC sold its roughly $300 million slice of the $18 billion Project Jupiter construction loan at about 85 cents on the dollar, and ING and Société Générale have offered up to $800 million more. For an electrical, mechanical or sitework sub working on one of these campuses, that price is a warning about the same risk you carry in your receivables: the project finishing late or not on the terms everyone planned.

## What did the banks sell?

The figures below come from reporting by Bloomberg, Reuters and the Financial Times, as summarized by [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/oracle-stock-hsbc-sold-its-300-million-slice-of-an-18-billion-data-center-loan-in-the-mid-80s/), [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/347740/ING-puts-500-million-of-data-center-loans-up-for-sale-Bloomberg-News-reports) and [Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/news/ing-shops-500-million-in-ai-data-center-loans-as-banks-trim/).

| Lender | What it sold or offered | Reported price |
|---|---|---|
| HSBC | About $300 million of the $18 billion Project Jupiter construction loan (New Mexico, 2.45 GW, part of Stargate) | About 85 cents on the dollar |
| ING | About $500 million across 12 loans, including a $25 million Jupiter slice and debt linked to Vantage Data Centers and CyrusOne. Bids due November 19 | Not disclosed |
| Société Générale | About $300 million tied to Project Lighthouse (Wisconsin) and Project Frontier (Texas) | Quoted above 90 cents, per the FT |

The Jupiter loans were quoted at about 89 to 91 cents in September, per earlier FT reporting cited by FinanceFeeds. That was the month Oracle sent the developer, a Blue Owl Capital unit, a force majeure notice seeking protection if delays kept the campus from opening in 2028. The project has faced delays on energy infrastructure, air-quality approvals and pipeline permits. Oracle has said the project remains on schedule.

HSBC's slice was less than 2% of the package, and the sale price does not show HSBC's actual loss. ING declined to comment on its sale.

## Does this mean subs on these jobs won't get paid?

No. In a syndicated construction loan, one bank selling its slice usually changes who holds the debt, not how draws get approved and funded. Nothing reported here says any project has stopped funding pay applications.

What the price tells you is how lenders now rate the job. A buyer paying 85 cents is pricing in a real chance of delay, a renegotiated lease or a late finish. Those are the same events that slow a sub's pay applications, stretch retainage out by months, or turn a change order into a dispute. [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/18/ai-bubble-financing-nvidia-coreweave-circular-deals-credit-mark-minevich/) lists power shortages, construction delays, rising borrowing costs and local opposition as the pressures on data center developers, and the market selloff behind the ING sale points the same way.

## What should a trade sub check this week?

Do this while the job still pays on time. Each protection is harder to use once payments slow.

1. **Get the payment bond.** If the GC posted one, get a copy and read who the surety is, what the notice requirements are, and the deadline to make a claim. If there is no bond, know that before your exposure grows.
2. **Calendar every notice and lien deadline by job.** Preliminary notices and lien filing windows are short and unforgiving. A missed date gives up the right no matter how large the balance is.
3. **Total your retainage by job.** On a long data center schedule, retainage can grow larger than any single pay application. Know the number on each AI campus you're on.
4. **Read your pay clause.** A pay-if-paid clause can shift the owner's funding risk onto you. Know which kind you signed before a payment is late.
5. **Watch for slow pay signs.** Draws approved later than usual, partial payments, or new paperwork demands before release are early signs. Raise them with your GC the first week they happen.
6. **Price new bids for the risk.** On a new data center bid, the owner's financing is part of the job's risk. Ask who is lending, and weigh retainage terms and payment timing the same way you weigh lead times.

## What's still unclear?

ING's bids close today and the results have not been reported. The banks have not said whether they are selling because of a specific project or to cut overall exposure. And none of this means AI data center work is drying up. Oracle reports about $664 billion in remaining performance obligations.

**The takeaway:** a loan price is not a stop-work notice, but lenders are telling you they see more delay risk on these jobs than they did a few months ago. If you have AI data center work in backlog, spend an hour per job this week confirming your bond, your notice and lien dates, your retainage total and your pay clause.
