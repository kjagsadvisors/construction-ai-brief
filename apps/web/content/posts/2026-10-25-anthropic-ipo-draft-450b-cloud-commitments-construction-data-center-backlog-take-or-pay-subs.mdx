Reports on Anthropic's draft IPO filing say the company owes about $450 billion under cloud agreements over the next seven to ten years, and most of it can't be cancelled. For a data center electrical, mechanical or sitework sub, that is evidence the demand behind your backlog is contracted. It does not tell you whether your specific owner can pay.

## What was actually reported?

The Wall Street Journal-sourced figures, as relayed by [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/21/anthropic-has-committed-450-billion-to-cloud-computing-its-draft-ipo-filing-shows/) and others, break out roughly like this:

| Provider | Reported commitment |
|---|---|
| Google Cloud | about $111B |
| AWS | about $110B |
| Microsoft | about $31B |
| Nvidia-based capacity (Nscale, Lambda) | up to $86B |

Other outlets add a separate chip-lease obligation and deals still being negotiated, which pushes some totals past $600 billion. Totals differ because outlets count different things. The draft is also not on SEC EDGAR, so every figure could change.

## Why does a take-or-pay contract matter to a sub?

Take-or-pay means the buyer owes the money whether or not it uses the capacity. Capacity has to sit in a building with power and cooling, and somebody builds that. A contracted tenant is a stronger reason to believe a project gets built than a developer's forecast.

The limit: the contract is between Anthropic and the cloud providers. It does not sit with the developer, the GC or you. Money reaches your pay application only if the entity that owns the site has funded the job.

## What is the catch?

Financing. In mid-October, [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/14/shares-of-oracle-neoclouds-drop-on-coreweave-debt-concerns/) reported that shares of Oracle and neocloud operators fell on concerns about CoreWeave's debt. Several operators build capacity with borrowed money. A strong tenant helps their credit, but it doesn't remove their leverage.

## What should estimators and PMs do differently?

- **Ask who the tenant is.** A named, contracted end user is better than "speculative AI campus."
- **Ask how the project is funded.** Equity, project debt, or a hyperscaler balance sheet carry different risk.
- **Check your subcontract.** Look at payment terms, retainage, and notice requirements before you buy gear with long lead times.
- **Price the schedule.** If the tenant's capacity commitments are time-bound, owners will push for speed. Get acceleration terms in writing.
- **Watch concentration.** One owner or one tenant behind most of your backlog is a risk, however good the headline looks.

## Should a mid-size sub chase this work?

Only if the above checks come back clean. The demand signal is real, but the figures are from reports on a draft, not a signed prospectus. Treat them as direction, not a forecast.

**Takeaway:** Use the news to ask better questions of your next data center owner: who is the tenant, who is funding it, and what happens to your payment if either changes.
