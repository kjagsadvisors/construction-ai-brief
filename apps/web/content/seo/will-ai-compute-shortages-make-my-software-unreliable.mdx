Yes, and the trend line points the wrong way. This isn't a one-quarter glitch anymore: Claude alone logged 164 documented service disruptions by early August 2026, then another 21 incidents in the three weeks that followed, and on September 3 the problem stopped being an Anthropic story — ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok all went down within the same window, three separate companies' infrastructure failing at once. Every AI feature your construction software vendor built on that infrastructure — submittal review, RFI drafting, takeoff assistance — inherits the same fragility, one layer removed.

## Is the compute shortage actually real, or is this a vendor excuse?

It's real, and the money being spent to fix it is the clearest evidence. Since May 2026, Anthropic alone has signed compute agreements worth an estimated $200 billion-plus: a deal to rent the entire AI capacity of SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center — more than 220,000 Nvidia GPUs and 300 megawatts of power, for roughly $1.25 billion a month through 2029 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/06/anthropic-spacex-data-center-capacity.html); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/higher-limits-spacex)) — plus a five-gigawatt Google/Broadcom partnership, a $30 billion Microsoft/Azure capacity deal, and up to five gigawatts from Amazon ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/anthropic-compute-deals-timeline-amazon-google-spacex-microsoft)). None of that spending stopped the outages: Anthropic hit its 164th documented disruption of the year on August 5, 2026, despite the new capacity already coming online ([Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323171/20260805/claude-goes-down-again-71b-compute-deal-cannot-prevent-anthropics-164th-outage.htm)). A company doesn't keep signing nine- and ten-figure infrastructure deals to manage a problem that doesn't exist.

## Has this actually caused outages, or just slower responses?

Both, and it's escalated since early summer. Claude logged 21 separate incidents between August 12 and September 2, 2026 alone, spanning claude.ai, the API, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork, with users hitting the message "Due to unexpected capacity constraints, Claude is unable to respond to your message" ([StatusGator](https://statusgator.com/services/claude/outage-history); [Deployflow](https://deployflow.co/blog/claude-anthropic-outage-protect-claude-infrastructure/)). Then, on September 3, 2026, the pattern jumped from one vendor to three at once: ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok all suffered outages in the same window, each company citing its own internal infrastructure issue rather than a shared external cause ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-confirms-claude-is-down-multiple-models-affected/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/03/chatgpt-claude-grok-outages); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/03/chatgpt-claude-and-grok-all-had-outages-at-the-same-time/5294322); [MacDailyNews](https://macdailynews.com/2026/09/03/major-ai-platforms-go-down-in-unprecedented-simultaneous-outage/)). Three independent companies hitting capacity limits in the same three-hour window is a stronger signal of an industry-wide crunch than any single vendor's status page.

## Does this affect construction-specific software, or just chatbots like Claude and ChatGPT?

It reaches construction tools too, just one layer removed. Submittal-review copilots, RFI drafting assistants, scheduling agents, and vision-based safety monitoring tools don't run their own models — they call inference capacity purchased from Google Cloud, AWS, Azure, or a frontier lab's API, the same pool Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI are all straining to keep up with. A construction software vendor is one account among thousands bidding for that capacity. It has no structural reason to get priority over a frontier lab's biggest direct customers, and every reason to get throttled first when the provider hits a ceiling.

That shows up three ways: the feature runs noticeably slower during your region's peak hours, requests get rate-limited or queued, or the vendor quietly routes traffic to a cheaper, less capable model to keep the lights on without telling you accuracy dropped. The first two are now well-documented at the model-provider level; the third — silent model downgrade — is harder to prove from the outside, which is exactly why it's worth asking your vendor about directly rather than assuming it isn't happening.

## Is the bottleneck still GPUs, or has that changed?

It's shifting, and that matters for how long this lasts. The GPU supply crunch that defined 2024–2025 has eased somewhat as hyperscaler deals like Anthropic's added real chip capacity through 2026. But data center construction itself has become the binding constraint: power transformers now carry roughly 128-week lead times, and grid interconnection queues in major buildout hubs — Northern Virginia, Phoenix, Dallas — run four to seven years ([Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/why-ai-data-center-projects-face-years-of-delays-after-approval); [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/gridlocked-how-power-constraints-are-shaping-the-future-of-data-centers)). Industry trackers now expect 30–50% of the announced 2026 U.S. data center pipeline to slip to 2027 or later, with only a fraction of announced capacity actually under construction today ([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/ai-data-center-power-constraints-2026/)). That's a harder ceiling than GPU supply: a chip fab can add capacity in months, a regional power grid cannot.

## Who's spending to fix this, and is it working?

| Company | Evidence of continued strain | What they've committed to fix it |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic | 164 documented disruptions by Aug 5, 2026; 21 more incidents Aug 12–Sept 2 | $200B+ across SpaceX Colossus 1, Google/Broadcom, Microsoft/Azure, Amazon deals since May 2026 |
| OpenAI | Co-involved in the September 3, 2026 simultaneous outage with Claude and Grok | Continued hyperscaler capacity deals and in-house inference-chip development |
| xAI | Grok went down in the same September 3 window as ChatGPT and Claude | Ongoing Colossus data center buildout in Memphis |
| Industry-wide | 30–50% of the 2026 U.S. data center pipeline projected to slip past 2027 on power/grid delays, not chip supply | Utility-scale interconnection and transformer orders now the critical path, not GPU orders |

The pattern: the shortage has outlasted a full round of nine- and ten-figure infrastructure spending. Every company on this list has an effectively unlimited budget for compute, and none of them has bought its way out of it yet — because the remaining bottleneck (grid power, not silicon) doesn't respond to a purchase order the way a GPU order does.

## What should you actually ask your AI vendor about this?

Three questions, and a vendor's ability to answer them tells you more than any feature demo:

1. **What happens to this feature under load?** A rate limit, a queue, or a silent fallback to a smaller model — ask directly which one, and get it in writing if the answer matters to your workflow.
2. **Does your SLA actually cover the AI feature, or just the core platform?** Most software SLAs guarantee uptime for the base application. Very few extend that guarantee to the AI layer specifically, and the gap is usually in the fine print, not the sales deck.
3. **Do you have a reserved-capacity arrangement with your model provider, or are you buying on the open market?** Vendors serious about AI reliability have already asked their own suppliers this question. If your rep can't answer it, they haven't asked.

## Is this going to get better or worse before it gets better?

Worse before better, and on a longer timeline than it looked like six months ago. Chip supply can catch up in a matter of quarters; the power infrastructure it now depends on cannot. Transformer lead times near 128 weeks and multi-year interconnection queues mean the capacity constraint behind today's rate limits and outages is a build-out problem measured in years, not a vendor problem measured in quarters. Anthropic's Colossus deal alone runs through 2029, and that's the fast-moving part of the fix.

## What this means for how you use AI in your workflow today

None of this is a reason to hold off on AI features that are already saving real time on submittal prep, RFI drafting, or takeoff — the productivity case for those tools doesn't disappear because the infrastructure underneath them is tight. It's a reason to stop treating an AI feature as guaranteed-uptime infrastructure the way you'd treat email or your document repository. Keep anything genuinely time-sensitive — a bid-day deadline, a same-day RFI response with contractual consequences — on a workflow with a human fallback, and reserve the AI feature for the volume of work where a slow afternoon costs you convenience, not a missed deadline.
