To qualify as an electrical sub for data center work, you have to clear a general contractor's prequalification before the project is ever posted: three years of safety data (an EMR below 0.85 and a TRIR below 2.0 are the common benchmarks), bonding capacity sized for electrical packages that commonly run $5 million to $50 million, financial statements with a current WIP schedule, and at least one referenceable mission-critical job. Expect 12 to 24 months to break in. No hyperscaler publishes a single standard, so every number here is a benchmark to confirm against each GC's own packet.

## Is there one prequalification standard for hyperscale electrical work?

No. Each GC runs its own process, usually through its own portal, and renews it every year. Turner uses Vertikal RMS with annual renewal. DPR requires annual prequalification and asks for a certificate of insurance, an EMR worksheet, a W-9, OSHA 300A forms, financial statements, and a WIP schedule at minimum. Whiting-Turner runs its own portal ([THE FRMWRK, "How to Break Into Data Center Electrical Contracting"](https://thefrmwrk.pages.dev/blog/how-to-break-into-data-center-electrical-contracting)).

What the GC is weighing is consistent, though. A study cited by Fieldwire found prime contractors prioritize five things when picking data center subs: expected completion date, financial capacity, health and safety record, previous performance, and the quote ([Fieldwire, September 2026](https://www.fieldwire.com/blog/data-center-construction-subcontractors/)). Price is on that list. It is the last thing checked, after the paperwork decides whether you are approved to bid at all.

## What EMR and TRIR numbers get an electrical sub screened out?

The benchmark most often applied to electrical contractors is an Experience Modification Rate below 0.85 and a Total Recordable Incident Rate below 2.0, checked against three years of OSHA 300 logs. Top-performing electrical firms hold an EMR below 0.70 and a TRIR below 1.0 ([Delta Wye Electric, "5 Ways to Verify Electrical Contractor Safety Records"](https://deltawye.com/evaluating-electrical-contractor-safety-records/)).

Hard cutoffs vary. HighWire, which sells prequalification software, gives a typical pass/fail rule as "EMR exceeding 1.0 will not be considered" and "TRIR above 2.0 will not be considered," and then argues against gating on a single number ([HighWire, January 2026](https://www.highwire.com/blog/contractor-safety-assessments-guide)). So a firm at 0.95 may pass one GC's form and fail another's.

The numbers are also no longer the whole safety review. A sponsored article on Construction Dive, paid for by safety-software vendor Breadcrumb, describes mission-critical owners now asking for digital audit trails, verifiable orientation records for every worker, and site-specific safety plans reviewed by the owner's safety team before anyone mobilizes ([Construction Dive sponsored content, June 2026](https://www.constructiondive.com/spons/what-the-data-center-boom-is-exposing-about-construction-safety/823234/)). That is a vendor's account, so weigh it accordingly, but it matches what a first-time sub should expect: more documentation than a commercial job has ever asked for.

## How much bonding capacity do you need?

Enough to carry a package far larger than standard commercial work. Electrical scope typically runs 40 to 45 percent of total data center construction cost, which puts a 10 MW to 20 MW build at roughly $28 million to $110 million of electrical work ([THE FRMWRK](https://thefrmwrk.pages.dev/blog/how-to-break-into-data-center-electrical-contracting)). Surety broker Grit Insurance says data center subcontracts routinely exceed $5 million, many run north of $25 million, and electrical and mechanical packages commonly range from $5 million to $50 million or more. It also reports that subs are increasingly required to carry their own bonds, and uses a contractor with a $2 million single-job limit as its example of a firm too small to be considered ([Grit Insurance, "How the Data Center Boom Is Reshaping Contractor Bonding"](https://gritinsurance.com/blog/data-center-construction-boom-contractor-bonding)).

On insurance, we could not find a published general liability or umbrella minimum for electrical subs that holds across GCs. Limits are set in each subcontract's insurance exhibit. Ask for that exhibit when you request the prequalification packet, and get your broker's quote on it before you price anything.

## What financial paperwork does the GC want to see?

More than year-end statements. Construction-accounting vendor Jonas lists what data center GCs look at beyond the baseline of project history, bonding, safety record, and insurance certificates: a WIP schedule available on demand, cost-to-complete rolled up across every active project, AIA G702/G703 progress billing, a change order log with approval status, and working capital, debt-to-equity, current ratio, and aggregate bonding capacity. Its warning is blunt: a contractor "underweight on working capital relative to the project size will be quietly passed over" ([Jonas Construction Software](https://www.jonasconstruction.com/data-center-work-is-booming-heres-what-it-actually-takes-to-get-in/)). Jonas sells the software that produces those reports. The list still matches what DPR asks for.

## Do you need BICSI, NETA, or other certifications?

No single certification is required, and "certifications matter less than demonstrated capability on comparable scope" ([THE FRMWRK](https://thefrmwrk.pages.dev/blog/how-to-break-into-data-center-electrical-contracting)). The ones that come up:

- **NETA.** Owners look for NETA-certified technicians for acceptance testing. One engineering firm calls it the most important credential for that work ([NFM Consulting, October 2026](https://nfmconsulting.com/knowledge/data-center-electrical-contractors/)).
- **OSHA 30 and NFPA 70E.** OSHA 30 and site-specific safety training are required on most data center projects.
- **Manufacturer experience.** Schneider Electric, Eaton, Vertiv, and Caterpillar gear, because owners want crews who have installed the specific switchgear, UPS, and generators on the job.
- **BICSI.** Relevant for structured cabling, not power distribution. The RCDD exam application is $510 for members and $725 for nonmembers, and eligibility takes five years of ICT experience, or two years of design experience plus a current BICSI credential ([DC Geeks](https://dcgeeks.com/bicsi-certifications-for-data-center-cabling/); [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/blog/bicsi-rcdd-registered-communications-distribution-designer-exam-guide-2026)). Confirm current fees in the [BICSI RCDD handbook](https://www.bicsi.org/education-certification/certification/rcdd).

If you only do power, skip BICSI and spend the money on NETA testing capability.

## Qualification checklist at a glance

| Requirement | Common benchmark | Who sets it | Our read |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMR | Below 0.85. Some forms cut off at 1.0 | Each GC or owner | The first screen. Pull yours today |
| TRIR | Below 2.0, strongest firms below 1.0 | Each GC or owner | Checked with three years of OSHA 300 logs |
| Bonding capacity | Sized for $5M to $50M+ packages | Your surety | The slowest item to fix after safety |
| Financials | Statements, WIP schedule, current ratio, working capital | Each GC | Needs to be on demand, not month-end |
| Insurance | Per the subcontract's insurance exhibit | Owner and GC | No reliable published minimum. Get the exhibit |
| Comparable experience | Referenceable mission-critical job | GC preconstruction | A small hospital job beats a big office job |
| Certifications | NETA, OSHA 30, NFPA 70E | Owner specs | BICSI only if you pull cable |

## How do you get the first job with no data center experience?

Start with a project you can use as a reference. A smaller mission-critical job, such as a hospital emergency department or a telecom facility, is worth more than a larger commercial job with no critical systems. Other ways in: second-tier work under an electrical sub that is already on the list, or operations work inside a running colocation or enterprise facility, such as tenant improvements, PDU replacement, and commissioning support ([THE FRMWRK](https://thefrmwrk.pages.dev/blog/how-to-break-into-data-center-electrical-contracting)).

Then get known before the bid. GCs review their preferred vendor list 90 to 120 days ahead of bid, and subs that win have typically been in conversation for 60 to 120 days before the project is public. Target preconstruction managers and chief estimators, and ask commissioning agents and MEP coordinators for introductions. For the technical scope that separates this from commercial work, see [our reporting on the short list of qualified electrical and mechanical subs](/posts/2026-06-30-data-center-electrical-credentials-725-billion).

## How long does it take, and is the work still there?

Breaking in typically takes 12 to 24 months, and "there is no fast path here" ([THE FRMWRK](https://thefrmwrk.pages.dev/blog/how-to-break-into-data-center-electrical-contracting)). That lines up with how owners plan: leading programs share 12 to 24-month lookahead pipelines with their core MEP partners, and cost consultancy Linesight notes that only a limited number of subs have the financial strength and bonding capacity for hyperscale work ([Linesight, April 2026](https://www.linesight.com/insights/optimising-mep-subcontractor-procurement-for-hyperscale-data-centre-construction/)).

The demand gap is real. In ABC's October survey, contractors with data center work reported 11.5 months of backlog, up from 11.3 in September, while the 88% of contractors without it fell to 7.6 months from 8.3 ([Construction Dive, November 10, 2026](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/construction-backlog-decline-october-2026/874316/); [BD+C](https://www.bdcnetwork.com/building-team/contractors/article/55417829/the-average-us-contractor-has-81-months-worth-of-construction-work-in-the-pipeline-as-of-october-2026)).

It also carries risks commercial work does not. Read the subcontract for [late-delivery penalties that flow down to subs](/posts/2026-10-26-ai-data-center-5m-daily-late-penalties-construction-sub-delay-clauses-ld-caps) and for [cancellation terms on long-lead gear](/posts/2026-11-14-softbank-3-7b-writeoff-cancelled-power-equipment-deposits-construction-electrical-mech-sub-long-lead-cancellation-terms), and watch [how much of your backlog rides on one owner type](/posts/2026-10-05-data-center-construction-spending-85b-vs-factories-168b-sub-backlog-concentration).

## What should a firm do first?

Pull your EMR and TRIR. If both are at benchmark, the rest is paperwork, bonding, and relationships, measured in months. If they are not, fix the safety program before you spend on certifications or a bigger bond line, because a failing safety record wastes both. Then request prequalification packets from two or three GCs building in your market and read their actual cutoffs.
