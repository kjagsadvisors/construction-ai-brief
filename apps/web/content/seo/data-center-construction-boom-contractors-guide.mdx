US data center construction spending crossed $50 billion a year in 2026, and hyperscaler capital spending commitments of roughly $725 billion this year mean that number keeps climbing. But the backlog headline hides a harder market underneath it: transformer lead times now stretch up to four years, two major state markets just restricted new projects, and the trades doing the actual electrical and mechanical work are running well short of the people needed to build it. This is a real, durable opportunity for contractors — it is not a market you back into without the credentials, patience, and equipment relationships to survive the ramp.

This guide is for the GC ops lead deciding whether to bid, and the electrical or mechanical sub owner deciding whether to invest in the certifications this work requires. It links out to our reporting on the specific pieces — state policy, grid connections, workforce credentials — as they develop.

## How big is the data center construction boom, really?

US data center construction spending eclipsed $50 billion annually in 2026, a pace the market has never sustained before ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-01/us-construction-spending-on-data-centers-eclipses-50-billion)). That spending is downstream of a bigger number: Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and Meta collectively confirmed roughly $725 billion in combined 2026 capital expenditure in their Q1 earnings, up 77% from about $410 billion in 2025 ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/big-techs-ai-spending-plans-reach-725-billion)). Moody's, reviewing the same earnings, raised its own forecast to $785 billion for the year and projects hyperscaler infrastructure spending will approach $1 trillion annually by 2027 ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/moodys-hyperscaler-capex-forecasts-marked-up-by-85bn-to-close-in-on-1trn-by-2027/)).

That capital is flowing directly into construction management fees, not just steel and concrete. ENR's 2026 Top 100 Professional Services Firms rankings show combined CM/PM-for-fee revenue hit $36.9 billion in 2025, up 31.2% from the year before — roughly $8.8 billion in new revenue in a single year, with the telecommunications and data center sector growing 31.3% and nearly 87% since 2023 ([ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/63180-enr-2026-top-professional-services-firms-deepen-depth-of-services)). Turner Construction reported record 2025 revenue with data center work as the primary driver. [The full breakdown of where those fees are landing is here](/posts/2026-06-23-ai-data-center-boom-cm-pm-fees-enr-2026).

## What's actually slowing data center projects down?

Not permits, mostly. On June 18, 2026, FERC ordered six regional grid operators — PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO New England, and NYISO, serving roughly 200 million Americans — to justify or reform how they connect large loads like data centers to the grid, with filings due within 60 days ([FERC](https://www.ferc.gov/news-events/news/ferc-launches-aggressive-targeted-action-speed-large-load-integration)). [We covered what that order does and doesn't fix](/posts/2026-06-22-ferc-data-center-grid-connections) — and the honest answer is it clears interconnection paperwork, not equipment shortages.

The binding constraint is transformers. Lead times on the large power units data centers require have extended to as long as four years for specialty units ([PV Magazine](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/)). More broadly, projects requiring new power supply and transmission infrastructure now stretch five to eight years from decision to energization, compared with one to three years before the pandemic ([SWK Technologies](https://www.swktech.com/data-center-projects-forecasting-risk/)). If your project doesn't have confirmed delivery dates on switchgear and transformers, that's the actual critical path — not the tariff filing at FERC.

## What does data center electrical and mechanical work actually require?

More than a standard commercial scope. Hyperscaler facilities run at N+1 or 2N power redundancy — every major component has a hot standby, commissioned to switchover tolerances measured in milliseconds — and cooling has moved from conventional CRAC units to precision in-row cooling, rear-door heat exchangers, and direct liquid cooling manifolds for the highest-density GPU racks. Acceptance testing (integrated systems testing, or IST) simulates power and cooling failures on an energized facility with the construction team present, not a punch-list walkthrough after the fact. [We wrote a full breakdown of the qualification requirements — Tier III/IV project history, NFPA 70E arc flash documentation, DART safety floors, and the 18-to-24-month ramp to a first hyperscaler award](/posts/2026-06-30-data-center-electrical-credentials-725-billion) — for subs deciding whether to pursue this work.

## How much do data center construction jobs pay, and is the labor shortage real?

It's real, and it's the most binding constraint after equipment. One labor-market analysis puts total 2026 data center construction hiring need at up to 499,000 workers, with 41% of the existing skilled-trades workforce eligible to retire by 2031 ([iRecruit](https://www.irecruit.co/insights/data-center-construction-labor-market-report)). Electricians are the sharpest pinch point — Fortune reported the sector needs 300,000-plus electricians against roughly 20,000 retiring annually, with too few apprentices entering the pipeline to close the gap ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/02/ai-data-centers-electrician-shortage-gen-z-training-careers/)).

That shortage is showing up in pay. Data center-specific electrician roles are commonly reported in the $72,000–$110,000 range, journeyman wireman packages at strong union locals clear $120,000-plus with overtime, and specialized roles in the hottest markets have been reported as high as $260,000 ([Cowboy State Daily](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2026/04/05/us-data-centers-drive-explosion-in-pay-for-electricians-some-as-much-as-260-000/)). First-year apprentices typically start around $42,000 while attending classes two nights a week — a fast, low-barrier entry point relative to the pay ceiling above it. Meta's $115 million America's Workforce Academy, launched in Indiana, Louisiana, Ohio, and Texas, is one direct route into that pipeline with guaranteed job placement.

## How much does it cost to build a data center in 2026?

It depends heavily on tier and region, but the range has widened as AI-density facilities pull the top end up. Enterprise Tier II space runs roughly $600–$750 per square foot, Tier III $750–$950, and Tier IV or hyperscale $950–$1,100-plus — while AI-optimized facilities with liquid cooling are running $1,100–$1,500-plus per square foot ([Buildermuse](https://buildermuse.com/economy/data-center-construction-costs-per-square-foot/)). Shell construction is a comparatively modest $250–$400 per square foot; it's the mechanical, electrical, and cooling fit-out that adds the other $800–$1,500. Northern Virginia remains the most expensive market in the country, driven by labor competition and utility connection fees, while Ohio and Phoenix run 15–20% cheaper on labor alone.

## Is the data center boom a bubble that could leave contractors overextended?

The demand side doesn't currently support that read — capex commitments are rising, not falling, and Moody's revised its forecast up in the same quarter some analysts were flagging bubble concerns. The real risk is narrower and more specific to construction: facilities are designed to stand for 25 to 30 years, and the mechanical and electrical infrastructure going in today is sized for 2026 GPU power density. [A neuromorphic-computing startup raised $475 million in June on the premise that AI compute could eventually need up to 1,000 times less power per operation](/posts/2026-06-26-unconventional-ai-data-center-power-design) — a long shot, not a near-term threat, but a reminder that facilities with rigid, fixed-load electrical and cooling design age worse than ones built with modular switchgear and liquid-cooling rough-ins from day one.

The more immediate risk is commercial, not technological: owner-furnished equipment arrangements shift a contractor's exposure from equipment margin into labor productivity, and projects needing new transmission infrastructure now run five to eight years from decision to energization — schedule risk that shows up as cash-flow and bonding exposure long before any compute bubble would ([SWK Technologies](https://www.swktech.com/data-center-projects-forecasting-risk/)). Peak crews on the largest campuses run 4,000 to 5,000 workers, which is a management and logistics problem most GCs haven't scaled to before.

## How do you get on a hyperscaler's preferred contractor list?

It's a documentation exercise more than a bid exercise. Hyperscaler procurement teams typically screen for prior Uptime Institute Tier III or higher project experience, written NFPA 70E arc flash hazard analysis and PPE programs for every technician, DART safety rates below program-specific floors, and — for some federal or defense-adjacent workloads — a background-checked or ITAR-cleared workforce. Firms that already have this in place made the investment around 2024 and are largely booked. For everyone else, the realistic path is 18 to 24 months: build the credential trail as a second-tier sub under a qualified GC, then bid direct once the documentation and project history exist. ## The honest read

This is not a market you drift into on a slow quarter. The capital is real and still accelerating, but the binding constraints — transformers, qualified crews, state-level permitting whiplash — mean the firms winning this work today mostly made the decision to pursue it two years ago. If your shop has clean safety records, capacity in electrical or mechanical disciplines, and the patience for an 18-to-24-month ramp before the first hyperscaler-tier award, the arithmetic favors starting now. If you're evaluating this quarter-to-quarter looking for fast backlog, this is the wrong market — the paperwork and qualification runway alone will outlast most short-term sourcing decisions. For the paperwork itself, once you're on a data center job, [AI is already changing how RFIs, submittals, and change orders move through the log](/seo/ai-construction-rfi-submittal-change-order-guide) — worth understanding before your first big campus award, not after.
