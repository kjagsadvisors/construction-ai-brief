No AI estimating tool on the market is free at the volume a small contractor actually bids. STACK, Kreo, Togal.AI, and Buildxact all advertise a free trial or a free tier, but every one of them caps the thing you need most once you're past a single test project — project count, measurements, AI takeoff, or a 7-to-14-day trial clock. The honest answer: use the free offers to prove the workflow on one job, then expect to pay roughly $100 to $300 per user per month once you want to run it on real bids.

That gap matters because free-sounding marketing is exactly where a small GC or sub loses time deciding. Here's what each free offer actually gives you, where a general AI chatbot fills in for nothing, and what it costs the moment you outgrow the trial.

## What's actually free versus a disguised trial?

Two different things get marketed as "free" in this category, and they're not the same offer:

1. **A capped free tier that never expires.** STACK's free plan falls here — one active project per month, up to 300 plan pages, and 50 measurements per project, indefinitely. It leaves out AI Takeoffs and the assembly library, so it works for a small job or a test, not a pipeline of bids.
2. **A time-boxed free trial with full features.** Kreo runs a 7-day trial on its Essential, Pro, and Business plans. Buildxact is generally listed with a 14-day trial, though third-party listings disagree on its details. Togal.AI's pricing page lists no trial; its "Try Now" button leads to a demo booking.

Neither is "free software" in the way a contractor might hope. The capped tier is free forever but too thin to bid with. The full-featured trial is real but temporary — useful for testing accuracy on your own drawings, not for running your actual pipeline of bids.

## Which free tiers or trials are actually worth using?

| Tool | Free offer | What it actually gives you | What you hit next | Paid price if you keep it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **STACK** | Free plan, no expiration | 1 active project/month, 300 plan pages, 50 measurements/project, core takeoff | No AI Takeoffs, no assembly library, one project a month | $299/user/month (Premium), $349 (Pro), billed annually |
| **Kreo** | 7-day free trial | Paid-plan features for a week | Trial expires; no permanent free plan | $100/user/month (Essential), $175 (Pro), $250 (Business, 5 seats), billed annually |
| **Togal.AI** | Demo booking (no trial listed on pricing page) | Walkthrough of vision-based takeoff | No self-serve free access | $299/user/month (Growth, billed annually) |
| **Buildxact** | ~14-day free trial (per third-party listings) | Estimating and project tools, 1 user | Trial expires; annual billing needs a 12-month commitment | Listings vary: roughly $149-$199/month entry, up to $599 at the top tier |
| **ChatGPT / Claude (general)** | Free account, no trial clock | Spec parsing, scope summaries, rough quantity drafts from a description | Can't reliably read geometry off an actual drawing sheet | Free tier is usable indefinitely for this narrower use |
| **Status quo (manual takeoff, spreadsheet)** | Always free | Full human judgment on every quantity | Slowest option; doesn't scale with more bids | No new spend |

The pattern: the fuller the free offer, the shorter the clock. STACK's tier never expires because it's missing AI takeoff and the assembly library, and it caps you at one project a month. Kreo and Buildxact give you the paid product for a week or two, long enough to judge it on one job before you have to decide.

## Can I just use ChatGPT or Claude instead of paying for any of this?

For part of the job, yes, and it costs nothing. A general AI chatbot is genuinely useful, free, for reading a spec section and summarizing scope, drafting a rough quantity list from a written description, or turning a messy sub quote into a cleaner comparison table you build yourself. None of that requires a takeoff-specific subscription.

What it can't do is replace takeoff software's core function: detecting walls, doors, and repeated symbols directly off a scanned or CAD drawing sheet and turning that into a measured quantity. That's a computer-vision problem the dedicated tools are purpose-built for and general chat models aren't. If your estimating bottleneck is reading drawings, a free chatbot won't close that gap — if it's reading and summarizing text-heavy specs, it already might. [Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5), released in mid-2026, held its predecessor's price while closing most of the performance gap to its flagship model — one more reason the text-and-spec side of this work keeps getting cheaper to automate in-house, even as drawing takeoff stays a paid, specialized category.

## Why hasn't a real free tier shown up yet?

Because the adoption curve doesn't need one. A [ServiceTitan-commissioned survey of more than 1,000 commercial contractors](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/30/3264650/0/en/ServiceTitan-Report-Finds-AI-Adoption-More-Than-Doubles-Among-Commercial-Contractors-as-Firms-Turn-to-Technology-to-Navigate-Cost-Pressures.html), fielded in January 2026, found 38% now report measurable results from AI, more than double the 17% who said so a year earlier — and cost estimating is the single leading use case, named by 24% of respondents. A separate [Houzz survey published mid-2026](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/102644-ai-use-jumps-among-construction-firms-houzz-survey-finds) puts overall AI tool usage among construction firms even higher, at 52%, up 20 points from the year before. Vendors selling into a market that's adopting this fast have little incentive to give away the paid tier; the free trial only needs to be generous enough to convert a contractor who's already decided to try AI takeoff, not to win over a skeptic.

That adoption pressure is also why the category won't get cheaper from labor economics alone. The [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects cost estimator employment will decline 4% from 2024 to 2034](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/cost-estimators.htm), naming estimating software productivity as the direct cause — which means the tools are already paying for themselves in headcount terms for the firms buying them, free tier or not.

## What should a small contractor actually pilot first?

Start with STACK's free plan, since it costs nothing and never expires: run one recent job through it side by side with your estimator's manual takeoff, and time both. If you want to test AI takeoff itself, Kreo's 7-day trial is the cheapest next step, with paid plans from $100 per user per month. If Togal.AI's speed or Buildxact's bundled project-management features matter more, budget for $150-$300 a month per user before you invest trial time in them.

Whatever you pick, don't skip the comparison step. Free trial numbers are vendor-reported on the vendor's demo drawings; run it on your own messiest recent set — the one with hand-marked revisions or a non-standard symbol — before deciding the software is faster than your estimator.
