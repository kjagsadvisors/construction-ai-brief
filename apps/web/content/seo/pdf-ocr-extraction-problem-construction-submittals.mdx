AI struggles with submittal PDFs because submittal review is an extraction problem before it is a spec-comparison problem. Typed spec sections and clean cut sheets now extract well and cheaply. Scanned faxes, hand-marked shop drawings, and anything whose meaning sits in a drawing or a photo still fail often enough that a person has to check the read. As of November 2026 the text side is close to a commodity, and the drawing and photo side is where the errors are.

## What goes wrong when AI tries to read a submittal PDF?

Two different things fail, for different reasons.

The first is raw text extraction on a bad scan: fax artifacts, skewed pages, a review stamp sitting on top of the product table, a fifth-generation photocopy of a cut sheet. OCR degrades here the way it always has. Characters come out wrong, rows go missing, and a table reads as a paragraph.

The second is more common and has nothing to do with legibility. A cut sheet's product table can scan perfectly and still be misread if the tool doesn't know that column three is voltage and column five is a footnote. That is the gap between OCR, which turns pixels into text, and structured extraction, which returns labeled fields and table cells that a comparison step can reason over. A submittal check built on unstructured text will misfire on a document that scanned cleanly.

## Why do drawings and photos extract so much worse than spec text?

Because the information is in the picture, not in the words. Dimension strings, revision clouds, symbol layers, and notes that only make sense relative to where they sit on the sheet do not survive a text-only read.

The best public evidence arrived on November 10, when Google DeepMind and Artificial Analysis released the Multimodal Knowledge Agent benchmark: 200 expert-built tasks across 10 sectors, construction among them, where PDFs such as floor plans, elevations, and piping schematics make up 43% of the input files. The top agent satisfied 56.6% of the harder grading criteria and 73.3% of all criteria ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/multimodal-knowledge-agent-benchmark/); [technical report](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/papers/mka_tech_paper.pdf)).

The detail that matters for submittals is what happened when the researchers took away the agent's ability to look at images. Across the benchmark, one model's score on the harder criteria fell 5.94 points. On a construction task built on field photos inside a road-inspection PDF, it fell from 86.7% to 14.3%: the agent ran OCR and image-processing scripts, concluded nothing was missing, and failed every check tied to the undocumented defects. A tool that only reads the text layer of your PDF will do the same thing with a marked-up shop drawing. We covered the estimating side of that result in [our breakdown of the benchmark](/posts/2026-11-11-deepmind-mka-benchmark-ai-agents-drawings-57-percent-construction-estimator-precon-drawing-review-vision-test).

Trunk Tools built its Cortex platform for this half of the problem, and says it took four years of development on live projects to get there. Its launch release describes a 20-sheet bulletin reviewed with written change narratives in under five minutes, including changes the architect didn't cloud ([American Surveyor reprint of the release](https://amerisurv.com/2026/06/17/trunk-tools-launches-cortex-to-tackle-constructions-hardest-ai-problem-drawings/); [our Cortex coverage](/posts/2026-06-21-trunk-tools-cortex-construction-drawings-ai)). That is a vendor claim, not an independent test.

## What has shipped to fix the extraction layer?

Three kinds of product, and they solve different pieces.

**Structure-aware OCR.** Mistral released OCR 4 on June 23. It returns text with layout, classifies each block by type (titles, tables, equations, signatures), attaches confidence scores per page and per word, and reads PDF, DOC, PPT, and OpenDocument files. Mistral reports human evaluators preferred it over the other systems tested at an average 72% win rate, and says itself that the public benchmark scores it cites are directional ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/); [our OCR 4 submittal workflow](/posts/2026-06-28-mistral-ocr-4-submittal-extraction-construction)). OCR 4.1, dated July 16 and listed as generally available, adds paragraph-level bounding boxes and block-level confidence scores ([Mistral docs](https://docs.mistral.ai/models/ocr-4-1)).

**Field and checklist models.** This category is new since our last update. Cloudflare's Clairic, in preview, returns only the fields you name, each with a confidence value, and returns null when the document doesn't determine the answer. It takes up to four images per request ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai/models/@cf/cloudflare/clairic/); [our Clairic test plan](/posts/2026-10-22-cloudflare-clairic-extraction-model-construction-submittal-invoice-field-capture-test)). Google's Gemini Decisions API, announced November 11, scores up to 1,000 yes/no, multiple-choice, or rubric questions against one input of up to 256,000 tokens, with a confidence score on each answer ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/)). A spec section is already a list of yes/no requirements, so the fit is obvious. The limit is that you get scores, and Google's announcement describes no written reasons or page references, which a reviewer needs before rejecting a package. [Our write-up covers that catch](/posts/2026-11-13-google-gemini-decisions-api-1000-questions-one-pdf-construction-trade-sub-submittal-spec-compliance-checklist). Both are developer building blocks, not products a contractor buys.

**Construction-specific agents.** Trunk Tools says TrunkSubmittal is embedded in Procore and Autodesk Forma as a workflow step and is 71% faster than before, per its [June release](https://amerisurv.com/2026/06/17/trunk-tools-launches-cortex-to-tackle-constructions-hardest-ai-problem-drawings/). Its product page lists the submittal types it supports: product data, test reports, certificates, O&M manuals, sustainability and LEED, warranties, and qualifications ([Trunk Tools](https://trunktools.com/trunksubmittal/)). Shop drawings are not on that list. One thing to ask before you count on the Procore side: ENR reported in October 2025 that Procore had denied Trunk Tools access to its API, with Trunk Tools promising customers a fix by December 1 of that year ([ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/61789-trunk-tools-removed-from-procore-api-access-groundbreak-attendance-refunded)). Trunk Tools' 2026 materials describe a working Procore integration. We have not seen independent reporting on how it connects today, so ask the vendor to show it running on your account.

## How do the extraction approaches compare?

| Approach | What it handles well | Where it still breaks | List price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structure-aware OCR (Mistral OCR 4.1)** | Spec sections, product data, O&M manuals; tables and blocks with confidence scores; self-hosted option for enterprise customers | Drawings and photos where the meaning is spatial; you still build the spec comparison yourself | $4 per 1,000 pages; $2 batch; $5 per 1,000 annotated pages |
| **Field extraction (Cloudflare Clairic)** | Pulling named values such as manufacturer, model number, dates, and amounts; empty answer instead of a guess | Preview status; four images per request; Cloudflare doesn't document how the confidence values are calibrated | $0.10 per million input tokens |
| **Checklist scoring (Gemini Decisions API)** | Up to 1,000 yes/no spec requirements against one PDF in one request | No reasons or page references described; 256,000-token input cap; no published construction accuracy | $0.10 per million input tokens |
| **Construction agents (Trunk Tools)** | Text submittal types inside Procore or Autodesk; separate drawing review product | Judgment calls on substitutions; performance figures are vendor-reported | Subscription, not published |
| **Manual review** | Everything, eventually | Volume: Trunk Tools puts a proper manual first pass at 45 to 60 minutes or more | Coordinator and PE hours |

## Is fixing extraction worth it, or is this already solved?

For text submittals, it is close to solved and cheap, and the remaining question is whether the review on top of it pays. The field numbers say it does at volume. On Gilbane projects, Trunk Tools found 72% of first-pass submittals non-compliant and only 3% fully compliant, and Gilbane said catching issues early shortened cycle times by nearly 50% ([ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/61435-gilbane-rolls-out-trunk-tools-ai-agents-across-its-jobsites)). ENR also reported Cleveland Construction cutting submittal review from roughly two hours to under 10 minutes and average cycle time from 55 days to 13 ([ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/63178-trunk-tools-launches-cortex-ai-platform-to-interpret-construction-drawings)). Trunk Tools' own release says its median user's cycle time dropped 74% and submittals stuck past 60 days fell from 42% to 2%. One vendor puts a rejected submittal at $805 on average plus two to four weeks ([BuildSync](https://buildsync.ai/resources/the-true-cost-of-a-rejected-submittal-805-each)). Every one of those figures comes from a vendor or its customer, so read them as direction, not as an audit.

For shop drawings, marked-up sheets, and photo-based submittals, it is not solved. A 57% score on hard visual checks is enough to draft a review and not enough to sign one.

If your log is small and a coordinator with a clean template keeps pace, extraction isn't costing you enough to chase. If it runs past 150 items, run this test before you trust any tool: take one Division 22 or 23 package that includes a scanned page and a hand-marked page, and check two things. Does the output keep the product table separate from its footnotes? And does the tool catch the hand markup, or only the typed text underneath it? If it fails either, the spec comparison downstream will not be reliable.
