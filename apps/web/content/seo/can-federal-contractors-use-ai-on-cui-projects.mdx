Not on the standard commercial app — but the list of authorized alternatives got a lot longer this year, and the compliance deadline that was supposed to force the issue just got suspended. Under CMMC 2.0, any AI tool that processes Controlled Unclassified Information must run inside an environment authorized to FedRAMP High or equivalent. Standard ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini accounts don't qualify. But government-cloud versions of all three now do (or nearly do), and the November 10, 2026 deadline that was going to make third-party CMMC audits mandatory has been suspended indefinitely. Here's the current state as of mid-September 2026.

## What is CUI, and why does it block standard AI tools?

Controlled Unclassified Information is government information that isn't classified but still requires safeguarding under federal law, regulation, or agency policy. On a construction project it commonly shows up as force-protection and anti-terrorism design details, SCIF or restricted-facility specifications, ITAR-controlled building systems, and sometimes the RFI or scope-of-work text itself if it references those details.

CMMC 2.0 requires any system that touches CUI to meet NIST SP 800-171, a set of 110 security requirements across 14 control families. A standard ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini prompt sends your text to the vendor's ordinary commercial cloud infrastructure, which hasn't been assessed against those controls. That transmission is the violation — the same document processed through an authorized, FedRAMP High government-cloud environment is fine.

## Which AI tools are actually authorized for CUI right now?

This list is longer than it was two months ago — every major model family now has a government-cloud path, though they're not equally mature:

- **Azure OpenAI Service, inside Azure Government** — holds FedRAMP High and DoD IL4/IL5 authorization for GPT-4o and related models, per Microsoft's compliance documentation. Still the most established path.
- **Claude for Government / Claude in Amazon Bedrock (AWS GovCloud)** — Anthropic's Claude for Government product runs inside a FedRAMP High-authorized environment, and Claude via Bedrock in GovCloud carries FedRAMP High / DoD IL4-5 authorization separately. Anthropic expanded the government offering to include Claude Code and Claude Cowork in July 2026.
- **Gemini for Government** — reached FedRAMP High authorization in January 2026 through Google's Assured Workloads boundary on Vertex AI, closing the gap with Azure and AWS.
- **ChatGPT Enterprise / OpenAI API for government** — authorized at **FedRAMP Moderate**, not High, as of mid-2026. GSA's OneGov agreement extending discounted ChatGPT access to agencies takes effect October 1, 2026. Moderate can cover some internal civilian workloads, but treat it as insufficient for CUI until your contracting officer confirms otherwise.
- **Standard commercial ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini accounts** — still not authorized for CUI, full stop. The website you'd sign up on is not the same environment as any of the products above.

## What happened to CMMC Phase 2 — and what still applies?

This is the biggest change since our last update. On July 13, 2026, the Department of War suspended CMMC Phase 2's mandatory third-party (C3PAO) certification requirement — the piece that was going to hit most Defense Industrial Base contracts on November 10, 2026 — and stood up a CMMC Reform Task Force to review the program, citing compliance cost and bureaucratic burden. On September 3, 2026, that suspension stopped being a policy memo and became binding regulation: Class Deviation 2026-O0025, Revision 3, writes it directly into DFARS text, instructing contracting officers to strip third-party assessment requirements from solicitations. It stays in force until formally rescinded — no new certification deadline has been announced.

That does **not** mean CUI handling got easier. Phase 1 requirements are fully intact: NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment, DFARS 252.204-7012, SPRS score postings, and annual affirmations still apply on every covered contract. What's gone, for now, is the mandatory outside audit — not the underlying obligation to keep CUI out of unauthorized AI tools. An assessor or contracting officer reviewing your self-assessment can still flag an uncontrolled ChatGPT or Claude workflow touching CUI as a finding.

Layer one more thing onto this: a bipartisan bill introduced in the House on September 3, 2026 — the Stop Rogue AI Act — would require federal contractors to inventory and log every AI agent running on their systems as a condition of winning new work. It hasn't passed and wouldn't take effect for at least a year even if it did, but [we covered what it would mean for GCs bidding USACE, NAVFAC, GSA, or VA work here](/posts/2026-09-06-stop-rogue-ai-act-federal-construction-contractor-compliance) — it's the next compliance layer to watch, not this one.

## Where does GSA's AI clause stand?

Unchanged in status, still moving: GSA's revised draft clause, GSAR 552.239-7001 ("Basic Safeguarding of Data within Large Language Model Artificial Intelligence Systems"), published June 17, 2026, with the public comment period closing August 3, 2026. As of this writing, GSA has not issued a final rule — contractor groups pushed back through the comment period, arguing the disclosure and flowdown requirements are still too broad even in the narrowed version. It would apply to GSA's government-wide vehicles — Federal Supply Schedule, GWACs, and OASIS+ — so a construction or design-build firm holding or bidding work under those vehicles should still expect this to land, just with no confirmed date yet.

## What should a federal GC or sub actually do this quarter?

1. **Map which of your workflows touch CUI.** RFIs referencing restricted design data, submittal packages for ITAR-controlled systems, schedules tied to sensitive facility phasing — write the list down. Everything not on it is fair game for standard commercial AI tools today.
2. **Check what foundation model powers every AI feature already in your stack.** A vendor's document-review or spec-search feature may be routing CUI-adjacent content through a non-authorized API without your team realizing it — [the same question we raised after the June 2026 Anthropic model shutdown](/posts/2026-06-20-anthropic-model-shutdown-vendor-risk-construction) applies here.
3. **Don't stand down on NIST SP 800-171 because Phase 2 is suspended.** Self-assessment, SPRS scores, and annual affirmations are still contractual obligations, and an uncontrolled AI workflow is still a finding on those.
4. **If you need CUI-eligible AI now, evaluate Claude for Government or Gemini for Government alongside Azure OpenAI** — the field of FedRAMP High options is wider than it was two months ago.
5. **Watch two things, not one:** whether GSA finalizes 552.239-7001, and whether the CMMC Reform Task Force's review (delivered to the DoW CIO on or around September 13, 2026) reinstates a certification deadline in a different form.
