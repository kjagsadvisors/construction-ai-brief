No, not on the standard commercial app. A federal contractor can put Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) into an AI tool only when that tool runs in a cloud environment meeting at least the FedRAMP Moderate-equivalent bar in DFARS 252.204-7012, or on a model hosted inside the contractor's own compliant systems. Government-cloud versions of GPT, Claude, and Gemini models now exist at FedRAMP High; the ordinary ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini accounts your office signed up for are not those environments.

This page was re-verified on November 16, 2026. Three things changed since our September version: GSA's AI clause went final, a US-built open-weight model shipped, and we corrected how we describe the FedRAMP floor (details below).

## What is CUI, and why does it block standard AI tools?

CUI is government information that isn't classified but still has to be safeguarded. On a construction project it usually shows up as force-protection and anti-terrorism design details, restricted-facility specifications, export-controlled building systems, and sometimes the RFI or scope text that references those details.

Defense contracts carrying DFARS 252.204-7012 require NIST SP 800-171 protections on any system that handles that data. A prompt typed into a standard chatbot sends the text to the vendor's commercial cloud, which is an outside system you haven't documented or verified. That transmission is the problem. The same paragraph run through an authorized government-cloud tenant is fine.

## Does CUI require FedRAMP High or FedRAMP Moderate?

**Correction to our earlier version:** we previously wrote that CUI needs FedRAMP High. The clause text sets a lower floor. Per [Secureframe's summary of the rule](https://secureframe.com/blog/fedramp-equivalency-cmmc), DFARS 252.204-7012 requires an outside cloud provider to meet security "equivalent to" the FedRAMP Moderate baseline, and a December 21, 2023 DoD CIO memo defines equivalency as 100% compliance with that baseline, validated by a FedRAMP-recognized assessor, with no self-attestation. The provider also has to comply with the clause's paragraphs (c) through (g) on incident reporting and forensics.

Two practical consequences:

- **High clears the bar easily.** FedRAMP High and DoD IL4/IL5 offerings are the low-argument choice, which is why most primes default to them.
- **Moderate is not automatic.** FedRAMP status belongs to a specific offering and boundary, not a brand. And for export-controlled data, [one compliance analysis](https://vdf.ai/blog/cmmc-ai-chatgpt-copilot-cui-defense-contractors/) puts it plainly: "FedRAMP Moderate alone does not settle it."

## Which AI tools can handle CUI right now?

| Option | Authorization we could verify | Usable for CUI? | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini accounts (free, paid, or business) | None for this purpose | No | The data rule, not the price tier, is what excludes them |
| Azure OpenAI Service in Azure Government | FedRAMP High; DoD IL4 and IL5 provisional authorization | Yes | You build and document it as a component of your own system |
| Claude in Amazon Bedrock (AWS GovCloud) | Approved for FedRAMP High and DoD IL4/IL5 workloads | Yes | API-level service; needs a GovCloud account and someone to set it up |
| Claude for Government | Runs inside a FedRAMP High boundary; includes Chat, Cowork, and Code in the desktop app | Yes, for eligible buyers | Anthropic's documentation is written for agency deployments; a contractor should confirm eligibility directly |
| Gemini for Government | Google's deployment guidance covers FedRAMP High and DoD IL4 | Yes | Must be deployed inside an Assured Workloads folder; some IL5 connector authorizations are pending |
| ChatGPT Enterprise / OpenAI API (OpenAI's FedRAMP environment) | FedRAMP 20x Class C (Moderate), announced April 27, 2026 | Conditional | Only a workspace OpenAI confirms in writing is inside that boundary, with the clause terms in your contract |
| OpenAI models via AWS GovCloud Bedrock | Listed at FedRAMP High and IL5 | Yes | Same API-level setup burden as Claude in Bedrock |
| Self-hosted open-weight model | None needed if it stays inside your own compliant boundary | Yes | Hardware cost, and you own the security of the box |

Authorization details are from [Microsoft](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/azuregov/azure-openai-fedramp-high-for-government/), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-in-amazon-bedrock-fedramp-high), [Claude for Government documentation](https://claude.com/docs/government/security/security-and-data-handling.md), [Google](https://docs.cloud.google.com/docs/security/compliance/deploy-gemini-gov), and [Knox Systems' review of OpenAI's FedRAMP status](https://knoxsystems.com/resources/chatgpt-fedramp). Knox notes there is no FedRAMP High path yet for OpenAI's managed ChatGPT product, with FedRAMP's high-impact pilot planned for the first half of fiscal 2027.

Our read: for a mid-size GC or sub without an IT department, none of these is a sign-up-and-go product. The realistic paths are a managed service provider that stands up a government-cloud tenant for you, or keeping CUI out of AI tools entirely and using commercial AI only on the unrestricted parts of the job.

## Is CMMC Phase 2 still suspended?

Yes, as far as any source we could check shows. The Department of War suspended Phase 2 on July 13, 2026. That phase would have made third-party (C3PAO) certification mandatory on many contracts starting November 10, 2026, and that date has now passed without the requirement taking effect. Washington Technology reported that a September 3 revision to Class Deviation 2026-O0025 directs contracting officers to strip third-party assessment requirements from contracts, which makes the pause harder to reverse than a memo.

The Reform Task Force's report went to the DoW CIO around September 11. [RSI Security's tracker](https://blog.rsisecurity.com/cmmc-updates/) and [CMMC Map](https://cmmcmap.com/blog/november-2026-cmmc-deadline/) both showed it unpublished with no restart date as of mid-October, and we found no later announcement.

What did not go away: Level 1 and Level 2 self-assessments, a current SPRS score, and DFARS 252.204-7012 itself. Enforcement hasn't paused either. RSI's tracker lists a September 1, 2026 settlement in which Honeywell Aerospace agreed to pay $2,042,518 over NIST SP 800-171 allegations, with no determination of liability. An uncontrolled chatbot workflow touching CUI is still a finding on a self-assessment you sign.

## What does GSA's final AI clause require?

GSA's clause, GSAR 552.239-7001, is no longer a draft. According to [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-GSA-Releases-Final-Language-of-AI-Contract-Clause), the final language took effect October 19, 2026 as a class deviation, with formal rulemaking to follow, and GSA planned to add it to Multiple Award Schedule contracts through MAS Refresh 34, scheduled for November 6.

The part that matters to builders is the scope. Per Wiley and [Winvale](https://info.winvale.com/blog/gsas-new-ai-clause-is-here-heres-what-mas-contractors-should-know):

- It applies when the government is buying a system where LLM functionality is a material feature and government data is submitted to or produced by the model.
- It self-deletes when LLM use is limited to the contractor's internal systems.
- Where it does apply, it flows down to subcontractors doing AI design, development, deployment, or operation and monitoring, requires logged and limited access to government data, and requires notice to the contracting officer within 72 hours of a material violation.
- It overrides conflicting vendor terms of service.

So a GC using an AI assistant for its own back office on a GSA job is likely outside the clause. A design-build team delivering a chatbot, an AI document portal, or an AI-enabled building system to the government is likely inside it. Contracting officers have discretion, so check the clause as it appears in your contract.

## Is self-hosting an open model a way around all this?

It's the cleanest architecture for CUI: if the model runs inside your own compliant boundary, no outside cloud provider is involved and the FedRAMP question doesn't arise. The catch is hardware.

Reflection AI released the weights of Beam, its first open-weight model, on October 16, 2026 under an Apache 2.0 license. It has 501 billion total parameters, and per [Digital Applied's write-up of the serving documentation](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/reflection-beam-open-model-apache-2-licence-hardware), the smallest official build is a 273 GiB download that assumes four NVIDIA B300 GPUs. TechCrunch noted Reflection's performance claims haven't been independently verified. Mistral's largest open model has the same server-class problem, which [we priced out for an estimating shop here](/posts/2026-10-31-mistral-large-4-open-weights-construction-estimator-private-ai-bid-files-hosted-vs-self-host).

One warning on model choice. In October, DHS barred civilian agencies from buying or using DeepSeek products under federal supply-chain law, as CyberScoop reported. That order is aimed at agency systems, but [a contractor on federal work should still know which models sit under its tools](/posts/2026-10-21-dhs-deepseek-federal-ban-construction-federal-work-vendor-ai-audit), including any it hosts itself.

## What should a federal GC or sub do this quarter?

1. **List the workflows that touch CUI.** RFIs referencing restricted design data, submittals for export-controlled systems, schedules tied to sensitive phasing. Everything off that list can use standard commercial AI today.
2. **Ask each software vendor in writing** which model powers every AI feature and what authorization boundary it runs in. A spec-search or document-review feature may be sending text to a commercial API without your team knowing.
3. **Keep your NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment honest.** The audit is paused. The signature on your affirmation is not.
4. **If you need AI on CUI now,** get quotes for a government-cloud deployment from the table above and compare that cost against simply keeping CUI out of AI tools.
5. **Read your GSA contract for 552.239-7001** if you hold or bid Schedule work and deliver anything with an LLM in it.
6. **Watch for the Task Force report.** It decides whether third-party certification returns. The [Stop Rogue AI Act](/posts/2026-09-06-stop-rogue-ai-act-federal-construction-contractor-compliance), which would add an AI-agent inventory requirement for federal contractors, is still only an introduced bill.
