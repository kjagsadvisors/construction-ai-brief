Humanoid robots on a jobsite today do one job: walk a planned route carrying a camera. Two contractors run them that way, Tilbury Douglas in the UK and Shimizu in Japan, and both are trials. No humanoid installs, lifts, or fastens anything on a live site without a person driving it, and the humanoids shipping in volume all still work indoors on factory and warehouse floors.

## Is any humanoid robot actually deployed on a construction jobsite in 2026?

Yes, but only as trials, and only for inspection. We found three documented cases of a humanoid on an active site:

1. **Tilbury Douglas (UK).** The tier-one contractor put an off-the-shelf Unitree G1, nicknamed Douglas, on a live site in April 2026 and called it an industry first ([Tilbury Douglas](https://www.tilburydouglas.co.uk/tilbury-douglas-becomes-first-contractor-to-launch-a-humanoid-robot-on-construction-site/)). Construction News put the hardware cost at [£15,000](https://www.constructionnews.co.uk/tech/tilbury-douglas-deploys-humanoid-robot-on-live-construction-site-21-04-2026/).
2. **Shimizu (Japan).** On July 8, 2026, Shimizu said it "has begun full-scale field trials" of humanoid robots for site inspections. In one trial, a humanoid holding a camera walked a pre-set route at 1.0 m/s, and Shimizu is testing multimodal AI models to analyze the footage ([Shimizu](https://www.shimz.co.jp/en/company/about/news-release/2026/2026027.html)). The release doesn't name the robot's maker.
3. **A university field test (US).** University of California, Irvine researchers put a Unitree G1 on an active construction site and had operators drive it by remote control through two tasks ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00718)). More on that below.

An earlier version of this page counted only Douglas. Shimizu's trial belongs on the list.

## What does a humanoid actually do on those sites?

It collects data. Douglas captures 360-degree imagery from the same positions on each walk so software can compare images over time, and its three target uses are defect identification, safety monitoring, and progress reporting. Tilbury Douglas estimates the robot saves about 40 hours of staff time a month.

The limits are specific, and they come from the contractor itself ([Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/tempering-the-robot-revolution/)):

- **Autonomy stops at a fence.** Douglas operates on its own only in a test area at a Birmingham site. Elsewhere a handler always accompanies it, and it has an emergency stop button.
- **Battery lasts about two hours.** The team keeps two batteries on hand to swap. Technical director Mark Buckle called battery life "probably the main limitation."
- **Stability is still a slight issue.**

Douglas is touring UK sites for 12 months of further trials ([Industrialised Construction](https://www.industrialisedconstruction.co.uk/2026/05/22/tilbury-douglas-trials-humanoid-robot-on-live-construction-site/)).

A quadruped has done this job for years. Boston Dynamics' Spot [already runs autonomous capture walks](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/unlocking-jobsite-data-with-spot/) on GC jobsites. A humanoid is a cheaper, less proven way to carry the same camera.

## Can a humanoid do physical work like carrying tools or painting?

Not on its own, and not at a useful speed when a person drives it. The UC Irvine study is the best public measurement we've seen. Operators controlled a G1 with a headset for the arms and pedals for walking, on an active site, across 10 trials each:

| Task | Success rate | Robot (teleoperated) | Person by hand | Slowdown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tool transport | 100% | 72 seconds | 4 seconds | About 18x |
| Surface painting | 80% | 149 seconds | 47 seconds | About 3x |

The authors "observed motor overheating during extended teleoperation sessions," and named continuous operation as a practical barrier.

A robot that needs a full-time operator and takes three times as long doesn't save labor. The authors pitch teleoperation as a way to cut physical strain and collect training data.

## What's stopping a humanoid from working a full jobsite unsupervised?

Three things, and the trials above show all of them.

**Navigation.** Douglas and Shimizu's robot both follow pre-set routes. A jobsite's walkable path changes every week. Newer navigation models don't close that gap yet: Mistral's Robostral Navigate steers a robot with one ordinary camera, but it was trained entirely in simulation, and Mistral names manufacturing, delivery, logistics, and hospitality as target uses, with no mention of construction ([Mistral AI](https://mistral.ai/news/robostral-navigate/)).

**Endurance.** Two hours per battery on Douglas, and overheating motors in the UC Irvine test.

**Safety sign-off.** NVIDIA's Halos for Robotics, announced in June 2026, includes the first accredited inspection lab for physical AI, and Agility's Digit is the first robot going through it ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-halos-for-robotics-the-industrys-first-full-stack-safety-system-for-physical-ai)). Digit works in warehouses. Nothing comparable covers a legged robot on an occupied, outdoor site, which we cover in [is there a safety certification for jobsite robots?](/seo/is-there-a-safety-certification-for-jobsite-robots). As Cambridge professor Ioannis Brilakis told IMechE: "If you are delegating health and safety tasks to a machine, you need to be able to trust the machine" ([IMechE](https://www.imeche.org/news/news-article/humanoid-robot-trialled-on-uk-building-site-but-regulatory-barriers-may-limit-wider-adoption)).

## Should a GC care that Douglas runs on Unitree hardware?

Yes. Unitree went public in Shanghai in August 2026, but the IPO cash doesn't fix the security record. UniPwn is a Bluetooth exploit that gives root access to G1 and H1 humanoids and Go2 and B2 quadrupeds, and an infected robot can spread it to other Unitree units in range. On July 28, 2026, the FCC barred new models of Chinese-made humanoid and quadruped robots from the equipment authorization needed to sell in the US. Models already authorized were not pulled ([Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321890/20260728/fcc-bans-chinese-humanoid-robots-power-inverters-linked-confirmed-backdoors.htm)). Reporting updated September 21 found that researchers still hadn't documented a public firmware patch for UniPwn ([Humanoid.guide](https://humanoid.guide/unitree-exhibits-g1-at-ifa-amid-unresolved-security-questions/)).

Before copying the Tilbury Douglas approach, ask your IT lead whether that robot goes anywhere near the site network. Our [earlier piece on Unitree and the Covered List](/posts/2026-08-07-unitree-ipo-fcc-covered-list-robot-dog-security-risk) covers the procurement side.

## Where will humanoids land next?

In prefab shops, and in more camera patrols.

Shimizu has the clearest published plan from a contractor. It completed an investment in Noble Machines, a US startup building the MOBY series of construction humanoids, on October 16, 2026. Simulations using Shimizu's BIM data are due to start by the end of 2026, with physical testing on real sites from spring 2027. The target tasks are transporting materials and equipment, navigating sites, and patrols ([Shimizu](https://www.shimz.co.jp/en/company/about/news-release/2026/2026044.html)).

For most contractors the nearer opening is off-site. "Off-site modular construction is so much more controlled," Brilakis told IMechE, and the vendor roadmaps agree: Digit 5 is aimed at pallet unloading and machine tending, with early access in the first half of 2027, as we covered in [what Digit 5 means for a prefab shop](/posts/2026-09-16-agility-robotics-digit-5-humanoid-cageless-prefab-shop-safety).

## Is a humanoid worth piloting now?

For most GCs, no. Here's how we'd sort it:

- **Worth it today:** a quadruped or a fixed-camera system for progress capture. Same data, proven hardware.
- **Worth a look if you run a prefab shop:** 2027 lease offers from warehouse-proven vendors. Read the vendor's financials first.
- **A waste for now:** any humanoid pitched for production work on an open site. Ask the vendor where the autonomy stops and the handler with the e-stop starts. For every humanoid we could find, it stops at a fenced test area or a pre-set route.
