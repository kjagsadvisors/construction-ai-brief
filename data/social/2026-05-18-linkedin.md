A year ago, 17% of US contractors said AI tools had moved a real number on their P&L.

This year: 38%.

That's not "we're piloting it" and not "we're evaluating." It's the share of contractors who can point to a measurable business impact — a number on the P&L — from AI tooling on their projects.

The Construction Owners Association of America's 2026 survey landed this month with three numbers worth holding in your head:

→ 38% of contractors report measurable business impact from AI (up from 17% in 2025)
→ 82% of large GCs are increasing AI investment budgets this year
→ 94% of mid-sized firms are either implementing or actively evaluating

Adoption is no longer a megaproject-and-CIO story. It's more than a third of the surveyed population, and the curve is steepening.

Here's the bid-table math, which is the only place this conversation actually matters:

When the GC across town can run a quantity takeoff in 90 minutes instead of 14 hours, route submittals against the spec automatically, and have RFI logs summarized for the owner before close of business — the labor cost embedded in their bid is structurally lower than yours. That margin shows up as either a sharper number on the line or a healthier per-job overhead contribution. Both ways, it's yours to lose.

Two leading indicators worth watching for the back half of 2026:

1. Insurance carriers asking about AI-assisted safety observation at renewal. Expect discounts or surcharges by Q4.

2. The AGC's next workforce survey. If the 500K-worker shortfall holds, the question for mid-market shops flips from "should we adopt" to "we have no choice."

The cost of doing nothing is no longer zero.
