Each one ends with something you can do before Friday.

**1. Claude now edits Google Docs, Sheets and Slides from a sidebar. Test it on a closed bid first.**

Anthropic's Claude for Google Workspace is in beta on Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise plans. Claude reads the file you have open and edits it in place, and Anthropic's help page says every change shows in version history. That matters for estimators because many subs and small GCs level bids in a shared Google Sheet that every vendor can open. The honest limit: an assistant with edit rights inside a file that carries your numbers can change a cell you did not mean to touch, and a fluent explanation is not a check. Take a bid you already closed, have Claude build the leveling comparison from the quotes, then compare it line by line against your own. Count the misses before it goes near a live bid, and keep the version history on. Our guide: [what an estimator should test first](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-07-claude-google-sheets-docs-add-on-construction-bid-leveling-scope-sheets).

**2. Amazon says it no longer signs NDAs with government agencies on data center projects. Read your own confidentiality clause.**

AWS CEO Matt Garman said Amazon no longer uses nondisclosure agreements with the government agencies it works with on data center projects, and paired it with a "Built Together" program of more than $1 billion over five years for host communities. The announcement comes amid more than 100 data center moratoriums under consideration across the country. Reports describe the change as applying to new agreements and limited to agencies. Nothing reported says it reaches the confidentiality, publicity or project-name clauses that owners and GCs push down to subs. But permit files and public hearings become more open, so more of a job's scope, power and water numbers may surface early. Before the next hyperscale bid, read the confidentiality clause in your subcontract and ask your GC what you may say about the project when a reporter or neighbor asks. More: [what GCs and subs should check](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-05-amazon-ends-data-center-ndas-gc-confidentiality-clauses-community-opposition).

Source: [Data Center Dynamics — AWS drops non-disclosure agreements for data center projects](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/aws-drops-non-disclosure-agreements-for-data-center-projects) and [WSOC — Amazon ends data center NDAs with government agencies](https://www.wsoctv.com/news/local/amazon-ends-data-center-ndas-with-government-agencies/JRQPI4ZXVBBHLHV46NUUNZAP2A/)

**3. OpenAI's Astra Ultrafast runs up to 8x faster at 6x the price. Pay for speed only when a person is waiting.**

OpenAI now sells its top GPT-6 Astra model in an Ultrafast tier. For short requests it is listed at $60 per million input tokens and $300 per million output tokens, six times the standard rate. The 8x is a vendor ceiling on output speed, not a guarantee, since tool calls and document loading still take time. The question for a contractor is who is idle while the model works. On bid day, an estimator waiting on a scope comparison an hour before the deadline may be worth the premium. A weekly cost report or a spec-book summary that can run overnight is not. If you run agents through an API, set the tier per job and cap spend, so a convenience setting does not quietly become a six-times line item. Our breakdown: [bid-day estimating agents and the price of speed](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-06-openai-astra-ultrafast-6x-price-construction-bid-day-estimating-agents).

**4. Google's Gemini 4 Argon tops one benchmark index but is limited to cyber defenders for now. Do not budget around it yet.**

Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Argon on September 30. Access is limited to trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind program, with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers next and no date given. Listed pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, rising to $4 and $20, and Google has not said how reasoning tokens are billed. Vals AI's index puts it first of 41 models at 68.9%, ahead of Claude Sonnet 5.5 at 67.04%, though Google's own other benchmark figures have not been reproduced by third parties. For a sub or GC, this is a watch item. The spread between top models is a point or two, so what you pay per finished task and how reliably it handles your documents matters more than rank. When it opens up, run it against the same three real documents you use to test every other model. Related: [Argon and submittal log drafting](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-02-gemini-4-argon-1m-token-output-construction-submittal-log-drafting).

Source: [eesel AI — Gemini 4 Argon pricing](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-4-argon-pricing) and [Vals AI — Gemini 4 Argon](https://www.vals.ai/models/google_gemini-4-argon)
