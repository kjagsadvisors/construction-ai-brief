Five items this week, and they share one thread: the money, the tools and the risk are all moving faster than most contractors' paperwork. Data center spending is reshaping backlogs, a cheaper AI model makes office automation worth pricing, and phishing is getting better at imitating your subs. Each item ends with something you can do before Friday.

**1. Data center construction is running at an $85 billion annual rate, up 73% in a year, while factory work falls about 20%. Measure your backlog concentration.**

The Census Bureau's August release shows data center spending up 7.5% in the month and 73% year over year, to a seasonally adjusted $85 billion annual rate. Factory construction is still larger at $168 billion but is down 19.8% from a year ago. ABC's analysis puts data center growth at a 149% annualized pace since March. Census figures are money put in place, not new awards, so they trail what is being bid today. For a Div 23 or Div 26 sub, the work is real but the exposure is specific: tag every job in your signed backlog by owner type, and set an internal ceiling on any one owner or sector. Then read the suspension, standby and long-lead terms on those jobs, because a power or permit delay lands on the trades first. Our breakdown: [data centers vs. factories and sub backlog risk](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-05-data-center-construction-spending-85b-vs-factories-168b-sub-backlog-concentration).

**2. OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol does computer-use tasks within 2.1 points of its flagship at about one-seventh the cost per task. Price one portal chore.**

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol on September 29 at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, one-fifth of the flagship Astra's price. On the OSWorld 2.0 benchmark, which tests an agent clicking through real software, it came within 2.1 points of Astra at $5.47 per task versus $23.80, per reported figures. Those are vendor-reported numbers from a clean test environment, not a GC's compliance portal that times out and asks for a two-factor code. The fit for a sub is repetitive, checkable office work: loading certified payroll or insurance certificates into GC portals, re-keying pay app lines, building bid-day comparison sheets from quotes. Run one chore for two weeks next to the person who does it now, and log minutes saved, errors caught and the token bill. If errors are not near zero, someone is still re-checking everything and the savings are not real. Give any agent its own login and read the portal's terms on automated access first. More: [which portal chores a sub can price out](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-04-gpt-6-1-sol-computer-use-cost-per-task-construction-portal-chores).

**3. Microsoft says phishing started 23% of the intrusions it investigated, up from 7%. Put a call-back rule on every bank-detail change.**

Microsoft's 2026 Digital Defense Report, covering July 2025 to June 2026, says phishing was the entry point in 23% of its incident-response cases, against 7% the year before. Adversary-in-the-middle kits, which steal a logged-in session and get past text-message codes, were 44.6% of identified phishing techniques. The sample is companies that asked Microsoft for help, so read it as direction, not an industry rate, and the report says nothing construction-specific. The link is practical: pay applications, lien waivers, retainage releases and updated ACH instructions all move by email, and AI makes a believable fake in a real thread easier to produce. This week, require a call-back to a number already on file for any change to a vendor's or sub's bank details, and move the few people who can approve payments to passkeys or hardware keys. Details: [what a contractor's payment controls need](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-03-microsoft-defense-report-ai-phishing-construction-payment-fraud).

**4. California bars AI-only firings and discipline starting July 1, 2027. If you have crews there, document the human check behind any telematics write-up.**

Governor Newsom signed SB 947, the "No Robo Bosses Act," on September 30. An employer cannot rely solely on an automated system to fire or discipline a worker. When AI output is the primary basis, a person has to corroborate it with other inputs such as managerial evaluations, peer reviews and personnel files, and the worker gets written notice that AI was used, what data it drew on, and a human contact. The law does not mention construction, but its definition is broad, which could reach fleet telematics scores, geofenced timekeeping flags, camera-based PPE detections and productivity rankings. Whether a given tool qualifies is a question for counsel. For any contractor, in any state, a supervisor looking at the underlying video or log before a write-up is cheap and makes the discipline easier to defend. Our guide: [crew discipline, telematics and SB 947](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-04-california-no-robo-bosses-act-sb-947-construction-crew-discipline-telematics).

**5. FieldAI signed a term sheet to raise $700 million at a $10 billion valuation. Settle who owns the scan data before a robot shows up.**

FieldAI builds the software brain for robots, not the robots, and sells it to construction, data center and defense customers. The round is reported as a signed term sheet, not a closed deal. The company says revenue plus signed contracts is above $135 million across more than 30 customers. Boston Dynamics and FieldAI describe Spot robots doing overnight progress scans and hazard checks on construction sites, and cite a drop of more than 90% in inspection and documentation time, which is a vendor claim from their own deployments. Treat it as unproven for budgeting. If more robots arrive, whether from a GC, a sub or an owner, the scans become a project record. Before the first capture, put in writing who holds the data, whether the vendor can train models on it, and who reads the morning flag report and closes each item. Our look: [what the robot-brain bet means for site capture](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-03-fieldai-700m-10b-jobsite-robot-autonomy-construction-scan-data).
