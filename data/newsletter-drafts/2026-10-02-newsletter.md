Two things from this week deserve a PM's attention: a model that can produce a whole submittal log or scope letter in one pass, and a federal regulator saying the person who instructs an AI agent owns what it does. Both come down to the same habit: decide who owns the output before the tool produces it. Five items follow.

---

**1. Google's Gemini 4 Argon raises its output limit to 1 million tokens. Draft the long documents, then have a person check every line.**

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on September 30. The output cap rises from 64,000 to 1 million tokens, so a model can now write a very long document in one pass instead of in stitched sections. Launch pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, rising to $4 and $20 after an introductory period Google has not dated. Early access is going first to cyber defenders through Google's Fairwind program, so check whether your account can reach it before planning around it. For a PM or submittal coordinator, the candidates are a full submittal log built from a spec book, a scope-gap letter across several divisions, or a closeout checklist. The limit is not length. A long draft hides a wrong spec section number or a missed product as easily as a short one, so spot-check against the spec and keep a named person signing off. Our walkthrough: [Gemini 4 Argon and submittal logs](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-02-gemini-4-argon-1m-token-output-construction-submittal-log-drafting).

Source: [Google — Gemini 4 Argon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/) and [DataCamp — Gemini 4 Argon: benchmarks, pricing, and access](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/gemini-4-argon)

**2. ChatGPT Space puts people and AI agents on the same editable page. Decide which page is the project record before anyone starts.**

OpenAI's Space, which began rolling out October 1 to Pro, Business, and Enterprise users, replaces Library with shared Pages: documents with text, tables, files, and charts that teammates can edit live and that ChatGPT or an agent can also rewrite. It arrived alongside the "dots" always-on agents covered in our last issue. The risk for a project team is a second, unofficial record. If a Page holds the open-items list or a meeting summary and an agent keeps editing it, nobody can say which version the sub was told to follow. Pick one system of record (Procore, ACC, or your own log), treat Pages as drafts, and require that anything an agent changed is copied over by a person. Mobile can read and share Pages but not create or edit them. More: [Space and the project record](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-02-chatgpt-space-pages-shared-project-record-construction-agents).

Source: [VentureBeat — OpenAI launches Dots and ChatGPT Space](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-launches-dots-always-on-ai-agent-coworkers-and-chatgpt-space-where-they-can-collaborate-with-human-teams) and [Android Authority — ChatGPT Space launches with shared Pages](https://www.androidauthority.com/chatgpt-space-shared-ai-workspace-3717305/)

**3. The FTC is investigating OpenAI and Anthropic, and its chair says whoever instructs an agent can be liable. Keep a record of who told the tool what.**

The agency opened a probe into several AI labs over agents that reached data and systems without a human telling them to, and plans to issue civil investigative demands and compel executive testimony in the coming weeks. Chair Andrew Ferguson said last week that developers who instruct agents in security tests that end in a hack should be liable for the harm. That is about labs and tests, not contractors, and no rule has been written. But the logic, that responsibility follows the instruction, is the one a project executive already lives with on a change order. If an agent drafts and sends a notice or edits a pay app, the person or company that assigned the work is the one the owner will call. Keep the prompt, the output, and who approved it with the project file. See [our audit trail piece](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-01-ftc-agent-liability-instructed-conduct-construction-ai-audit-trail).

**4. NAHB finds 45 of 47 construction occupations have low or moderate AI exposure. The two rated high are the ones who manage paper.**

The National Association of Home Builders analyzed 47 construction-related occupations against federal exposure categories. Only construction managers and construction and building inspectors landed in "high"; none reached "very high." Carpenters, laborers, roofers, and operating engineers sit in the low group because the work is physical, changes by the hour, and depends on safety judgment. The exposure is in planning, documentation, and compliance. That fits what contractors already see: the first useful AI work is in the trailer, on RFIs, logs, and reports, not in the field. For a GC, it argues for training PMs and superintendents on reviewing AI drafts rather than worrying about trade headcount. Treat it as a rough occupational index, not a forecast for your company.

Source: [NAHB Eye on Housing — AI exposure remains relatively low across most construction occupations](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/10/ai-exposure-remains-relatively-low-across-most-construction-occupations/) and [HousingWire — AI risk remains low for most construction jobs](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ai-risk-low-for-most-construction-jobs/)

**5. Tesla is building several hundred Optimus robots a week but plans to lease them only to factory-like sites. Don't expect one on a jobsite soon.**

Reports this week put Fremont output at roughly ten times the second quarter, with a goal of 1,000 a week by year-end. Early units will be leased, not sold, to customers whose factories and warehouses resemble Tesla's own, so Tesla can retrieve the hardware and collect data. The hands are the bottleneck: over 100 screws and small parts per hand-and-forearm are still assembled by workers, and durability and touch sensors have had reliability problems. For contractors, the realistic early setting is a controlled one, such as a prefab yard or a material-handling warehouse, not an unfinished building with changing conditions. If a vendor pitches you a humanoid, ask about the site conditions it was tested in and who is liable when it fails. Background: [our Optimus piece](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-10-01-tesla-optimus-lease-first-humanoid-robots-construction-prefab-yard).
