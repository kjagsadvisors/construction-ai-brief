**1. Six big banks now say losses from an AI agent using your login are yours.**

Citi revised its Online User Agreement this week. Customers who give third-party AI tools account access are "responsible for activities conducted through those agents," including what a hacker does if the tool leaks the credentials, per TechCrunch. Citizens emailed customers saying the same, PNC changed its terms on November 18, JPMorgan and Bank of America announced similar policies in October, and Wells Fargo's updated agreement takes effect November 24. The same week, Google gave Gemini computer use on the Mac for paid U.S. subscribers, so pointing an agent at a bank portal just got easier. The coverage describes consumer online banking, not business or treasury accounts, so pull your commercial agreement and search it for "agent" and "credentials." Until you have an answer in writing, let AI match invoices to POs and stage the payment batch. A person with their own login releases it.

Source: [TechCrunch — more banks say customers using AI agents are on their own](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked/). **2. Banks are selling AI data center construction loans below face value.**

HSBC sold its roughly $300 million slice of the $18 billion Project Jupiter construction loan at about 85 cents on the dollar, and ING and Société Générale have offered up to $800 million more, per Bloomberg, Reuters and FT reporting. Nothing reported says any project has stopped paying. But a buyer paying 85 cents is pricing in delay or a late finish, the same events that slow pay applications and stretch retainage. If you have data center work in backlog, spend an hour per job confirming the payment bond, your notice and lien dates, your retainage total and your pay clause while the job still pays on time.

Source: [FinanceFeeds — HSBC sold its Jupiter loan slice in the mid-80s](https://financefeeds.com/oracle-stock-hsbc-sold-its-300-million-slice-of-an-18-billion-data-center-loan-in-the-mid-80s/). **3. Claude Data turns on for Claude Enterprise accounts today unless an owner already chose a setting.**

Anthropic's support article says Claude Data, the renamed Claude Dashboards, turns on by default for Enterprise organizations on November 20. It builds dashboards from plain-English questions against connected warehouses and apps, and shows the SQL behind each chart. The setting to understand is live sharing: it runs on the builder's access, so viewers see the results even without access to the source. Live sharing is off by default on Enterprise and on for Team plans. Have the account owner save a deliberate choice under Organization settings > Artifacts.

Source: [Anthropic — get started with Claude Data](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/17454700-get-started-with-claude-data). **4. Google's Gemini 4 Neon is built for spreadsheets and PDFs. Test it on closed-bid quotes first.**

Google released Neon on November 18 as the middle model of the Gemini 4 family, at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output. Its demo turned a single image into a 100,000-cell Google Sheet running more than 500,000 live formulas. The demo was a showpiece, not a messy Division 23 quote with alternates. The useful test for an estimator: run five quotes from a bid that already closed, mixing clean PDFs, scans, phone photos and hand markups. Count the lines, confirm totals are formulas, and check exclusions stayed out of the base.

Source: [Google — introducing Gemini 4 Neon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/). **5. Pulley raised $64 million to speed up commercial permits with AI agents. Do not shorten the permit line yet.**

Pulley, which pairs AI agents with licensed permitting staff, raised a $41 million Series B led by Jump Capital and a $23 million Series A led by CRV and Susa Ventures. Customers include Amazon, Starbucks, Meta and Equinix, so GCs on retail, TI and data center work will see it. Pulley says it gets approvals up to 70% faster and that over 40% of projects clear with no review comments. Both figures are self-reported, and most projects still get comments. In precon, ask who owns the permit and what the service has done in that jurisdiction. Keep your normal permit duration in the baseline until it has a track record there.

Source: [CityBiz — Pulley raises $64 million](https://www.citybiz.co/article/936101/pulley-raises-64-million-to-expand-ai-powered-real-estate-development-platform/). **6. A lawsuit says an AI alert was cancelled and armed police came anyway.**

Four students sued Omnilert, the maker of an AI gun-detection system, along with their school district and county police, after the system flagged a bag of chips as a possible firearm. The complaint says staff and police reviewed the image, saw the mistake and cancelled the alert, and officers still arrived and handcuffed the students. Nothing has been decided. AI-monitored camera towers on jobsites run a similar chain: software flags motion, a monitoring agent reviews it, the agent calls police. Often it flags your own early crew. Send the monitoring company a current after-hours list for every site, and get the cancel procedure in writing.

Source: [Lawyers' Committee for Civil Rights Under Law](https://www.lawyerscommittee.org/lawyers-committee-files-lawsuit-on-behalf-of-black-students-detained-and-searched-due-to-ai-alert-falsely-identifying-a-bag-of-chips-as-a-firearm/). **Robotics spotlight: who pays for the robot?**

At the Groundworks Summit, a construction robotics conference held November 11, one panel took on the question most site robot pilots stall on. One sub may carry the upfront cost while the savings spread across the whole job. Monumental's founder said its bricklaying robot places 800 bricks a day, about twice a human bricklayer. If a robot vendor pitches your crew, price it against your next several jobs, not one, and settle with the GC up front who carries the cost.

Source: [Robotics & Automation News — conference report](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/11/17/conference-report-construction-industry-on-the-verge-of-a-robotics-revolution/107197/).
