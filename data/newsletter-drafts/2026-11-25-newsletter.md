Six items before the holiday. The lead is a new test of AI agents on messy office work, and it has a number your office manager should see before an agent touches payables. The rest cover homemade Claude tools turning into desktop apps, free AI consultants for small businesses, new privacy options, hiring, and state rules on AI in employment.

**1. The best AI agents finished about six in ten everyday office tasks, and often couldn't repeat a success.**

ShellBench, released November 24 by the OpenClaw Foundation with Hugging Face, Red Hat, UC Berkeley and others, gave 12 models 100 tasks in cluttered workspaces full of emails, spreadsheets, receipts and PDFs. The top scores were GPT-6 Astra at 62.5%, Claude Fable 5.2 at 61.9% and GPT-6.1 Sol at 61.4%. GPT-6 Astra passed 75.1% of tasks at least once in three tries but only 48.8% on all three. TechCrunch reported that in one task, agents recommended duplicate or incorrect invoice payments 40.3% of the time. If you're thinking about an agent for matching supplier invoices to POs and delivery tickets, run the same batch three times and require it to cite the invoice, PO and ticket behind every match. A person still approves every payment.

Source: [OpenClaw — ShellBench](https://openclaw.ai/blog/shellbench). Also: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/even-the-best-ai-agents-struggle-with-everyday-life/). Our take: [run it three times before it touches invoices](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-25-shellbench-ai-agents-fail-four-in-ten-everyday-office-tasks-construction-trade-sub-office-manager-invoice-matching-run-three-times).

**2. Claude can now install the tools it builds as Mac and Windows apps.**

Anthropic's "Install as app" beta turns a Claude artifact, like a change order log or a conduit fill calculator, into a desktop app with its own permissions. The highest level, full access, lets it run programs and read, change or delete files without asking each time. Anthropic's help page says it "doesn't review apps before you install them." On Team plans the feature is on by default, with full access off until an admin turns it on. Most subs don't have an IT department, so the owner or office manager should check those admin settings now and decide who can grant full access before the office starts installing whatever the estimator built last week.

Source: [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/18516354-install-an-artifact-as-an-app). Our take: [what to decide before rollout](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-25-claude-artifacts-install-as-desktop-apps-full-access-construction-trade-sub-office-homemade-tools-admin-settings-before-rollout).

**3. OpenAI will pay local consultants up to $8,000 a month to help small businesses for free.**

OpenAI's OpenWorks pilot pays partners in 20 communities to work in person with independently owned U.S. small businesses, which also get three months of ChatGPT Business at no cost. Private equity-owned and venture-backed companies are excluded, so a family-owned mech or electrical sub likely qualifies and a PE platform company does not. Decrypt reports partners commit four days a month, split across several businesses, so bring one repetitive, measurable office problem, such as turning signed T&M tickets into change order requests. Remember who pays the consultant, and ask what the tool costs once the free period ends.

Source: [OpenAI — OpenWorks](https://openai.com/openworks/). Also: [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383848/openai-will-pay-8k-preach-ai-gospel-small-businesses). Our take: [bring one office problem](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-25-openai-openworks-free-local-ai-consultants-small-business-construction-trade-sub-office-one-problem-who-pays-consultant).

**4. Anthropic now lets companies keep Claude prompts in their own cloud instead of its servers.**

Zero data retention with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards went into public beta on November 23. Prompts and answers are written to the customer's own Amazon S3 or Google Cloud Storage and deleted on Anthropic's side. Automated monitoring scans that record and opens a "Safety Case" when it spots misuse, and only the customer's team can read the flagged transcripts. If your precon team sends spec sections or RFI drafts to Claude through the API on hospital, federal or data center work with owner confidentiality clauses, this answers the owner's IT reviewer. It also means someone at your company has to read those alerts, so name that person before you sign up.

Source: [Claude — zero data retention with EFS](https://claude.com/resources/articles/zero-data-retention-with-enterprise-frontier-safeguards). Also: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-enterprise-frontier-safeguards-zero-data-retention-beta). **5. Anthropic's study of 58 companies found few AI layoffs, but about two-thirds are hiring less.**

The report, based on 66 interviews at 58 US companies that were already Anthropic customers, found only two companies where AI fully eliminated a job and someone lost it. The change shows up in hiring: about two-thirds said they hire less, mostly by not adding people as they grow. Construction firms appear in it, including one that replaced morning PM calls to crews with an agentic check-in workflow. For GCs, the risk is quietly skipping the project engineer hires where future PMs learn submittals, RFIs and pay apps. If AI takes that work, decide who learns the job and how.

Source: [Anthropic — Working with AI](https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai). Our take: [the project engineer pipeline](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-24-anthropic-58-company-study-two-thirds-hire-less-few-layoffs-construction-gc-ops-project-engineer-pipeline-review-owner).

**6. Governors want states to keep control of how AI affects workers.**

On November 22, a bipartisan National Governors Association council asked Congress for a federal AI framework that leaves states room to govern how AI is used. Bloomberg reported that includes oversight of AI's effects on workers and employment. That puts governors at odds with the White House push to preempt state AI laws, and the fight is expected to pick up in January. For subs hiring in several states, applicant ranking on job boards, resume scoring and camera tools that feed write-ups can all count as AI in employment decisions. Illinois already requires notice. List which of your HR tools use AI, and plan for state-by-state rules.

Source: [National Governors Association](https://www.nga.org/news/press-releases/governors-call-for-ai-action/). Also: [Ogletree on Illinois](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/illinois-steps-up-ai-regulation-in-employment-key-takeaways-for-employers/). **Automation spotlight: P&G runs factory shifts with nobody on the floor.**

Procter & Gamble now runs unattended shifts at 15 sites, often overnight for several hours at a time, as part of a push for up to $10 billion in productivity savings, per PYMNTS. Its supply chief said success "heavily depends on a workforce with the skills to scale and optimize these technologies," per MLQ's investor-day summary. For a sub's prefab shop, the lesson is not to buy a robot. Pick one machine that already runs long jobs from a file, like a pipe cutter or CNC plasma table, and test one well-prepared overnight run with a camera someone can check from home.

Source: [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/pg-steps-up-use-of-fully-autonomous-factory-shifts/). Our take: [one machine, one overnight test](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-24-procter-gamble-unattended-factory-shifts-15-sites-construction-trade-sub-prefab-shop-overnight-one-machine-test).
