Four items this week. Two are about how contractors are staffing and testing AI, one is about how a large energy company is putting AI agents into operations, and one is about whether the data center sites your crews are bidding will get built at all. Each ends with something you can check or ask this week.

---

**1. A workforce survey says every contractor it polled feels the labor shortage, and more are turning to AI to plan around it. Read the source before you quote it.**

Bridgit's second annual State of Workforce Planning report, released November 2, surveyed 250 construction leaders. All 250 said the labor shortage affects their business, up from 93% last year. 68% said it has raised their costs, up from 43%. The share of leaders who prioritize experience with the specific build type when hiring fell from 59% to 27%, which Bridgit ties possibly to the data center boom putting contractors into unfamiliar work. Leaders also reported more interest in AI for forecasting labor needs, and named trust in AI recommendations and poor fit with construction workflows as their top concerns. Bridgit sells workforce planning software and the sample is small, so treat these as one vendor's read, not an industry census. The practical takeaway for a PM or ops director: if you are staffing a building type your crews have not done, write down which skills you are substituting for experience, and test any AI forecast against your own last two projects before trusting it.

Source: [Construction Dive press release — New data shows large contractors turning to AI in face of ongoing labor shortage](https://www.constructiondive.com/press-release/20261102-new-data-shows-large-contractors-turning-to-ai-in-face-of-ongoing-labor-sho-1/). Vendor-reported figures.

**2. Suffolk opened a campus to test jobsite tech before it reaches projects. The useful part is the process, not the building.**

Suffolk Construction announced 100MAG on October 29, and Construction Dive reported on it November 4. It puts project planning, design, self-perform crews, and the company's technology investment and partnership teams under one roof, with a stated focus on AI, robotics, data and quantum. Ideas from its Jobsite of the Future program, its venture arm and its BOOST accelerator get tested there, and what works is scaled across projects and standardized by its operational excellence group. Suffolk gave no investment amount or headcount. A 50-person sub does not need a campus, but the sequence is copyable: pick one tool, run it on one job with a named owner, write down what it saved and what broke, and only then standardize it. If you are a sub working for a GC with a program like this, ask whether your scope is a pilot site and who pays for the extra coordination.

Source: [Construction Dive — Suffolk deepens tech push with new innovation center](https://constructiondive.com/news/suffolk-new-innovation-center-tech-ai-push/873767).

**3. ADNOC will run OpenAI's agent platform across oil and gas operations, with human experts keeping consequential decisions. Ask your own vendors where that line sits.**

At the ADIPEC conference on November 2, ADNOC, OpenAI and G42 announced a multi-year deal making ADNOC the first energy company to adopt OpenAI's Frontier platform for coordinating AI agents. The agents will pair GPT models with ADNOC's industrial data on G42's UAE infrastructure, starting with forecasting crude supply, demand, prices and refining margins, and extending its existing subsurface platform. Human experts retain oversight of consequential decisions, and financial terms were not disclosed. This is not a construction deal, but it is a template for what owners will expect from their contractors' AI use: agents working on real operating data with a named human signing off. Before you let an agent touch pay apps, schedules or change orders, decide in writing which outputs need a person's approval, and ask any vendor to show the log of what its agent did.

Source: [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/11/02/adnoc-signs-agreement-with-openai-and-g42-to-deploy-frontier-ai) and [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/adnoc-openai-g42-frontier-ai-deal). Related from us: [what happened when agents raised their own spending caps](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-02-ai-agents-raised-own-spending-caps-cornell-yale-construction-ap-pay-app-approval-email-access).

**4. Voters had 34 data center measures on local ballots this month. Check entitlement status before you commit crews.**

Ballotpedia counted 34 data center-related measures in 35 jurisdictions across eight states, with Ohio the most active at 20 initiatives in 17 jurisdictions, according to S&P Global's October 1 report. Other tallies put the national count near 39, so totals vary. Proposals range from requiring financial guarantees from developers who receive tax incentives to outright bans, and local governments in 40 states have an active ban or moratorium. We did not verify the results of the November 3 votes, so check your local outcomes directly. For a GC or sub pricing a campus, a ban or moratorium after award can freeze a job before mobilization. Ask the owner for the zoning and entitlement status in writing, and ask what your subcontract says about payment for preconstruction work and held materials if a vote or moratorium stops the project.

Source: [S&P Global Market Intelligence — Data center backlash shapes midterm ballots](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/10/data-center-backlash-shapes-midterm-ballots-campaigns-across-us-106324121) and [Stateline — Data center backlash set to sway midterm elections](https://stateline.org/?p=18816).
