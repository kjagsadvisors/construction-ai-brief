Five items and a robotics note. The lead is a security problem that reaches a contractor through small tools built in-house, not through a big software vendor. The rest cover a price change with a date on it, deposits on long-lead equipment, a prompting habit, and a new entrant in project management software.

**1. A scan found 24,664 Lovable-built apps sending database records to anyone. Check the apps your own people built.**

Security firm Crissy Field, working with the newspaper Handelsblatt, analyzed 159,041 projects built with the AI app builder Lovable. Of those, 24,664 sent database records to a visitor's browser with no login, and 7,403 of them had records containing email addresses. The researchers say their sample is not representative and the email figure is not a confirmed leak rate. Nothing was hacked. The apps were published with access rules missing or too loose. This matters to a GC because a project engineer can now build a sub prequal form, a punch list tracker or a timesheet app in an afternoon, and those hold financial statements, W-9s and phone numbers. Open each homemade app in a private browser window while logged out. If names or documents appear before sign-in, take it down until someone who understands the database fixes the access rules.

Source: [Crissy Field — Lovable research](https://www.crissyfield.de/research/lovable-en/). Our take: [four checks for an app you already have](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-16-lovable-apps-24664-open-databases-construction-gc-ops-homemade-prequal-punch-list-apps-login-check).

**2. Gemini 4 Argon doubles in price on December 1. Rerun your spec-review test on the cheaper Hydrogen first.**

Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12 at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, with a 1 million-token input window. SiliconANGLE reports that Argon's introductory rate of $2 and $10 ends November 30 and becomes $4 and $20. Google says Hydrogen scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index against Argon's 50. That is Google's own figure, and one point on a general index tells you nothing about a scanned Division 26 section. If your estimating team piloted spec or bid-document review on Argon, pull the same closed bid with its known scope gaps and run it on both models before the end of the month. Count misses and false flags. If you buy the feature through a software vendor, ask which model is behind it and whether your price changes in December.

Source: [Google — Gemini 4 Hydrogen](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/) and [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/12/google-releases-gemini-4-hydrogen-a-lower-cost-model-said-to-outscore-argon/). Our take: [the two-week test](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-14-google-gemini-4-hydrogen-1-50-argon-promo-ends-nov-30-construction-estimator-spec-review-rerun-closed-bid-test).

**3. SoftBank wrote off about $3.7B on power equipment it reserved and then cancelled. Reread your own cancellation terms.**

SoftBank's November 10 results included a charge of about $3.7 billion tied to power generation equipment for US AI data centers. The Next Web reports the company had made about $9.5 billion in advance payments by the end of September "in anticipation of shortages," then its plans changed, and its chief accounting officer said the fees on cancelled orders could not be recovered. An electrical or mechanical sub runs the same mechanism at a smaller scale every time it releases switchgear, generators or chillers early: the deposit buys a production slot, and the manufacturer keeps it when the job changes. Before the next early release, get the manufacturer's cancellation schedule in writing and attach it to your quote. If the GC or owner asks you to order before design is final, get that direction in writing with cancellation charges named as reimbursable.

Source: [The Next Web — SoftBank's power equipment writedown](https://thenextweb.com/news/softbank-loss-7bn-ai-data-centre-power-equipment-writedown). Our take: [long-lead cancellation terms](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-14-softbank-3-7b-writeoff-cancelled-power-equipment-deposits-construction-electrical-mech-sub-long-lead-cancellation-terms).

**4. AI models talked users out of a wrong approach 47% to 60% of the time. People did it 79% of the time.**

The Allen Institute for AI released XYBench on November 13. It holds 8,115 questions in which the asker has picked the wrong way to solve their real problem. On the Stack Overflow questions, the top human answer steered the asker toward the real goal 79% of the time, and the five models tested did so 47% to 60% of the time. The questions are programming and household how-to, not construction, but the pattern fits project paperwork. Ask an assistant to draft an RFI requesting approval of an alternate product and it will write a clean RFI, when the route on most jobs is the substitution procedure in Division 01. Our fix is one extra sentence: state the outcome you need before the task, then ask whether there is a better way to get there.

Source: [Ai2 — XYBench](https://allenai.org/blog/xybench). Our take: [give the goal, not just the task](https://constructionaibrief.com/posts/2026-11-15-ai2-xybench-ai-answers-wrong-question-construction-gc-ops-pm-rfi-substitution-state-the-goal-prompt).

**5. A former GC's founders raised $5M for agents that draft RFIs and chase submittals. The results quoted are the company's.**

Shackleton AI came out of stealth this month with a $5 million seed round led by Battery Ventures. The founders previously built a commercial general contractor to $35 million in annual revenue, and the product puts bidding, documents, scheduling and finance in one platform. Per CFOtech, its agents draft RFIs, chase overdue submittals, monitor budgets and turn field notes into daily reports, with staff approving each action. The company says some customers finished buildouts up to 35% ahead of schedule. That figure is self-reported, and the coverage gives no pricing and no list of integrations. For a midsize GC, the question to put to this or any similar vendor is whether it replaces your accounting and project management systems or sits on top of them, and what it costs to get your data back out.

Source: [CFOtech — Shackleton AI lands $5 million](https://cfotech.news/story/shackleton-ai-lands-usd-5-million-for-construction-platform).

**Robotics spotlight: OpenAI folded its robotics team into the rest of the company**

Fortune reported on November 13 that OpenAI disbanded its stand-alone robotics team and moved the staff into its main research and product groups. The company's reasoning is that general models such as GPT-6 Astra can now control a robot directly. Fortune cites an Nvidia study in which an Astra-run agent reached a 74.8% success rate on a simulated manipulation benchmark, against 51.0% for the best specialized robot model on that leaderboard. That result is from simulation, and a researcher quoted by Fortune notes that large models are slow and need a constant network connection. If this approach spreads, a shop robot becomes hardware plus a rented model. A mechanical or electrical sub pricing a robot for a prefab shop should ask which model runs it, what the robot does when the connection drops, and whether model usage is inside the lease or billed on top.

Source: [Fortune — OpenAI disbands its robotics team](https://fortune.com/2026/11/13/openai-disbands-robotics-team-as-its-general-models-start-running-robots/).
