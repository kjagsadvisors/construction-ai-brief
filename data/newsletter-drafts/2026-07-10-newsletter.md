Five things from the past few days, plus a robotics number worth knowing. Two of them are the same story from different vendors: an AI agent that doesn't just answer questions, it operates the software for you — your bid tab, your permit portal login.

---

**1. OpenAI's new agent finishes a spreadsheet or slide deck on its own — that's your submittal log and bid tab it's aimed at.**

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work on July 9 alongside its GPT-5.6 model family: hand it one instruction and it plans the steps, works across your files without asking permission at each one, and hands back a finished spreadsheet, deck, or document. On OpenAI's own SpreadsheetBench, it scores 45.5% versus 20.0% for Copilot in Excel — more than double, and still a number you shouldn't hang a live cost log on. The failure mode that matters isn't the agent refusing the task, it's a plausible-looking bid tab with one wrong cell nobody catches until the bid goes out.

**2. Meta's new model can operate a computer the way a person does — permit portals are the construction workflow it was built for.**

Meta shipped Muse Spark 1.1 the same day, a model trained to click, type, and navigate unfamiliar desktop, browser, and mobile screens on its own, scoring 80.8% on the OSWorld-Verified computer-use benchmark. A permit expediter working a multi-jurisdiction job re-keys the same project data into a dozen city and county portals that were never built to talk to each other or to any PM software — exactly the kind of no-API, click-through-the-screen problem this model targets. One in five multi-step tasks still needs a human to catch or finish, which is too high to leave unattended on anything with a filing deadline, but this is the model generation where "AI operates the portal for you" stopped being hypothetical.

**3. A Bloomberg investigation puts a hard number on the data-center labor crunch: a shortfall of up to 499,000 skilled workers.**

Reporting published July 8 found a lack of electricians, pipe fitters, and site supervisors is forcing some data-center contractors to turn away work or poach crews from smaller firms just to stay staffed, after several quarters of record order books. Wage data backs it up: overall US pay rose 2.4% year-over-year, but electrician wages rose 4.6%, journeyperson electrician wages 6.6%, and commercial electrician wages 9.9% — a premium the market is already pricing in. It takes roughly four years to turn a recruit into a certified electrician, so this gap doesn't close on a hiring cycle.

If your firm has any electrical or mechanical crews adjacent to the mission-critical market, the wage premium data centers are paying is now the number to benchmark your own retention offers against, not just your bid pricing on data-center-adjacent work.

Source: [Bloomberg — Labor Crunch Tests Growth Limits for US Data Center Builders](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-08/labor-crunch-tests-growth-limits-for-us-data-center-builders)

**4. A construction-financials AI platform just raised $15M — the pitch is automating the back-office work your accounting team dreads.**

Agave, which builds AI tools for AP invoice automation, expense management, and vendor compliance, closed a $15M Series A from Accel on July 7. The company says it's profitable, has tripled revenue year-over-year, and already works with more than 500 general and specialty contractors ranging from $5M to $5B-plus in annual revenue — real traction, not a pilot deck. Vendor compliance and AP automation touch the same certified-payroll and lien-waiver paperwork that eats a controller's week; worth a look if your back office is still manually chasing that stack.

Source: [Businesswire — Agave Raises $15M Series A to Bring AI to Construction Financials](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260707861533/en/Agave-Raises-$15M-Series-A-to-Bring-AI-to-Construction-Financials)

**5. Buildots is giving away the industry's actual numbers on how badly MEP schedules slip — and the gap is bigger than most PMs assume.**

Buildots' new Intelligence Lab, built on aggregated and anonymized project data, found a 20-50% gap between planned weekly MEP output and what crews actually deliver on data-center jobs — and, separately, that the final 20% of any tracked activity takes 27% of its total duration. Neither number is a surprise to anyone who's run a punch list, but having it quantified across a real dataset instead of one job's gut feel is useful the next time you're setting a schedule buffer or defending one to an owner.

Source: [ENR — Buildots launches Intelligence Lab, a project-data research hub](https://www.enr.com/articles/63235-buildots-launches-intelligence-lab-an-ai-powered-project-data-research-hub)

**Robotics spotlight: rebar tying is the clearest ROI case in construction robotics right now, and the math is public.**

Industry tracking now counts four robotic workflows in repeatable, non-pilot production on job sites — layout, groundworks, rebar tying, and digital capture — each with documented return on investment. The clearest case is rebar: TyBot ties 300 to 450 intersections an hour against 40 to 80 for a skilled worker, finishing a 200,000-square-foot industrial mat in 4 to 6 days versus 8 to 12 for a human crew. If you're still evaluating whether robotics on the jobsite pencils out, rebar tying on a big flatwork pour is the workflow with the numbers already proven.

Source: [ZACUA Ventures — Construction Robotics Report 2026](https://zacuaventures.com/construction-robotics-report-2026/)
